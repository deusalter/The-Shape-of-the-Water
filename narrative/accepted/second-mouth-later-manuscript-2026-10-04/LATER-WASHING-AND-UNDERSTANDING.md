# When the washing comes down

Lead adaptation of the proposed circumstance and argument in WORLD-ETHICS-V-12, 2026-10-04. Selected for continued development, outside frozen version 4. This version performs the two individual support trials on entry, preserves a real choice about the combined trial, and keeps Blaise's personal disclosure separate from his philosophical question. No final result about God is awarded by either physical outcome.

Present throughout: Blaise, Noor and the continuing orchard Dora. The speaking piece is coupled to the root. The other Dora is at the dry-country performance space and does not hear this encounter. The old support below is a later active connection established during the journey, not either P or Q from the supper crime.

## The first two movements

Noor has turned her coat inside out. Its lining lies over the back of a chair. She is trying to sew a pocket without tipping the stones out of the other one.

You move a bowl off the seat.

“Leave that there.”

“Were you keeping the chair?”

“I was keeping the pocket flat.”

You put the bowl back. Two stones knock together beneath it. Noor pulls the thread through and holds the loose end between her teeth while she looks for the scissors.

They are beside her foot. You pass them up.

“Thank you,” she says. “Now I can get dressed.”

Across the drying passage, a pair of trousers hangs with one leg over the line. Wind gets into the other leg and holds it out toward the orchard. Beneath it, the ground rises to a narrow split in the white ridge. Dora's upper roots have reached the split. The thick lower root is still caught on this side.

“Which way are they going?” she asks.

“The trousers?”

“They look decided.”

Noor has made two fresh folds in the living strips that run around the ridge. The thick old connection goes through it. She puts the coat on the chair and shows you the three places by touch, keeping the two new folds slack.

“Hold the old one open. If either of these pulls, don't answer by closing your hand.”

You rest your palm against it. Pressure moves under the heel of your hand. Noor asks Dora which new strip she wants first.

“The high one.”

Noor opens that fold a little. Dora presses into the new tissue. You see the far end lift from the wall, turn, and settle beside a damp patch. The old root still answers beneath your hand.

“Back?” Noor asks.

“Wait.”

The strip moves once more. A drop breaks away from its end.

“Now.”

Noor lets it settle into its earlier position. The greater pressure returns to the old connection. You have kept your hand where she put it.

They try the lower strip next, with the same support still open. This one takes longer. Dora brings it as far as the fork below the washing, pauses, and draws it back herself.

“I could stay there,” she says.

“With the tree still here,” Noor says.

“I can see that.”

They have not used both strips at once. Neither individual movement has required the old support to close.

Noor puts a clean stone on the floor beside each tested fold. She looks up at the trousers again.

“I want the upper slope,” Dora says. “You said there were red leaves.”

“They looked red.”

“Then let me go and be disappointed.”

## A thing Blaise could keep

You brought a board to put under the pressure marks. Until now you have been using it to keep your coat out of the wet. You pull it free and turn the clean face upward.

“One more movement,” you say. “Slowly.”

Noor takes over the old connection so you can draw. Dora asks which movement you want.

“The one at the beginning.”

“I've learned something since the beginning.”

“Then show me that.”

You follow the pressure with your left hand and mark with your right. The line bends toward the edge. You shift the board under the charcoal. A little pressure comes back at your wrist, farther from the place you are measuring.

You stop her and ask to begin again.

“It's the same hand,” she says. “You could move it.”

You move it. The new line crosses the old one. For a moment the crossed marks look like a person standing with both arms raised.

**Choice: Tell Noor and Dora that you want an account complete enough to keep a person.**

“If I understood enough of it, I keep thinking I could hold on to what matters. However far the body went.”

“In there?” Dora asks.

“In what it means.”

You rub a crumb of charcoal out of the crossing. It leaves a grey mark where the person's head had been.

Noor asks whether you mean an account somebody could read after Dora has died, or something through which Dora could continue. You say you are not sure you have kept those wishes apart.

“I could help with the first,” she says. “I don't know how to make your board do the second.”

**Choice: Keep that wish private. Ask Noor what she means when she calls understanding eternal.**

“Would this knowledge go on being hers if the activity stopped?” you ask.

Noor looks from the marks to your hand.

“What are you asking to go on?”

“The one who understands them.”

She takes a little time before answering. Dora holds her pressure still while you move the board off your knee.

*Both approaches continue below. Only the first tells the two listeners about Blaise's preservation wish. The second grants no private-motive knowledge to either listener.*

## The disputed joy

“An account could last longer than all three of us,” Noor says. “That would give it a great many years. I don't mean those years when I say eternal.”

“Then what?”

“This life understood through what it depends on. Not only that it happened on a particular afternoon, or that I watched her do it. What makes it possible for these different activities to be one life that changes.”

“You've followed the connections.”

“Some of them. Following can still mean watching one thing happen after another. I mean understanding why this particular thing is what it is, through the existence that expresses itself as bodies and thinking. The dependence belongs to it. You couldn't first make Dora and then decide whether she was going to need Nature.”

The speaking piece makes a small sound.

“I wouldn't have chosen the damp,” Dora says.

Noor smiles. She adjusts her wrist. The old support is taking a little more pressure than it did when you began.

“I used to think that saying everything depended on God had explained everything equally. Which meant it hadn't helped me understand anyone. Now when I begin to grasp a particular life through that dependence, the general sentence becomes difficult again. It asks something of my understanding.”

“What does God add to the tissue you can show me?”

“The tissue is a finite arrangement. It comes and goes. I mean the existence through which it can be and be understood at all. I don't put another person behind it to push. And I don't put thinking outside it, looking through a little hole.”

“You still haven't proved that there is only one such existence.”

“No. Not by drawing this.”

She touches the clean corner of the board with her free hand.

“But I am trying to understand her through it. Through what I take to be the same immanent cause expressed in these activities and in my understanding. If that knowledge is adequate, its truth doesn't depend on which afternoon I reach it. It isn't waiting for a later afternoon when my body has stopped.”

You look at the lines again. The figure with raised arms has gone. There are two pressure sequences and a stain from your cuff.

“You're telling me why the truth wouldn't die. I asked about the person.”

“I know.”

“Isn't that an answer to a different question?”

“To part of yours. I am this mind doing the understanding. I take its knowledge of itself and its body through that necessary dependence to be eternal. I don't take the remembered afternoon to go with it.”

“I don't know how to recognize the *I* in what's left.”

“Nor can I give you a way of meeting it afterward.”

She is quiet for a while. The trousers fall against the line. Then the wind lifts the empty leg again.

“That doesn't make me want fewer afternoons,” she says.

Dora asks her to move her little finger. She has been pressing the edge of the fold too tightly. Noor loosens it and apologizes.

“Why call it love?” you ask.

“Because understanding this gives me joy, and I understand the existence it depends on as its cause. More exactly, if I understand adequately, this finite act is Nature understanding itself. Its love of itself expressed through this mind. That is a much larger claim than being fond of Dora.”

“Difficult to say to her.”

“I'm still here,” Dora says.

“I am fond of you,” Noor tells her.

“You can give the bowl back.”

“After the game.”

“You said that before the last game.”

Noor looks at the bowl holding down her coat. You turn the board so she cannot put it over the bowl by mistake.

“And you're happy before we know whether this will work?” you ask.

“I was enjoying finding out how the new reach followed from what she could already do. Before there was a use for it. I wanted to understand more. I still do. Helping her reach the slope matters too. It isn't the prize that makes the understanding worth having.”

You think of the pressure that came back at your wrist, where you had not thought to put a mark.

“If it is knowledge of her.”

“Yes.”

## The disagreement changes the work

Noor asks for the charcoal. She joins the two sequences where the new strips leave the old support.

“We can bring them up together here.”

“While this stays open?”

She looks at the hand still holding it.

“Yes. I was about to close it when they answered.”

“We only tried one at a time.”

“Then I'll keep it open.”

You keep the board where she can see it. There is another part of the drawing you want to stop treating as settled.

“I think we know a real body. We can agree about its movements, test them, learn more. But a body can be an object for us at all because it is given in space, with changes ordered in time. What we know through those conditions may be perfectly objective. It doesn't follow that we know this life independently of them.”

“I don't think understanding its dependence needs a view from nowhere.”

“I'm not asking you to stand somewhere else. I'm asking what lets your knowledge reach what your argument calls its essence. You have not acquired another kind of intuition by understanding a pressure better.”

Noor studies the two lines. Her thumb has charcoal on it now.

“And you don't disprove that reach by making me inspect another fold. You'd be right to ask for the inspection even if the argument were true.”

“Yes.”

“Good. I was beginning to think you wanted a loose connection to settle it for us.”

She draws a short break in the join, leaving both observed sequences legible. You can still follow them.

“I claim to understand a necessary dependence. I don't claim this board has everything her body is doing. I was using it as if it had.”

Dora brings the pressure back to your wrist.

“Can we keep the argument at this end?” she asks. “I want to try the other.”

## Two available actions

You put the board on the chair and bring your palm beside Noor's on the old connection. Dora moves the pressure against your hand, then back toward hers.

“He's got it,” Dora says.

Noor loosens her hand. You feel the full pressure settle beneath yours before she lets go.

**Choice: Keep the old support open. Ask Dora to test both new strips together, stopping when she asks.**

Noor takes the new folds, one in each hand. Dora asks her to open the higher one first.

The pressure travels away from your palm. Then the lower strip answers too. You expected the old root to become lighter. Instead both new movements press toward the same narrow place beneath it.

Dora's voice catches in the speaking piece.

“Back.”

Noor returns the lower fold to its resting position. The old root takes the pressure again. After a moment Dora asks for the higher one to settle too.

You wait until she has moved the pressure against your hand in the familiar way before Noor lets the new folds settle into their resting positions.

Noor brings the lamp below the visible fork. The two strips join a narrow neck before they meet the broad lower root. You can follow both living edges into it. Separate successful trials concealed their shared limit under this load.

“That needs more room,” Noor says.

“I need less, for now,” Dora says. “Put me at the fork.”

They repeat the lower movement alone. It reaches the place it reached before, with the old connection still active. Dora stays there while Noor arranges the resting fold. The tree has not passed the ridge.

**Alternative choice: Postpone the combined trial. Keep the old support and help Dora use the lower reach already tested.**

You ask to inspect beneath the fork before putting both new paths under pressure.

“Then put me at the fork while you do it,” Dora says.

Noor eases the lower strip forward. Dora takes the movement as far as she did on the individual trial and keeps it there. The old connection stays active. Noor leaves a stone beside the other resting fold so nobody will mistake it for the one in use.

You hold the lamp beneath the fork with your free hand. From this angle the tissue passes behind the broad root. You cannot yet see enough to decide whether the new strips remain separate there. You will need access from below. Noor arranges to return that way with you.

*Only the combined-trial route establishes its result and the exposed shared neck. The postponed route knows that independence has not been established, not that a hidden neck has been discovered. Both leave Dora's present organization active and the whole-tree movement unfinished.*

## The upper light

Noor takes a broad wooden stay from the shelf and fits it beneath the old fold, clear of the answering tissue. You keep your hand in place while Dora tries the smaller movement again. The fold presses against the stay and remains open.

“Leave it there,” Dora says.

You lift your palm slowly. She draws the pressure back, then takes it to the fork once more. The stay has not shifted. Noor waits through the movement before getting up.

The largest sheet hangs between the fork and the ridge. Dora asks you to take it down.

You lift it clear of the line. The wet hem falls against your trousers. Noor finds this funny until you offer her the wet end.

Dora brings a little pressure against the wall at the fork.

“Here. Give me that corner.”

She holds it while you walk backward with the other end. You fold the sheet once, then again. On the second fold she lets you take the whole weight.

Beyond the white ridge, the upper leaves have turned toward the light. Some are red. Dora has already moved her small opening toward them.

Noor puts the bowl in the crook of her arm and lifts the stones out one at a time. She drops them into the repaired pockets.

“I'll bring it back before I play.”

“Put it by the picture,” Dora says. “She'll know which one.”

Noor hesitates, then agrees to ask the other woman where she wants it. She takes your board off the chair and gives it back without rubbing the lines away.

## Author and integration bounds

Selected source work: the review's references to Ethics I.D3–6, I.18, II.5–7s, II.13, V.24–36 and V.41–42; Kant's conditions of sensible cognition and restriction on positive noumenal cognition. Source scope is recorded in `docs/reviews/WORLD-ETHICS-V-12/NOTES-AND-BOUNDS.md` and WORLD-PHILOSOPHY-10. This adaptation claims no new complete primary reading.

Noor makes a defended, incomplete metaphysical claim. Conditional adequacy does not certify her achieved third kind of knowledge. She distinguishes particular understanding from the general assertion that all things depend on Nature, and intellectual love from reciprocal divine affection, approval or survival of autobiographical memory. Her happiness is not evidence for the claim. Blaise's objection concerns the warrant for cognition beyond the conditions under which an object can be experienced; it does not deny ordinary objective bodily knowledge. He also presses the unresolved individuation of eternal mind. Neither objection turns the hidden neck into a noumenon.

The finite engineering correction does not refute immanence. Noor says so and keeps her positive claim. Blaise can release his demand for a complete preservative trace without adopting it. Dora gains a chosen present activity and further means to investigate; no metaphysical answer unlocks that gain.

The earlier separate trials and the available fallback are now performed in this scene. The old support passes from Blaise to Noor for the drawing, back to Blaise before either branch, then to a checked mechanical stay for the smaller resting reach before their hands are used for laundry. The stay keeps the responsive fold open; the wood carries no consciousness or memory. The optional combined result must have its own source. The pressure board records observations and remains useful; it stores no mind. The bowl's return is an undertaking, not an already completed event. If earlier dialogue has not given Noor the bowl, add an actual borrowing scene before this encounter or remove the callback. Her game and Blaise's possible performance belong to the same washing-down gathering; they require route-dependent arrival or missed-promise scenes, not an automatic shared ending.
