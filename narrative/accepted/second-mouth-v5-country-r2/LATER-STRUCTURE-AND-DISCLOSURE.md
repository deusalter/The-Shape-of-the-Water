## What makes recurrence structural

The plot does not wait until movement IV to introduce recurrence. The opening crime uses an occupied return; the empty-place test is optional but actual; Emil demonstrates a repeating living support; movement II gives Blaise his first deliberate bodily return; movement IV repeats it under changed conditions. Both late operations resolve the particular return on which this investigation has depended.

Each authored recurrence has a finite boundary, current support paths and a before/after ledger. Outside people continue their own lives. Remembered testimony from a previous pass is not independent corroboration; knowledge of a private conversation does not pass to someone whose activity did not carry it. The local earlier configuration is real within the fiction. Its reappearance does not establish reversal of one universal objective time, nonsensible intuition or an eternal memory-bearing self.

The psychological pressure is not merely that Blaise should be more tactful. He can produce an earlier bodily feeling while retaining the later wish that caused him to seek it. He must decide what that achievement means for his continued existence and agency. The surrounding people do not become scenery he can rewind until they give him the right response. A refusal to repeat remains playable; investigation then uses encountered physical evidence and willing testimony instead of secretly performing the refused act.
