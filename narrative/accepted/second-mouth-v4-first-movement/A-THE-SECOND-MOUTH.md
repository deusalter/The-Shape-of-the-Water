# A. The Second Mouth

First incompatible treatment, saved 2026-10-04. Provisional audition, not selected or owner-approved canon. This is a bodily missing-person story whose living world exchanges its material. It is not the temporal voyage in treatment B. The same candidate names are being auditioned in different dramatic functions; these are alternatives, not a shared chronology.

## Ending written first: the mouth in the wall

Dora has asked you to bring something she can bite.

You bring a pear, then feel stupid carrying it through the long room. Every wall is full of fruit. Some of it opens when you pass. One small yellow mouth asks what took you so long.

“I had to find a knife.”

“What for?”

You look at the pear.

“Good point.”

The mouth is too small to take it whole. You hold the pear against the wall and Dora bites. A little juice comes down the plaster. Under your hand, farther to the left, another part of the wall tightens. You have learned to wait for that movement. Pull away too quickly and she loses the pressure she needs to swallow.

Three other people are holding the room with you. Noor sits in the window, one bare foot against the root that passes through it. A man you met this morning keeps two fingers beneath the seam of a door. The fourth person is behind the wall. You hear her complain that the pear is dripping on her trousers.

None of them hears what Dora says to you through the little mouth.

“Is she eating?”

She means the woman at the supper table. The new Dora, though neither woman uses that name for herself.

“She had bread.”

“She always hated bread.”

You almost say *you*. Dora feels the movement in your hand and closes her mouth.

The new woman had eaten three slices while you waited. She had wanted to know whether it was going to rain. You could have told this Dora about that. You had chosen the bread because it sounded like proof.

“She wanted butter,” you say.

The mouth opens again.

“Then take her some.”

You cannot leave yet. The last change has not reached the root under Noor's foot. If you stop answering the wall's pressure, the part of Dora you are holding will lose contact with the room before its new connections work. You have seen the same interruption kill one of the small transparent animals in the orchard. Its shape stayed perfect. It never took another step.

**Choice: Keep your hand in place through the final exchange. Give up the return supported by this part of the room.**

The pressure moves under your palm. First it pushes upward. Then sideways. You follow it until your shoulder begins to shake.

Noor says the root has warmed. The man by the door takes his fingers away. His hand is quite red. From the other side of the wall comes a delighted curse.

You let go.

Your hand has not changed its shape. You know that before looking at it. You put two fingers in your mouth and try the double whistle.

One note. You try again.

“Stop,” Dora says. “You've done that all your life.”

“I was checking.”

“You can check tomorrow.”

She has said it carelessly. There has never been a tomorrow for the supper room.

You put the pear on the sill where she can reach it. When you turn to go, she asks you to move it half an inch to the right.

**Alternative choice: Stop before the final exchange. Keep the return, and help the woman at the table leave it.**

This alternative must be chosen while withdrawal can still be safely managed. It cannot be offered as a mid-procedure abandonment after the other people have trusted their bodies to your timing. Dora's distributed continuation will have a known finite decline; she gets a performed farewell and the chance to spend that interval elsewhere. The woman at the table has already asked to leave and has her own reasons. Neither woman is discarded as the failed copy. This encounter is unwritten and must be auditioned before treatment selection could count as an ending lock.

## Opening audition: the difficult note

You used to whistle two notes at once. Dora would ask for it after supper. Lately she says even the one you have left makes her fillings hurt.

Tonight you mean to make her ask.

The room is preparing to change. A seam has opened down the middle of the long table. Everyone has moved their plates to one side. The new wood underneath is pink and soft; if you touch it, it takes the print of your finger and slowly lets it go.

You sit at the corner that has already hardened. You learned to test a chair before trusting it when you were six. That is most of what growing up amounts to here: a succession of quite specific things to sit on, eat around, step over and never believe merely because they look finished.

Outside, the country is turning its skin. The orchard moves past the windows root first. Beyond it, a white ridge splits and folds back. The exposed surface shines in the low light. Farther off, people walk along the old surface carrying beds and rolled rugs toward a new opening. A dog is refusing to go through. You can hear the argument, which has reached the stage at which the dog is being reminded of all the other things people do for it.

Dora Helmer brings a bowl to the table in the crook of her right arm. Her left hand is elsewhere for the evening. She says it is helping a tree stand up. She puts her mouth to the bowl and tips the last cherry stone onto the cloth.

“That one's for you.”

“The stone?”

“I ate the difficult part.”

She drops into the chair opposite. Without her hand she keeps forgetting where to put her elbow. It touches the new wood and leaves a small clean hollow.

You could tell her the table is still soft. You wait to see whether she will notice. She notices you noticing.

“Say something clever and I'll put your face in it.”

You push her plate toward her instead.

Noor Brangwen is on the floor beside the wall. She has opened the low panel with a bread knife. Two pale cords run behind it. She touches each with the back of a finger before putting her hand around it.

“Still using this one, Dora?”

Dora closes her eyes. Her left shoulder moves, though the empty sleeve remains flat.

“Tree's being awkward.”

“I'll leave it.”

Noor puts the cord down. The second one passes through the little wooden mouth under your chair. You made that mouth years ago, when the room could not hold your voice through a return without help. Its lips are rough where you carved against the grain. You have always meant to make them smooth.

The mouth has forgotten one of your notes.

You tried it after the last turn. One whistle, then air. The room brought back your unburnt fingers, the supper, the patch of sunlight on the table. It did not bring back the little movement inside your cheek that made the second note.

“I can put the old mouth on,” you say.

Noor stays on the floor.

“You can wait.”

“It's three screws.”

“Then it'll take you no time after supper.”

She taps the second cord with a finger.

“Leave this open until the tree clears the corner. Will you?”

You say you will. It seems easier than explaining what three screws can and cannot do.

You look at Dora. She has turned her head toward the window. The whole tree is going past, with her hand buried in the split beside its root. It is unmistakably hers. She has been biting the skin beside her thumbnail again.

The tree leans. Dora's shoulder rises. It steadies.

“There,” she says. “Useless thing.”

Noor asks whether she wants the hand back now.

“Let it get round the corner. I don't want to catch it.”

You have the old mouth in your coat pocket. Exact teeth, exact tongue, the knot in the wood where you once slipped with the knife. The new one is better made. The old one knows your whistle.

**Choice: Leave the mouth alone and ask Dora to help you find the missing movement after supper.**

You put both hands on the table where Noor can see them.

“Help me later?”

Dora gives you an appraising look.

“I was going to sleep.”

“Before the room turns?”

“During, if people stop taking bits of it off.”

You leave the old mouth in your pocket.

Dora begins to tell you about the man trying to persuade his dog to move. She does his voice badly, too high and too careful. It makes Noor laugh. You almost try the whistle to keep them laughing. You stop with two fingers against your lips.

Under the table, something gives a small hard knock.

Noor bends to look. The second cord has fallen out of the wooden mouth. Beside it lies a wedge the colour of the old table. Its narrow end is fresh pink wood.

Dora stops in the middle of a word.

Her left hand is on the cloth.

Outside, her other left hand still grips the moving root.

Noor reaches past your legs and takes the loose cord. You draw your feet back to give her room.

“I left it,” you say.

“I can see.”

She gets up with the cord in her hand. At the window she tries to catch the tree, but it has moved too far for her to reach its split from the floor.

“Give me the speaking piece.”

You fetch the warm slitted piece from the shelf. Dora uses it when her hand is away and she wants to swear at whoever is supervising. Noor kneels on the sill and presses it beside the wrist.

The voice that answers is Dora's.

“Who turned the room?”

At the table, the woman looks from you to Noor. She folds her new left hand inside her right.

**The branches meet at the moving tree.** Continue from Noor's answer, “Nobody,” below. On this route there was no concealed touch, no admitted promise-breaking and no inference that Blaise accelerated the interruption. The wedge and released cord are witnessed directly; on the covert-touch route they must be discovered separately.

**Choice: Wait until Noor looks through the window, then close the mouth and start unscrewing it.**

You need both hands to reach underneath the chair. Your fingers find the three screw heads. You shut the little wooden lips.

Dora stops talking.

She had begun to say something about the dog outside. Her mouth remains open around the first sound.

You let go of the screw.

“Dora?”

She turns her head. For a moment she seems to be listening to someone farther away.

Then she says, “What?”

Noor looks up from the panel. You have not yet moved your hands.

“What did you touch?”

“Just my mouth.”

You hear how it sounds. You take your hands away. The lips spring open. The cord has slipped out of their shallow seat; opening them does not put it back.

Dora picks up her spoon. She holds it in her left hand.

Between the table legs, a little dark wedge remains caught in the pink seam. It has not yet been drawn free.

Through the window the tree is still moving. Dora's other left hand is still holding its root.

Noor gets to her feet. She goes to the window so quickly that her knee knocks your plate to the floor. The plate breaks. No one looks at it.

“Say your name,” she tells the hand.

The hand is a hand. It cannot comply.

You start to laugh. You do not mean to. Noor looks back at you with her mouth drawn tight.

“Get the speaking piece.”

You know where it is. Dora uses it when she sends a hand away and wants to swear at whoever is supervising. You fetch it from the shelf above the door: a flat warm thing with a slit across one side. Noor presses it into the tree's split, beside the wrist.

It takes a moment to answer.

“Who turned the room?”

You look at Dora. She has put the spoon down. She is watching you look.

“Nobody,” Noor says.

“Then where's my chair?”

The voice comes from the little piece in the tree. Dora, at the table, draws her feet back under the seat.

You remember the mouth under your hand. You remember how little resistance its lips gave you.

“I'm here,” you tell the tree.

“I know. I can hear you breathing.”

Dora at the table says, “So can I.”

The tree is moving. In a few seconds it will be past the window. Noor catches the sill with one hand and the root with the other. It pulls her sideways.

“You can do this later,” she says. “Either help me or fetch a rope.”

**Follow-up: Take the root with Noor and hold the tree beside the window.**

**Follow-up: Fetch the soft rope and ask the woman at the table to help tie it.**

**Follow-up: Tell Noor you closed the mouth before taking hold.**

The woman at the table stands. You have not asked her yet.

“Where do you keep the rope?”

She puts a hand on your shoulder to get past you. It is warm. There is a little dampness in the palm.

The voice in the tree says your name.

You do not turn around quickly enough for either of them.

## Fixed history and causal requirements

The world is a finite living arrangement inside Nature, not a cosmic person whose organs are destined to improve Blaise. Its local matter changes in great exchanges. Organisms can preserve their reciprocal activities through replacement, distribution and reunification. People have developed ordinary practices around this fact, including lending bodily support, receiving visiting body parts and arguing over who monopolizes a shared room. They also make things that serve no maintenance function and can be bad at them.

The recurrence is material and local. The supper room is one organized body capable of returning to an earlier arrangement. External continuities carry a traveler's altered memory and bodily learning through the return. This does not establish an immortal soul. No portable token or meter certifies personhood.

**The actual missing-person event:** René has previously arranged the replacement of Dora's room-side body from an exact older configuration. He cuts its direct connection to the orchard while Dora's larger continuing organization is outside, and prepares the delayed release of its remaining connection through the room. He calls the replacement a rescue. He knows the connections matter because he has preserved them during earlier exchanges. His immediate motive is to prevent the orchard's new continuing organization from ending the fixed return on which another person's failing body relies. This is forced severing and substitution: it destroys an ongoing room-side branch and capacities while Dora's larger orchard-side organization continues. It is not a total death retrospectively canceled by finding her alive. The exact selected sequence is now recorded in A-CAUSAL-LEDGER.md.

**Blaise's optional act:** closing the wooden mouth interrupts an additional borrowed connection. It can make the substitution become observable earlier by cutting off a compensating return path. It is neither the sole cause nor a magical guilt penalty. On the refusal route the prepared principal cut exposes the same event later, with different available testimony and responsibility. The exact two-path dependency must be diagrammed before integration; the audition does not yet establish it sufficiently.

**What the investigation can establish:** which reciprocal processes Dora's continuing life actually uses; which were kept active and which were severed; when the replacement began acting separately; René's preparation and knowledge; Blaise's particular contribution; the finite requirements of helping each present woman continue. Shared memories and familiar speech alone cannot settle numerical identity. The authored distinction relies on an established physical law and a continuous causal history, not on which woman the protagonist finds more convincing.

**Evidence route:** first a harmless change in a small orchard animal demonstrates preserved organization through replacement. A second experiment distinguishes interruption from material replacement; the animal's decline must not be performed casually merely to label it a clue. The already dead specimens and natural failures provide comparison before any intervention in a person. Independent traces in the room, the moving root and the dependent person's support establish the cut's partial order. The speaking piece transmits a currently connected person's speech; it cannot evoke a dead life by playing a recording. Its limitations must be visible.

**The large discovery:** the country is not a set of separable containers with people inside. A person is a particular ongoing organization within it, and can survive a transformation that destroys her old silhouette. Other persons who help her remain distinct and have their own limits. The practical discovery does not itself establish Spinoza's whole metaphysics. A substantial positive argument for one immanent reality is made by someone whose rescue plan depends on it, and is challenged through the difference between an operational rule and knowledge of reality independent of human conditions.

**The ending's cost:** the return room and Dora's distributed continuation use the same finite stretch of responsive tissue. Keeping one in a fixed returning configuration prevents the other from developing the new reciprocal connections needed after the cut. Continuing the exchange ends this local return; it does not end all recurrence everywhere or earn a cosmic reward. Blaise's double whistle is one embodied capacity formerly sustained through that room. Act IV must actually let him recover and use it before the ending takes it away. The loss at the opening, its performed recovery, and its final surrender are separate events; the finale does not pretend that an already missing capacity has newly vanished. Other losses and gains must be specified and tested before the last choice.

## Long-form construction

Five large movements, provisionally 65,000–85,000 encountered words on a substantial route. At 180–240 words per minute this is roughly 4.5–7.9 hours of reading; actual play remains unmeasured. Extra routes and archival writing are excluded. Large places recur under changed bodily conditions rather than simply adding grotesque rooms.

- **The table and the moving orchard / 10–13k:** learn the inhabited body's ordinary practices, expose the simultaneous claims, preserve immediate lives, and establish what the interrupted connections actually did. Blaise tries his familiar return as a practical repair. It succeeds on a broken object and fails as a recovery of the lost continuity.
- **The country under its old skin / 13–17k:** travel through dwellings inside cast-off surfaces. Residents make instruments from discarded chambers and hold a festival whose noise is not a clue. Dora's distributed continuation acquires capacities and affinities Blaise cannot share. The woman at the table develops a different relationship with him. Each can speak with someone besides the player and decline an encounter.
- **Those who hold / 14–18k:** visit the people supporting the moving orchard. Cooperation involves actual competing bodily needs. Establish the dependent person's predicament and the route by which René prepared the substitution. A Kantian investigation into the conditions under which these strange bodies can be objective things changes the design of a failed test; a Spinozist account of attributes prevents treating the speaking wall as a mind pushing inert matter from outside.
- **The exact return / 15–20k:** examine the former arrangements in the dry country. Blaise is offered the particular old scene he wants, down to his recovered whistle. Taking it would require treating present people as raw material. Discover how his own returns have been maintained and reconstruct the principal cut with independent evidence. A dead life is not brought back by understanding it, yet the stronger claim of intellectual love receives a performed dispute about what remains true of its particular existence.
- **The second mouth / 13–17k:** choose and carry out a finite redistribution, follow each surviving person's actual future, and inhabit the first unreturned supper. A route keeping the room has its own consequential future and cannot consist of a paragraph announcing failure. Neither alternative can silently dispose of the woman whose claim the player has come to find less attractive.

The strengths are an unusually embodied ontology, recurrence involved from the opening, and a rescue target that changes through evidence. The dangers are using every ordinary pleasure as a symbol, reducing people to detachable capacities, and permitting the body's mystery to become a hidden spiritual identity test. The ending-first excerpt is sensuous and concrete, but its mechanism needs more preparation than B's visible route work. The source-supported individuality question is the core conflict; the story must earn rather than merely state its fictional answer.
