## Selected: A, The Second Mouth

I select A after writing two actual openings and ending encounters, reading the Hello Charlotte research and world-metaphysics source records, and receiving independent dramatic, philosophical and naming criticism. A makes the world matter in ordinary actions before explaining it. The woman at the table offers to fetch rope without permission from Blaise's theory of her; the continuing woman acquires an activity he cannot restore by recreating her old outline. Those acts are the strongest basis for an original long game in this packet.

B, No Common Noon, is preserved as the rejected alternative. Its heel-controlled evasion, vertical sea, public meal and independent older Noor are real gains. Its present alibi and severed-bridge motive need a more exact graph, and its end tends toward relinquishing a reunion rather than developing a new form of life. Selecting A costs B's naturally expansive voyage structure and unusually clear initial deceit. A will need an autonomous geography, multiple kinds of encounter and a protagonist whose appetite reaches beyond the whistle. The developing wish for an unborrowed self is one specific larger pressure, not a lesson every character should announce.

## Naming decisions at this checkpoint

Blaise Bloom is owner-retained. Noor Brangwen and Dora Helmer remain provisional selected working names for A, subject to the new encounters earning their particular significance. Their symbolic intent is not “names only.” Noor's maintenance of communication and refusal of another person's prescribed future place historical contact against literary self-development. Dora's painting and dispute with her prior picture give form and representation a concrete activity; Helmer pressures the assumption that a familiar domestic performance exhausts a woman's life. The painting scene was deliberately developed after the initial name critique; this record does not pretend it was present from the beginning. It must continue to be worth having apart from the allusion.

The critic's reservations remain: Noor's independent desire is underdeveloped in A, Dora/Nora can sound too directly assembled, and René lacks an ordinary or intimate scene. Verified provenance alone does not close those questions. All future names need the same historical-given/literary-surname and intentional-contribution check.
