# The note before the breath

Separate lead audition, 2026-10-04. This is the next performed scene after the preserved five-manuscript checkpoint. It does not change that checkpoint, version 4 or any old transcript. Selected for criticism, not installed or fully accepted into the later route.

Placement: after the first house encounter and the material comparisons have made the occupied restoration's mechanism intelligible. René is not directing this procedure. Noor and Blaise have returned to the supper room. Both Doras are elsewhere by their own chosen routes; neither is reinstated, observed or addressed through this return. The hazardous cast under Dora's place remains out of contact. The old mouth from Blaise's coat is still a piece of material, not a stored speaking consciousness.

## The place under the chair

The table has hardened around the hollow Dora made with her elbow. You put two fingers into it. There is room for three, if you turn them.

Noor is waiting beneath the low panel. She asks you to move your chair.

“How far?”

“Until I can get out from under you.”

You move it. She comes out with dust in her hair and lays the bread knife on the table, well away from the new wood.

The old mouth is on your plate. You have cleaned its screw holes with a splinter. Its lips are still rough where you carved against the grain. You run your thumb over them and catch yourself smoothing the wrong side.

“I could do it now,” you say.

Noor looks at the mouth.

“That is what you asked me to come back for.”

“I meant the carving.”

“I'd leave it until we know what it does.”

She crouches beside the place your chair usually occupies. Under the floor, the returning fold runs around the old seat and up the wall behind it. Its outer edge is a thick dry lip. Beside it, the new tissue continues along the wall toward the window.

You follow the fold from below while Noor holds the lamp. The edge turns inward before it reaches Dora's place. You can see the empty gap where the removed square was. Nothing bridges it now.

Noor gives you the lamp and takes a chipped cup from the shelf. She puts it inside your returning place and marks its outer surface with charcoal. On the other side of the lip she marks a scrap of wood.

“Watch both.”

She moves the little catch on the outer face of the lip. It has been holding the fold open. The cup settles with a small knock. Its handle points toward the wall again. The charcoal mark has gone. The mark on the wood remains.

She waits, then opens the fold and takes the cup out. You turn it in your hands. The chip is where it was before she marked it.

“That's this part,” she says. “Come and see the back.”

You go below again. The outer lip has not moved. You inspect the gap beneath the other chair before returning to yours.

Noor holds the old mouth beside the current one. They have the same three screw holes. The shallow seat of the current mouth is empty: the cord released at supper has not been pushed back into it.

“We don't use that cord,” she says.

She follows a different responsive strip from behind your place to the living wall outside the fold. When you put your left hand against it, pressure spreads beneath your palm and farther along the wall. It takes a moment to come back. You move two fingers. A little answering fold rises beyond the rim, clear of the returning place.

You ask Noor to touch it. She does, lightly, and you feel the touch along the outside of your little finger.

“More?”

“No. I felt it.”

She leaves that part alone. You keep moving your fingers until you can raise the answering fold without lifting your hand.

## What he agrees to

“That stays active,” Noor says. “It has to be answering before the inside turns, and it has to keep answering while we join you back through the changed place. I can hold the fold open. I can't promise to fetch your last thought afterward if you pull away from this.”

You look at your hand. Apart from the fingers moving, it has done nothing unusual.

“And you stay here?”

“Yes.”

“Exactly there.”

She puts the lamp on a chair outside the returning edge. Then she stands beside it.

“Here.”

You had meant her body. She has answered as if you were arranging an audience.

She asks you to show her how you will stop before the catch is released. You lift your right hand. She takes her fingers off the catch at once.

“After it begins, let me finish opening the fold. Keep the left hand where it is until you can feel the return answer from the inside again.”

You try the stop once more. This time you are not testing her. You leave your right hand raised while you look at the old mouth.

“I haven't said no,” you tell her.

“I know.”

**Choice: Stop here. Leave the old mouth uninstalled and take your hand off the wall after the support is released.**

Noor props the catch open. She asks you to ease the pressure out of the answering strip. You do it slowly, until the little fold outside the rim has settled. She checks it with you before you lift your palm.

Your hand is colder where it was touching the wall. You put it under your other arm.

Noor gives you the old mouth. You put it in your pocket without looking to see whether she is disappointed.

“I want to know what it would feel like,” you say.

“You can still want that.”

You sit down in the chair outside the rim. The lamp warms your knee. You put two fingers in your mouth and make the single note. There is a little breath after it where the other one should begin.

Noor winces. You try it once more, quieter, and stop of your own accord.

**If Blaise has borrowed Basil's pipe:** You take the pipe out, turn it to the angle Basil showed you, and bring the two tones together. There is room to make the lower one softer. You had not tried that before. Noor waits while you find it. You put it away when you have had enough.

**If Blaise has not borrowed it:** You ask Noor to listen while you change the shape of the single note. She sits on the empty chair. At first you can do only louder and softer. Then the air catches against the edge of a tooth and gives the sound a roughness you can repeat. You do not call it the old whistle.

You take the charcoal and mark the board beneath your chair where the lip turns away from the other place. Noor holds the lamp while you draw. You have come back with the same mouth you arrived in.

*Refusal continues to the material comparison and house inquiry, or the gathering if their actual order allows it. It grants the witnessed empty-place return and external answering relation, but no bodily restoration or recovered-whistle source. No one repeats the declined action offstage.*

## Going through with it

**Alternative choice: Have Noor install the old mouth. Begin the limited return while you maintain the outside connection.**

Noor changes the mouth while the catch is held open and your left palm keeps the outer strip answering. She seats the live link to your returning place in the old mouth's own groove. It lies beside the three screw heads, clear of the lips. The released supper cord remains separate.

She tightens the screws and asks you to move the two fingers again. The answering fold rises outside the rim. You feel her touch there. Then she lets you touch its edge with your right hand, so you can feel both places at once.

You draw a charcoal line across the back of your right hand, which will be inside. Noor draws another on the exposed side of your left thumb, beyond the lip. She does not ask what you want to remember.

You choose the burnt fruit Emil gave you. Its taste is still faintly present. You keep the choice to yourself.

“When you're ready,” Noor says.

You make two taps with your right hand. She releases the catch.

The live strip tightens beneath your left palm. Noor keeps its inner join open as the fold moves around it. A pulse runs from the wall through your wrist, back into the changing place; you feel it arrive inside your arm while her finger is still touching the answering tissue outside.

The chair becomes cold through your trousers. You feel the cold a moment before you feel yourself sitting on it. The edge presses into a part of your thigh you had moved clear of it. Your right foot is farther under the chair now. You have not felt the movement between those positions. The pressure through your left wrist has kept answering.

The taste of burnt fruit vanishes. There is something hard against a back tooth. You know it is a pear fibre before your tongue reaches it.

The left hand on the wall goes on being touched. Noor has kept one finger against the answering fold outside. She is where you asked her to stand.

You wait for the rest of her to become earlier.

Dust falls from her hair onto her shoulder. She rubs it away with her free hand and leaves a pale streak. The mark is new.

“Blaise?”

You remember being asked for the whistle. You can almost fit Dora into the empty chair opposite. The elbow hollow is there; the woman is not. Noor has said your name from beside the lamp.

You try to say *wait*. Your tongue takes too much space in your mouth. It catches against the familiar back tooth and then slips into a position you have not been able to find for a long time.

There is the movement.

You had remembered it as a tightening in the cheek. It begins much farther back, almost at the throat. The certainty of your memory remains exactly where it was. The movement is elsewhere.

“Can you hear me?”

You nod. Noor asks for a word. You say her name because you can see her looking at you. The sound is a little too clear. Your own ear is closer to it than you expected.

She opens the fold fully. The pressure already answering through your left wrist broadens as she exposes the inner join. She asks you to bend the two fingers and wait for the answer from both sides.

You do. She checks the new inner join before letting you settle the answering fold outside and take your hand away. The whole room has not moved to catch up with you.

The line on the back of your right hand is gone. The line on your left thumb remains. Noor's charcoal is still lying on the plate. You pick the pear fibre out of your teeth and put it beside the charcoal.

“I was thinking about something,” you say.

“Do you remember it?”

You remember the burnt fruit with painful clarity. There is no trace of its taste now. You can remember liking it while your tongue insists you have just eaten something else.

“Yes.”

“You don't have to tell me.”

You put two fingers in your mouth.

The first note rises. The second comes under it, without the effort you have been rehearsing. You stop, astonished at how easy it is. Then you do it again before Noor can speak.

She lifts a hand to her ear.

“I heard it.”

You let it fall to one note, bring the other back, and stop them together.

Nobody in the opposite chair asks you to repeat it.

## After the sound

**Choice: Ask Noor to listen once from the doorway. Play the recovered whistle for her.**

She takes the lamp with her. You wait until she is outside the rim, farther from you this time, and make the sound smaller. It takes more care than making it loud.

At the doorway she closes her eyes. You keep the lower note steady and let the upper one move. You had forgotten you could do that.

“Again?” you ask.

“That last bit.”

You begin at the last bit. The chair is still cold. You plant your feet differently and continue.

**Choice: Ask whether the return can be tried again with no active part left outside.**

“So I could find out which feeling comes back without me holding on to it.”

Noor looks toward the lip, then at the pear fibre.

“I wouldn't know how to keep you through that. I won't help you turn the part that's carrying the question off.”

“There could still be somebody to answer.”

“Yes.”

You look at the chair opposite. You have already met somebody who came back in an earlier arrangement. She had wanted to know whether her painting was finished.

Noor does not take the old mouth away. She leaves the catch propped open. You sit with your new old tongue against the roof of your mouth until you can stop pressing it there.

*This choice is a request and Noor's refusal, not a secretly executed unsupported restoration. The game must not offer a disguised action that kills an organization merely to collect an ending.*
