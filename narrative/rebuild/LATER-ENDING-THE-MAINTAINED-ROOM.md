## Before the operation

Emil has taken the plant out of its pot. Soil covers the sill and most of his sleeve. He has been holding the bare roots over a bowl while you speak. Now he looks down as if someone else has put them in his hand.

“It was smaller when I started.”

You offer to take it.

“Get the bowl underneath. It's dripping.”

You hold the bowl. He puts the roots down carefully, keeping the lowest leaves out of the water.

René stands at the other end of the room. He has brought the old clean strips, though everybody has told him the decision is not about replacing those strips again. He has folded them so neatly that the edges look cut from one piece.

Emil asks him to go outside.

“I need to show him the lower connection.”

“You showed me. I can show him.”

René looks at you. You keep the bowl where Emil has asked you to hold it.

After René has gone, Emil takes a leaf between two fingers. He has broken it without noticing. There is a wet mark on his thumb.

“Don't make me thank you,” he says.

“I wasn't going to.”

“People generally aren't. Then they're standing there.”

You put the bowl on the sill. He goes to the low opening and shows you the part that must remain free to move. He can describe its ordinary rhythm much better than René did. Where René said *wait*, Emil says what you will feel before the pressure changes, and how much room to leave your finger.

You follow him once. He makes you do it again because you have pressed too hard at the end.

“It won't go away if you loosen your hand.”

You loosen it. The movement comes back.

“There.”

He settles beside the opening. You are outside the old fold, where the experience you both carry will continue. The smaller returning part is empty of anyone else's activity.

**Choice: Stop before beginning. Tell Emil you want to speak with the orchard woman once more.**

The second choice returns to an actual conversation, not a repeat of an already completed death. It does not secretly reverse his decision. The first continues below.

## The orchard after the tables

Alma has brought the wedding cloth in. It hangs from a low branch, with damp yellow folds touching the ground. Three little pink marks run along the hem. She is trying to decide whether to cut them out or add more.

Dora wants her to leave them alone.

“You said the same thing about the blue room,” Alma says.

“I was right.”

“You don't know which marks I mean.”

“Describe them.”

Alma describes them very badly. She uses her fingers until she remembers Dora is not looking through her eyes. Then she takes a fold of the cloth over to the root and lays the stained part against the responsive tissue beside the speaking piece.

“That tells me how wet it is,” Dora says.

Alma laughs. It takes her by surprise, and she apologizes at once.

“Don't do that.”

“Do what?”

“Be sorry before I've even done it.”

You have brought the lamp. There is still plenty of light, but you had wanted something to carry. You set it on one of the cleared tables.

Dora asks how the speech went. Alma tells her the bride forgot the middle and made everyone go back to the beginning. Her husband tried to help by supplying a word and supplied the wrong word. For the rest of the meal they called each other by it.

“What word?”

Alma looks at you.

“He knows worse,” Dora says.

She tells it. Dora laughs through the speaking piece. The sound is quieter than you expect. You bend toward it and she asks you to move your head out of the way of the air.

You sit on the ground.

The root no longer takes the weight of the upper branches. That change has already happened in the order Dora chose. She has kept the rain ribbon and the small pressure by which she can answer a hand. The places that have stopped answering have been examined; there is no unvisited second orchard being kept in reserve.

You put your hand where she has asked. The pressure comes, pauses, then returns with a different force. You want it to be a sentence. You have never agreed on a sentence for it.

“I'm here,” you say.

“I can feel that.”

After a while she asks for Alma's story again. The rude version, including the wrong word. Alma sits beside you and begins.

When the pressure stops, she keeps talking until she has reached the part Dora laughed at.

You stay with the root through the rest of its changes. Noor comes down from the ridge and sits on the other side. She checks the remaining response with you, in the way you have both learned, and waits through the interval in which it could still answer. The little mouth remains seated. Nobody detaches it to create a clean moment for you.

The last active reach loosens under your palm. It does not move back toward your hand.

Later, Alma takes the lamp inside because it is beginning to rain.

## What continues

The woman who came from the table has put her picture on a wall beside the performance space. Basil found a place for it, but someone has hung a cloak over one corner. You recognize the blue before you recognize the chair.

She comes over and moves the cloak. She has paint on the back of her wrist.

“Did she see the rain?”

“Yes.”

She looks at you for a long time. Then she asks you to hold the picture while she changes the nail.

You hold it. This time it hangs at the height she wants.

She goes back to the other people. You remain beside the doorway until Basil moves the mountain panel. The chair on the platform comes into view.

At Emil's house the plant has been tied to the outside of the window. Its lowest leaves move whenever the room exhales. The stain on the sill has not come out.

Emil has kept the return himself since your last visit. You watch one complete movement before he asks you to take the next. He has not forgotten which connection you tend to press too hard.

Your double whistle still works. In the doorway you set your fingers against your tongue and make both notes, briefly, before taking the next movement from Emil. He looks up from the sill.

“Tell me what happened at the orchard.”

You take your fingers out of your mouth.

**Choice: Tell him about the final interval, including that you stayed through the last response.**

You begin with the cloth because it is the first thing you remember clearly. You have to go back and explain who Alma is. Emil gets you a chair. When you reach the silence at the root, he puts his hand against the place in the sill that answers him.

He does not stop the movement. He asks you to go on.

**Choice: Tell him only that Dora died. Say you cannot give him the rest tonight.**

He closes his eyes. You wait for him to tell you that you owe him an account. He opens them again and asks if you will come back.

You can make that promise, or leave it unmade. The room goes on moving while you answer.

## Fate and philosophical limits

This ending does not make the living table woman a consolation prize, does not redeem René through usefulness and does not diagnose the protected adult's wish to live as metaphysical stupidity. The orchard individual's death is real within the selected route, following the established finite physiology, not inferred merely because a speaking device went quiet. Those stages require actual earlier investigation and scenes. The source of the limits remains a construction obligation, not a permission to add a death timer.

Noor's claim about eternity, if the player has heard it, neither generates a farewell voice nor takes away her wish that Dora had more time. This scene leaves space for that argument's earlier significance without explaining the death as its lesson. Blaise can still make and keep an ordinary promise in the continuing house. The preserved return cannot make this later conversation never have happened outside its limited reach.
