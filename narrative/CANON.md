A shallow water tray, a daylight slit and the inclined mirror produce a reflected bright stripe. The pool itself is not the optical tray.
3. A concrete gallery pier lies within the door's opening arc. An old felt pad is too low to protect the mirror frame after the large mirror is mounted. 5. Miriam entered willingly. She tested the interior pull while Simon held the door slightly open. After he closed it, light leaked at the cloth seam. Simon added a black cord around the cabinet and across the door. He intended a darker seam. He failed to consider the exit as a whole. In this fictional history he did not intend confinement against a request, injury or destruction. These are author commitments, not player-certifiable facts about an inaccessible mind.
6. Miriam wanted to leave, pulled the ring, heard the latch release, and pushed against a door held shut by the exterior cord. She requested exit twice. Simon heard her and answered the first request as though she were objecting to the optical sight: “One minute. Let it settle.”

## Character claims and the author's restraint

Simon initially treats a reproducible sight without an observer inside as evidence for an “unconditioned sight.” He later distinguishes invariance through altered empirical conditions from the stronger claim. He still argues that stable relations may be relations of the world itself. His unsafe apparatus does not refute that argument.

Miriam believes in an immanent reality whose finite bodies and minds are expressions, not independent substances. She thinks adequate understanding of particulars can disclose their dependence in that reality and can be a form of intellectual love. She can fairly articulate a Kantian limitation without accepting it as her own final view. She admits she has not established her full argument. Her calm, fear, help and grief are not authorial tests of whether she has adequate knowledge.

Ada trusts a practical reconstruction, but has no assigned anti-metaphysical creed. She asks what an explanation is sufficient for because she needs to do a particular thing. She also sings, avoids offers that may not last, admits the unchecked plan, and wants her name cleared.
