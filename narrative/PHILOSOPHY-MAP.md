# Philosophy in The Mercy of Morning

The owner rejected the bodily-support central premise. Its exact prior map is preserved in `archived/second-mouth-documents/PHILOSOPHY-MAP.md`. The active literary sources are the ordered modules under `mercy/`; lead_writer owns their final prose. The delivered proposal and earlier passes remain separately preserved. This map is an index to actual scenes and reviewed arguments, not an assertion that gameplay proves a philosophical system.

| Argument | Actual encounter and consequence | Limit or disagreement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kant: objective event order differs from the order of subjective apprehension | `a2.sequence` and `a2.confirmation`: outside construction has advanced while Blaise has an earlier-looking memory. Independent accounts locate the restored morning after his request. He reads that history publicly instead of treating his felt innocence as proof. | Empirical evidence establishes an event order; it does not alone prove the transcendental argument that objective succession requires rule-governed connections. |
| Kant: empirical realism and conditions of experience | `a1.west-road` and `a2.the-ground`: travelling outside Aubade discovers another experienced region. Blaise argues that conditions of knowing an object do not provide knowledge of it independent of all those conditions. | The outside is not an unlocked noumenal realm. Disproving Erasmus's local claims does not settle the ground of all existence. |
| Kant: reason's demand for the unconditioned, limits of speculative inference | `a2.the-ground`: Blaise challenges the passage from the concept of necessary substance to its existence; Vera challenges his restriction of adequate understanding. | Their philosophical disagreement survives the solvable missing-person case. Neither speech receives a factual-correctness score. |
| Kant: apperception and the Paralogisms | `a1.second-return`, `a2.sequence`, `a2.reason`: a unified first-person experience does not disclose a simple enduring substance. Blaise still has a history and reasons addressed to him. | These are not demonstrations of a soul, its destruction, or a causeless will. Forgetting a request does not itself settle personal identity or responsibility. |
| Spinoza: substance, attributes, modes and immanence | `a2.the-ground`: Vera distinguishes essential nature from changing states, argues against two substances of the same attribute, and explains the infinite-substance claim. Blaise presses individuation and existence. | An absolutely infinite substance is argued for rather than inferred from everything being connected. Erasmus is a finite agent, not an adequate image of God or Nature. |
| Spinoza: conatus, activity and passion | `a2.purpose`, rehearsal, and `a2.a-form-of-love`: understanding a desire can change what its satisfaction would mean; appetite need not disappear for a person to become more active. | Vera wants approval herself. Insight is not guaranteed dominance over stronger passions, and positive change is not certified by a warm social scene. |
| Spinoza: intellectual love, eternity and blessedness | `a2.a-form-of-love`: understanding particular existence through attributes is proposed as finite participation in immanent self-understanding. Blaise asks what warrants adequacy and how this love remains the activity of this mind. | Eternity is not endless autobiographical memory. Intrinsic pleasure in rehearsal does not prove the stronger Ethics V account; the argument and its objection remain active. |
| Freedom and accumulated interpersonal choices | `a2.decision` and the closing scenes: Blaise can refuse restoration without proof that his refusal is uncaused, or request a genuinely happy morning which removes the present listener's intervening history. Earlier acts affect Julian's invitation. | The ending is a personal act under unresolved philosophical pressure, not selection of the correct doctrine. |

The return is original fictional metaphysics: Erasmus can restore a remembered morning within Aubade, cannot summon what has left, and can preserve his memory and one witness's. The outside history continues. These limits are demonstrated, disputed and disclosed within the story; they are not Kantian or Spinozan doctrine. The player's unchanged transcript is a reading aid, not a supernatural faculty attributed to the restored Blaise.
