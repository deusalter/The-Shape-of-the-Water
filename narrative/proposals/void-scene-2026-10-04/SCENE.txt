THE MOUTH OF THE MORNING

Exploratory scene. Not installed canon. The owner's correction asks for a much more radically trippy experience, rather than making every surreal event serve an explanation of the existing relationship plot. This draft tests that direction. No historical philosopher or real black hole is claimed to explain these fictional events.

Blaise stepped out of the theatre and put his foot on nothing.

The other foot still belonged to the room. He could feel the floor through it. Between his knees the doorway had become impossibly far away.

He looked down.

The square lay beneath him, small enough to cover with his hand. Above it was another square. Between them something black occupied the place where distance should have been.

Light made a furious ring around it. The upper part of the ring folded over the black centre and appeared beneath it again. He watched a bright knot travel both ways at once. There was no surface on which his eyes could settle. Even the darkness seemed to have been cut out of a larger darkness.

A chair passed through the light.

Then a street.

Then the theatre, with its roof removed. Inside it a blue figure was lifting the horse's head. Blaise recognized his own coat. He raised his arm. The figure continued lifting.

Another theatre came around. In this one he was under the table. In the next, the room was empty except for the chair Erasmus had taken onto the stage.

He could not tell whether the rooms were circling the darkness or the darkness was holding them open.

“Which one am I in?”

His voice came from the empty chair.

He had not heard himself speak from the place where he stood.

The ring widened. Its bright edge passed through his chest without reaching him. There was a room inside the edge now, and a Blaise inside the room, and on that man's chest the same little line of light.

He tried to close his eyes.

The room remained. His eyelids were visible from the outside.

For a moment he understood why he had wanted an invisible part of himself. A part which could never be put into the picture. He had called it many things, depending on whom he was trying to persuade. Here he wanted it with the blunt desire of an animal for a place to hide.

But an observer behind these eyes would need another view. And behind that view, another. He could keep moving the person he meant without finding the man who was frightened.

He was frightened here.

The theatres drew together. Walls passed through walls. One Julian was taking a door from its hinges while another leaned against Blaise, asleep. The sleeve lay across the departing man's arm. The two gestures remained perfectly legible. They did not become one gesture.

Blaise reached toward the sleeping man.

The doorway moved under his hand.

“Wait.”

Julian did not look up. Neither Julian did.

Blaise remembered saying the word. He remembered thinking he would say it. He could no longer place the thought before the sound. Something had happened; he was certain of that. The order with which he usually made something into an event would not hold.

Was this a view of the order without him? He wanted to say yes. It would mean his life could be understood from somewhere that did not have to live it. Yet he was still looking. Still receiving one thing, then trying to join it to another. Even this impossible sight arrived under conditions. The enormity of it supplied no exemption.

The light turned white.

Everything in it lost its outline. The sleeping man, the departing man, the hand Blaise had extended. For one intolerable instant he could see no place at which loving Julian ended and the world began. He tried to think the distinction back into existence. He needed it. Without it, he could not even say who was losing whom.

Then the outlines returned, sharper than before.

His hand was still his hand.

Perhaps belonging to one existence did not make things exchangeable. A particular life could be an expression of it without being a detachable piece. He could almost follow the thought. It offered a kind of intimacy for which nobody had to choose him.

He wanted somebody to choose him.

The black centre grew.

The white ring had become a narrow line. It still contained a street, a chair, the uneven shoulder of a coat. He could see them with a precision their size should have forbidden.

“Give me the morning,” he heard.

His own voice. Close to his ear.

He turned. A blue figure stood where he had just been standing. Its arm was extended. Farther behind it another figure was turning toward the same place.

He moved sideways.

The figures stayed where they were.

One of them opened its hand.

Blaise had not opened his.

For the first time since he entered, the light ceased to turn.

The open hand contained a small, ordinary patch of darkness.

He could not see what it held.
