# The other shore

Provisional scene audition. This replaces nothing in the released game. Isambard Cuttle and Hypatia Honeychurch are proposed names, supported by the naming packet cited in REVISION.md. The passage occurs after an inhabited coast has appeared, upside down, above Aubade's market.

The woman in the boat is eating something green. Every time she spits a pip over the side, a dark star appears above your head.

“Don't stand there,” Isambard says.

You move.

“I meant under it. Oh, never mind.”

The boat has no underside. You can see its seats, the woman's bare feet, a sleeping dog. Behind them is the black centre of the eclipse. Around that centre the market stretches into a burning hoop, stall after stall after stall. The butcher's red awning passes through the sun. Nobody has stopped buying meat.

Hypatia is laughing. You have never heard this laugh from her. She puts her hand over her mouth, takes it away, laughs harder.

“Sorry. I had it wrong. For years, I had the bloody thing upside down.”

“The sky?”

“My drawing. Give me something.”

You offer the folded play. Up in the boat, the woman lifts its wet corner from under her heel. She folds it. The crease opens under your thumb. Hypatia snatches the page and begins drawing on the king's speech.

The woman in the boat looks at you.

You dislike the taste of the fruit.

No. She dislikes it. You have never tasted it. You are still tasting it. There is a tough piece between two teeth you do not have. Your tongue goes searching; her tongue finds it. She is worrying about someone who has not arrived at the harbour. You cannot find the person's face, or a name. There is the worry, intimate and unattributed, beside your irritation that Hypatia has used the clean copy.

You open your mouth.

The woman says, “Don't swallow it.”

Your mouth is empty.

The black centre fills with windows. Through each you see the woman looking at you, except that you also see yourself looking through the windows, and behind that self another opening shows the butcher pulling paper around a joint. The paper is white enough to hurt. Its whiteness spreads between the stars. You want to shut your eyes. She wants to keep looking for the harbour.

For a moment you are both furious.

It ought to be possible to say who is furious. I can say that I am. There: an answer. But she can make the same answer, and I have not heard her make it, I have made it. I am frightened of losing my place in a sentence. That sounds laughable. I would laugh if there were somewhere to put the laugh where she wouldn't have to feel it.

You try to remember Isambard's full name, anything that belongs to your own ordinary head. Isambard Ernest Frank Christian Loveless Goodfellow Cuttle. He once made you say it before he would lend you his good boots. You got stuck at Loveless and he lent them anyway. Isambard, who is standing beside you. His sleeve is wet. You want his sleeve. You put your hand out and catch it.

“I'm here,” he says, then, sharply, “That's skin.”

You loosen your grip.

Suppose I am not a little man inside a larger one. I have been able to say that quite comfortably with a cup in my hand. But the cup was always mine, the discomfort mine, the clever sentence mine. I left the owner sitting there while I denied him. Now this woman wants the harbour. Her wanting does not ask my permission to be here. Neither does it make me want the harbour. I want this to stop. I want to keep enough of it to know what happened. Those wishes pull against each other so hard that for an instant I cannot see.

“Take your hand off him,” Hypatia says.

“It is off.”

“Not you.”

The woman has reached towards the place where Isambard stands. Her fingers close on empty air. She snatches them back, embarrassed. The embarrassment reaches you before you see her hand move.

“He's yours, is he?”

“No,” you say, much too quickly.

Isambard gives you a look.

The woman spits the piece of skin into her palm. You feel relief in a mouth that remains empty. Then she turns the boat. The black windows turn with her, showing streets where people are taking washing down, a room full of yellow bowls, a man asleep beside a lit stove. None of them turns towards you.

“Don't lose it,” Hypatia says. “The turn, I mean. It isn't a picture in a second head. The knowing and the turning belong to the same thing. If you can find how they're one, you can... Blaise, turn her back.”

You try. Your shoulder moves. The boat carries on.

“I'm going that way,” the woman says, pointing. “What is wrong with all of you?”

“I didn't mean... oh, hell.” Hypatia scratches something out on the play.

The woman is already farther away. She shakes her empty hand at you, impatient, and the taste disappears. The market contracts to its usual impossible size. Above it, her boat crosses the sun. You can still see the dog sleeping.

Hypatia offers the play back. A hole has appeared in the paper where she drew the harbour. Through it you see moving water.

You put your finger behind the hole.

Something on the other side bites it.
