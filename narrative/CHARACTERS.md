# People and the player

Provisional lead-writer selection, 2026-10-03. Three recurring principals are a deliberate compact scope. Their exact voice evidence is in VOICE.md; their name sources are in NAMES.md. The philosopher's name is never a character ID.

**Ada March, char_001.** Maintains the bath, sometimes sings second alto with the bath choir. Wants the cabinet made safe, the accusation corrected and enough of her evening back to eat. She knows what she heard and did, and can reconstruct the cord; she cannot diagnose Miriam's distress or inspect Simon's motives. She did not complete the swing check after writing the reminder. She does not conceal the unticked plan. With the player she supplies actions in short, revisable units. With Simon she narrows the question until he can answer it. With Miriam she becomes longer-winded, because an offer of a lift threatens a disappointment they already know. Their earlier relationship is a former intimacy, not a plot secret; the game does not require a label or a reunion.

**Simon Vane, char_002.** Builds shop displays. Wants his work to matter beyond its sales function and wants another person to see without his introduction dictating the experience. This conflicts with his practiced habit of getting ahead of an audience. He made a real effect, added a dangerous binding, misunderstood a request and sent an unjustified accusation. He acknowledges those actions when asked. His remembered intention is plausible and incomplete, not a lie detector reward. With Miriam he can tease and argue; with the player under pressure he qualifies, self-corrects and sometimes watches his own phrasing too closely. With Ada, talking about his sister, he can stop performing. A public insult changes his willingness to address the player privately, not the objective evidence he provides.

**Miriam Verney, char_003.** Teaches adults to swim; paused her work to care for her late mother. Wants an accurate sequence, dry clothes, agency over her own experience, and access to the kind of intellectual conversation Simon's work might sustain. She opposes his advertised inference but shares the wish for fundamental understanding. She is the most philosophically practiced speaker, yet her ordinary talk spends energy on socks and a former partner's choir timetable. Her private account of her mother bears on the ethical reach of understanding without making illness a benevolent lesson. Pressing “panic” before hearing the sequence shuts down that private account. The factual testimony remains available and unchanged.

**The player.** Owns the broken mirror and has prior ordinary acquaintance with the bath. Their attention is physical and relational: where a door opens, who gets looked at, when a task is handed over. They may be patient, precipitate, publicly corrective or protective of a person at another's expense. They cannot choose a culprit unsupported by evidence or make the world conform to a doctrine. The role is bounded: establish the sequence, choose its audience, and decide on further help. The relationship decisions matter even when they do not change the mechanism.

**Place.** A present-day English-speaking coastal town is implied but unnamed. The saltwater bath closes for winter. Pool work, folded chairs, stored coats, choir rehearsals, a temperamental cooker, family visits and borrowed equipment supply ordinary uses of the building. No unexplained public authority deputizes the player. The owner's material stake and an accusation sent to a small group create the occasion for a negotiated account.
