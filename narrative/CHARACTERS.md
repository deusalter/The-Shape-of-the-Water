# People and the player

Provisional lead-writer selection, revised 2026-10-03 after the male-protagonist/psychological-direction correction. Blaise is the central character; the three original principals retain their distinct lives. Emmy and Ruth are drafted additions, not separate philosophical factions. Their exact voice evidence is in VOICE.md; their name sources are in NAMES.md. The philosopher's name is never a character ID.

**Ada March, char_001.** Maintains the bath, sometimes sings second alto with the bath choir. Wants the cabinet made safe, the accusation corrected and enough of her evening back to eat. She knows what she heard and did, and can reconstruct the cord; she cannot diagnose Miriam's distress or inspect Simon's motives. She did not complete the swing check after writing the reminder. She does not conceal the unticked plan. With the player she supplies actions in short, revisable units. With Simon she narrows the question until he can answer it. With Miriam she becomes longer-winded, because an offer of a lift threatens a disappointment they already know. Their earlier relationship is a former intimacy, not a plot secret; the game does not require a label or a reunion.

**Simon Vane, char_002.** Builds shop displays. Wants his work to matter beyond its sales function and wants another person to see without his introduction dictating the experience. This conflicts with his practiced habit of getting ahead of an audience. He made a real effect, added a dangerous binding, misunderstood a request and sent an unjustified accusation. He acknowledges those actions when asked. His remembered intention is plausible and incomplete, not a lie detector reward. With Miriam he can tease and argue; with the player under pressure he qualifies, self-corrects and sometimes watches his own phrasing too closely. With Ada, talking about his sister, he can stop performing. A public insult changes his willingness to address the player privately, not the objective evidence he provides.

**Miriam Verney, char_003.** Teaches adults to swim; paused her work to care for her late mother. Wants an accurate sequence, dry clothes, agency over her own experience, and access to the kind of intellectual conversation Simon's work might sustain. She opposes his advertised inference but shares the wish for fundamental understanding. She is the most philosophically practiced speaker, yet her ordinary talk spends energy on socks and a former partner's choir timetable. Her private account of her mother bears on the ethical reach of understanding without making illness a benevolent lesson. Pressing “panic” before hearing the sequence shuts down that private account. The factual testimony remains available and unchanged.

**Blaise Bloom, protagonist.** An adult man who owns the broken mirror and knows the bath. In the first-night case he can inspect the mechanism, address testimony and choose its audience. In the selected continuation his own apparent persistence becomes the case's central problem. He remembers an encounter the present Ada has not had, anticipates a voice before its located sound, and tries to recover a touch by reproducing a gesture. The person before him responds to what he does now. He can ask for present kindness and still fail to obtain the recognition he wanted. His question is whether the life he remembers remains a life, whether this “I” belongs to both occasions, and what makes a different act his own. His hope of being allowed another chance meets the problem of why the return begins after someone else's injury. He can press an immanent account of purpose and intellectual love without becoming a mouthpiece for Pascal or a pupil graded by Miriam. He is not defined by a diagnosis, concealed job, rent, workroom or an obligatory trauma. His focal attention follows mouths, hands, breaths and the timing of his own responses. The name's deliberate Pascal/Bloom resonance is documented in NAMES.md. `loop/SELECTED-LEDGER-L2.md` distinguishes his remembered sources from current evidence and avoids certifying a substantial soul merely from the felt continuity of thought.

**Emmy Vane, drafted continuation.** Simon's sister, who has helped prepare the invitation. Works at a dental practice and has a son whose arrangements interrupt her day. She distinguishes what she changed on the page from what she knew about the apparatus. Her ordinary voice can be brisk without turning every sentence into a principle. She can finally answer Simon's remembered childhood chair story: she wanted people to look because she liked the drawings. Source-file work and that answer are performed in CYCLE-01 A/B, which remain drafts for adaptation to the selected psychological arc.

**The two encountered Miriams, L3 development selection.** An explicit new exterior departure and sustained co-presence encounter put a Miriam who remembers Sunday beside one who remembers the original accident and the renewed arrival. The exterior speaker is a separate conversation target with her actually acquired history; she is not an all-knowing narrator or a portable store of Blaise's private conversations. The interior speaker is not defined as unreal, inferior or empty. Their common name remains intentional. Prose distinguishes actual position and encounter history, with no permanent “real/copy” label. Both need ordinary and pressured scenes beyond being evidence before this construction can become the completed story. The new ending gives the exterior witness a refusal to surrender her remembered day and the interior speaker a private thought she cannot make the other think. These are drafted events, not currently live v2 scenes.

**Ruth Garth, drafted continuation.** An adult swimming learner and invitation recipient. Her first concern about “wherever you stand” is whether she can use a chair. She can enjoy the effect, question its promise, decline an audience and remain dissatisfied with three tiles of swimming while recognizing she has done them. She is not an infallible outsider. Her lesson and optical visit are performed in CYCLE-01 A/B; those scenes must earn their place around Blaise's central experience rather than delaying it for length.

**Place.** A present-day English-speaking coastal town is implied but unnamed. The saltwater bath closes for winter. Pool work, folded chairs, stored coats, choir rehearsals, a temperamental cooker, family visits and borrowed equipment supply ordinary uses of the building. No unexplained public authority deputizes the player. The owner's material stake and an accusation sent to a small group create the occasion for a negotiated account.
