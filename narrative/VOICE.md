## Ada: a task can make her expansive

She notices what someone can do with the thing in front of them. Her inferences stay near an action and she can admit she left one undone. She seldom wraps a correction in an apology. With the player, an ordinary answer can open into a remembered social occasion. With Miriam, she rehearses an anticipated disappointment in a long sentence, then returns to exact practical times. Do not imitate only the terse corrections or turn every meal into her signature. She is capable of letting a joke go without finishing it.

### Exact ordinary anchor, workshop
“I do second alto,” she says when you look. “Did. They’ve moved it to a room over the funeral shop. Too far on a Thursday.”

You ask whether she misses singing.

“I can sing here. It’s the coming in I miss. Someone says the wrong thing about the weather and you’re in.” She tips the pan toward you. Two potatoes have rolled together in a little butter. “Want one? Cold at the bottom. The cooker cuts out unless you keep your hand on the plug. I’m not fixing it until I’ve eaten.”

### Exact pressured anchor, shared

“You said you’d check the swing,” Ada tells him.

“You wrote it.”

“I told you.”

“I don’t remember.”

“All right. I remember telling you. You remember reading it. The mirror still hit the pier.”

### Exact intimate anchor, ada_miriam

“I liked hearing you from the kitchen,” Miriam says. “When you didn’t know you were being heard.”

“I knew. You stopped the tap.”

They look at each other. Ada is the first to look down. She has a strip of loose towelling caught around one finger.

“I don’t want a lift because you nearly got hurt,” she says. “You’ll do everything for two weeks and then you’ll hate the drive. It’s forty minutes each way. You’ll start telling me it’s good to have time to think.”

“Then let me come once. I might hate it the first time.”

Ada frees her finger. “Thursday. Quarter past nine. If they do the long one, twenty past.”

## Simon: the explanation gets ahead of the object

He anticipates an objection, narrows a term, watches the effect of his wording, and occasionally catches himself improving the account too much. His metaphysical interest is not just a disguise for shame: he gives the strongest sustained challenge to Miriam's inference. With Ada his joking becomes tentative and he waits for her to decide whether she heard a joke. Do not add a self-correction to every speech. The ordinary sock exchange matters because he need not make a theory of it.

### Exact ordinary anchor, gallery

Simon has gone upstairs to fold the borrowed chairs. Miriam has followed him as far as the landing. She tells him one of his socks is inside out.

“It’s the soft side,” he says.

“It’s the label side.”

“The label has given up. It’s become soft.” He raises his trouser cuff. She looks despite herself. He asks whether she wants a chair; she tells him she has just climbed nineteen stairs to get away from chairs.

### Exact pressured anchor, simon_account

“You had an inside release,” you say.

“I kept checking the latch. Pull, click. Pull, click. I was looking at one part. I knew how that part worked.”

You ask whether he considered what the cord would do to the whole door.

“No. Or I considered it for half a second and considered something else. I don’t have an honest way of measuring the difference now.”

### Exact intimate anchor, simon_ada

“When we were children she used to make me introduce my drawings. Stand on a chair. Tell everyone what they were. I liked the drawing. I liked all of it until I had to stand up. Then I could hear the difference between what I’d made and what I could say.”

“You say plenty now.”

“Yes. I got good at that part instead.” He checks whether she has taken it as a joke. She has not. “I wanted her to sit in there without my telling her anything.”

“You’d have told her where to sit.”

“All right. Where to sit. Which way to look. When to...” He stops. “I could have let her get bored.”

## Miriam: precision about another person's invitation

She marks sequence, corrects the scope of a claim, and notices when a question is taking ownership of an experience. She can sustain an argument longer than the others; ordinary talk interrupts that capacity rather than proving she secretly only speaks in doctrines. With Ada she makes an offer smaller so it can be accepted. In private she says what a memory cannot prove, then still permits herself to value it. Avoid making every denial a balanced philosophical pair. Her pressed variant becomes plain and closed.

### Exact ordinary anchor, bench

“That one has the good elastic,” Miriam says.

“You’ve told me.” Ada puts it over a rail.

“The other ones leave a trench. I can still see the trench when I go to bed.”

“Buy bigger socks.”

“Then there is sock in front of my toes. I swim to avoid having things in front of my toes.” Ada makes a small sound through her nose. Miriam looks satisfied by it.

### Exact pressured anchor, optics

“Suppose what is fundamental has to be understood through itself,” she says, “rather than borrowing what it is from something else. You can’t get two such things just by pointing to different arrangements of the same nature. These bodies differ in position, motion, what they can do. I think those are real differences within extension, not separate pieces of self-existing being. Thought is another expression of what exists in its own right. It isn’t a little substance arriving to drive the bodies.”

“Why must it be the same being?” Simon asks. “Two things could have something in common without becoming one thing. And why must anything satisfy your definition of independence?”

“Ordinary things can share properties. I’m talking about what constitutes an entire nature, not whether two people have brown eyes. If you’ve given them the same complete nature, you still owe an account of what divides them. Their positions are already relations within extension. I don’t think you can use those relations to make separate ultimate sources of extension.”

### Exact intimate anchor, miriam_private

“Too many teaspoons. I gave away the cups, inexplicably. She used to make barley water in a saucepan. You couldn’t make soup in it afterward. Not without a lemon hanging around at the back.”

She asks what you know of her mother. You say only that you remember Miriam’s lessons being canceled for a year so she could look after her.

“Two winters. I would explain why she couldn’t go downstairs alone. Next day I’d explain it again. I was collecting reasons as though there would eventually be one she couldn’t refuse. I thought if I understood enough, I would stop being angry with her.”

Did she?

“Less often. Never for good. Sometimes what helped was understanding why a sound frightened her. Sometimes what helped was a neighbour sitting there while I went out.”

## Shared rhythm and revision limits

The `shared` anchor below lets one question change the sense of the preceding one. Ada's short interruption is not treated as a refutation of the desire for complete understanding. Simon specifies what he needs to act on, Miriam preserves the larger inquiry, and the task changes from speech to preparing an open model.

Miriam intervenes before he replies. “You can ask what made every one of us possible. I want to ask it too. But we don’t need the complete history of the world to decide whether to put someone back in that cabinet tomorrow.”

“I know,” Simon says. “I’m trying to work out where an explanation is allowed to stop.”

“For doing what?” Ada asks.

He looks at the plan. “For opening it tomorrow.”

“It stops there.” She touches the cord. “Take the door off. You can carry on with the other question while I do that.”

Miriam asks her to wait until you have kept whatever measurements you need. Ada puts the screwdriver down. This costs her a little effort. She has begun to enjoy the prospect of removing the door.

Simon picks up the small camera. “Before you take it apart, will you look at what it actually did? From outside. You can see every part.” Miriam asks Ada whether they can make an open model on a bench. Ada looks at the time, then goes to find something that will hold water.

## Lead's name-stripped and transfer inspection

The fresh-reader packet is `reviews/VOICE-BLIND-PACKET.md`; its separate key must not accompany it. This lead has already inspected the passages without attribution, but cannot be a blind independent reader of their own draft.

Specific confusion: “I want to know what would be sufficient” and “I don’t have an honest way of measuring the difference now” can both sound like Miriam because both speakers qualify what would warrant a claim. In context Simon's first protects his work from a verdict he thinks cannot be met; his second protects an account of his own attention. Miriam's qualifications usually protect the scope of a claim or another person's access to her experience. The surrounding turns, not an ornamental verbal tic, need to preserve that distinction.

The short question “For doing what?” cannot reliably identify Ada alone. It is retained because conversational usefulness is more important than making every sentence a signature. Conversely, transferring Ada's long Thursday objection to Simon would require a different lived expectation of repeated care and a different willingness to mention exact inconvenience. Transferring Simon's childhood introduction to Miriam would change his attempt to be seen without dictating the response, which the cabinet repeats in a compromised way. Transfer tests are editorial reasoning, not a numerical uniqueness result.

Remaining evidential scope notes belong to the investigation but should be tested in a paced human reading.
