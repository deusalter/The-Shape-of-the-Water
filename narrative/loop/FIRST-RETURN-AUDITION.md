## Opening addition, before the first arrival's choices

You meant to tell Ada on Monday. Then Simon needed the mirror and there was a job you could do without saying anything difficult. You helped him carry it down the stairs, your hands in the places where your hands always went. On the landing he asked whether you would need it back for the new room at the bath. You said you would need it back.

That was true. It was not the answer.

Your new job starts on Tuesday. Repairs and alterations, work that arrives in numbered sacks instead of in the arms of somebody who wants to stay while you do it. Thirty-four hours a week. A wage at the end of the month whether anyone likes the shape of their calves. You have read the offer often enough that the page opens at the fold through the starting date.

Ada cleared the upstairs room for you. She put the old benches on end, repaired the fluorescent strip and found a table that does not rock. You had agreed to see the first customers on Wednesday. Their times are in a message you have not answered since the other offer arrived.

There was always going to be a moment to tell her. Monday was about the mirror. Tuesday she was busy. By Friday, telling her would also mean explaining the days in which you had not told her.

Now the mirror is in pieces. You recognize the damaged silver at the old corner, and the dark wooden frame against which you have measured so many strangers. There should be a pin cushion hanging from the top. You took it off before you lent the mirror and put it somewhere sensible. You cannot remember where.

“Henrietta,” Ada says. “Don't stand in the glass.”

You look down. One bright piece lies an inch from the side of your shoe. You step around it, toward the bench.

### Early choice

- **“Tell Ada now that you cannot take the upstairs room. Ask to speak after the immediate safety problem is clear.”**

  You ask her to come as far as the workshop arch. She puts the saucepan on the bucket and follows. You speak quietly, with the laundry fan running between you and the two people by the water. “All right,” she says. “Not now.” You tell her the Wednesday appointments need changing. She looks at you then. “Not after Wednesday, either.” When you return, Simon asks what you were saying. Ada tells him it is not about his cabinet. He and Miriam have seen you speak, but have not heard the news.

- **“Deal with the broken mirror first. Keep the job offer to yourself for now.”**

  You take the folded offer out of your coat pocket, mistake it for the wrapping Simon has asked you to hold, and put it back. Ada is holding the saucepan. Nobody has required you to decide that this is still the wrong moment. You have decided it.

Editorial effect: both are available without knowing the future loop. The second is an actual present withholding. Neither changes the established accident or creates a virtue score. Henrietta's obligation is concrete and visible to the player before this choice.

## First experienced return

Entry: after the next-day inquiry and a closing scene at the service passage. Before this event the route has established that the service passage leads to a side yard; it is not the entrance used on arrival. Henrietta exits carrying a wrapped saved rectangle of mirror. The timing reference is her phone; the bath clock is known to be slow. A return's exact conditions are not yet inferable.

### Passage

Simon has put cardboard over the corners. The shortest side of the parcel rests against your thigh. He offers to carry it as far as the yard, but you want to find out whether you can manage it before promising that you can manage the bus.

Through the open service door you can see the bins, the wall and a patch of light where the evening reaches the paving. Ada is behind you, counting the old locker keys into a jar. Each one makes almost the same sound. After the seventh she starts counting again under her breath.

Your phone vibrates in your pocket. You cannot reach it without putting down the glass. You remember the screen showing Sunday, and the time a little after seven, when you checked for the bus. You had more time than you needed. You are quite certain of that, although later you will wish you had been certain of something more useful.

You turn the parcel to fit through the opening. The cardboard catches the jamb. There is a small resistance, then none.

You are holding the outside door of the bath.

It is lighter than it should be. The smell is different, warm water and the burnt edge of something cooking. You have no parcel. Your hand is on the entrance handle, not the side door. A chip in its varnish presses the ball of your thumb.

At the shallow end, the largest surviving piece of mirror is propped against the pink rabbit float. Its ears rise on either side of your reflection.

Ada sits on the upturned bucket with the saucepan in her hand.

You do not move. The doorway pulls against your hand. Someone has opened an inner door, and the air wants the one you are holding.

The glass is still bolted to the cabinet door in pieces. There is more of it than there was a moment ago. The cardboard corners are not here. Simon stands beside the propped mirror. He turns his phone face down before you can read it, and you remember the accusation on its screen.

You look down for the parcel. Both hands are empty. Your coat sleeve has a pale thread caught at its cuff. You pulled that thread off while Emmy was talking. It is there again, looped through the buttonhole.

“Ada,” you say.

She shifts the saucepan against her knee. “Mind where you put your feet.”

You remember her saying something like that. You cannot remember whether it was those words. The failure terrifies you more than an exact repetition would have. You want the world to give you a sentence you can put beside another sentence and hold still.

Miriam is sitting near the water with her hand wrapped around a bag of peas. She flexes her fingers and stops. Her face changes before you can decide whether this is the moment you remember.

You go toward her too quickly. She draws her feet closer to the bench to make room.

“What happened?” she asks.

You point toward the cabinet. She thinks that is what you are asking about.

“I asked to come out,” she says. “Before Ada cut it.”

“Yes.”

She watches you.

“I know.”

“How?”

Your phone is still in your pocket. When you take it out, its screen shows Saturday. The job offer names the same Tuesday, still ahead. You open it again, pressing hard enough to select the wrong message. The news you have not given Ada is waiting where you left it.

#### If the earlier route actually recorded the wrapped-glass photograph

The photograph you took before collecting the parcel is absent from the recent pictures. You remember making it. You remember checking that all four cardboard corners fitted in the image. The folder stops at a picture taken before your first arrival.

### Return to the shared passage

Miriam puts the peas on her knee. “Henrietta. Sit down before you drop that.”

You sit beside her. The bench is dry where it was wet a moment ago, a day from now, when Ruth had been getting her glasses. You cannot settle on a name for the time. You can remember the water on the wood without giving it a place.

### Choice L1-R01: the first account

- **“Say exactly what you remember: Sunday closing, the wrapped glass, the service door, and this arrival happening before.”**

  Miriam listens without looking toward Simon. “And you remember talking to me?” You say yes. “That could mean several things. I believe you're frightened. I don't yet know what happened.” You want her to say something stronger. She asks whether you have eaten. You tell her that is not an explanation. “No. It's something I can help with while we find one.”

- **“Ask to compare the date and the glass before explaining the memory.”**

  Miriam tells you the day. You ask her to show you her phone. She does, holding it where you can see rather than giving it to you. Its date agrees with yours. You tell her you remember the glass already being wrapped for delivery. “It hasn't,” she says. “You can look.” She keeps her phone in her hand. You have made her uncertain of your purpose, not certain of your history.

### Common passage

Ada brings a chair because she has seen the way you sat down. You tell her you do not need another thing to sit on. She puts it beside you for the phone.

“I know what a chair's for,” you say.

“Then use it for that.”

You put the phone down. The screen goes dark, and your own face replaces the date. It is the face you had this morning. It is also the one you had yesterday. Neither fact helps.

Ada asks whether the glass caught you. You turn your hands over. There is a faint old burn beside one thumb from the pressing iron. No new cut. You remember the ache in your forearm from holding the parcel, but the arm itself is not aching now.

“That isn't what I mean,” you tell her.

“I haven't heard what you mean.”

You say that you came here yesterday, that you know how the door was held, that the next day you helped unpack the glass. Ada looks toward Simon, then back to you.

“If you've seen a film, say you've seen a film.”

#### If the previous occasion included the incident recording

You start to answer that you have. You remember the small voice on the recording, and the door filling its frame. It is not an answer to the thing she is asking now.

#### If the previous occasion used only the physical proof route

“I heard you. I heard all of you. We tested the door.”

“We haven't tested it,” Ada says. “We haven't moved it.”

### Return to the shared passage

### Choice L1-R02: a test that others can witness

- **“Ask Ada to keep a prediction in view before the next message arrives.”**

  You remember a message from a visitor asking whether the bath has somewhere to leave a bicycle with a child's seat still attached. You write down the wording you recall, including the extra question about taking the seat off. Ada reads it, folds it and keeps it under the key jar. Simon opens the visitors' conversation far enough for all three of you to see its latest received message. It is not the one you have described. He leaves the phone in view, with the current time visible. The prediction is in Ada's keeping before any new message arrives. You have set up a test. Its outcome has not happened yet.

- **“Ask to examine the cord and door now; keep the apparent return as an untested account.”**

  Ada lets you see the cut ends. You recognize their unequal lengths. You could have learned that by looking now. When you tell her about the back loops, she points you toward the safe way around the bench so you can inspect them without moving the cabinet. “There they are,” she says. “You can show me how you think it worked.” She is willing to examine the object without accepting the occasion you remember.

Editorial constraint: the prediction's message must be authored and encountered on the earlier route before this choice can quote its content. If absent from that route, substitute a different earned prediction or omit the prediction choice. No invented memory grants evidence the player never read. Any changed outgoing communication can alter the next incoming reply; guard the test or record that confound explicitly. A successful prediction remains one current observation and Henrietta's attributed recollection, not independent proof of a universal reset.

## The private confidence is not a receipt

Entry: later on this returned occasion. Offered only if the player encountered Miriam's intimate account of her mother's barley-water saucepan in the previous occasion. Miriam has not disclosed it on this occasion. Ada has gone to inspect the workshop. This is an interpersonal branch, not required evidence for the recurrence.

### Passage

Miriam asks you to hold the bag of peas while she changes the cloth around her fingers. It has softened at the corners. You remember how she held it against a different towel, then stop trusting the order of the towels.

You want her to know you are not merely guessing. There is a thing you could say. You can feel it becoming ready, shaped like a gift.

### Choice L1-R03

- **“Repeat the saucepan detail as if the two of you have already shared the conversation.”**

  “The lemon stayed in the soup,” you say. “Your mother's saucepan.”

  Miriam stops with one finger inside the loose fold of cloth.

  “Who told you that?”

  “You did.”

  “When?”

  You say yesterday, and hear that the word will not do. You try again. “In the day I remember.”

  She takes the peas from you. They slide against the wet cloth; she has to catch them with her good hand.

  “I haven't told you about that.”

  “But I couldn't know unless...”

  “You do know. I can hear that. I don't know how you know.” She looks toward the workshop doorway. “I also haven't decided to talk about it with you.”

  You had expected the detail to bring the rest of the conversation with it: the trust, the quiet, the way she had let the saucepan remain between you without explaining it away. Instead there is a fact in the room that she wants to put somewhere you cannot reach.

  “I thought it would help,” you say.

  “Help you show me something.”

  She does not say anything else. The bag rests between her hand and her knee. She no longer asks you to hold it.

- **“Tell her you remember a private conversation about her mother, but ask before repeating any detail.”**

  “What about her?” she asks.

  You tell her you will not say more unless she wants you to.

  “You remember I looked after her. You knew that already.”

  “More than that.”

  She tightens the cloth and tests it with her thumb. You wait for her to ask what you remember. She does not.

  “Don't use that to convince the others,” she says. “If there's another thing you can test, use that.”

  “And with you?”

  “Not now.”

  You give her the peas. She accepts them, then turns the bag so the softer part will lie against her hand. The action is so familiar that you have to stop yourself from helping before she asks.

- **“Keep the remembered confidence private. Tell her something she could not know about you.”**

  “I've taken a job,” you say. “I start on Tuesday. Away.”

  Miriam looks up from her hand.

  “Does Ada know?”

  You answer according to what you have actually told Ada on this occasion. It is harder than you expected to keep that separate from the other answer, the one you remember getting.

  “I still have the Wednesday names,” Miriam says. “Two people asked me to remind them.”

  “I know.”

  “They don't.”

  She puts the peas back on her knee. You tell her you had been waiting until you knew what to say.

  “You knew that bit,” she says.

  There is no wonder in her voice. You are grateful and disappointed. She asks which Tuesday. You have to tell her how soon it is.

Exit: her response changes current relationship access, not the truth of the physical event or a cosmic score. The quoted remembered detail is explicitly a source from a prior occasion; using it as if permission persisted changes the encounter. Respecting her refusal is not a key that makes time advance. The last branch is a disclosure of Henrietta's actual present commitment and must update only Miriam's current knowledge.
