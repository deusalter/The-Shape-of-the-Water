## The argument needed here

The unity required for my representations to be mine does not, by itself, give an intuition of an enduring, simple, separable soul. In the numbered identity passage, Kant distinguishes the identity of the subject accompanying representations from a demonstrated numerical identity of a thinking substance through changed circumstances. The latter cannot be obtained merely by analyzing the thought “I.” Likewise, distinguishing my thought from my body does not show that the thought can exist without a body. The conclusion diagnoses the substitution of an ability to abstract from empirical conditions for knowledge of an actual separate existence.

This does not conclude that Blaise does not exist, that his memory is false, that ordinary personal recognition is impossible, or that empirical investigation of continuity is forbidden. It limits a particular inference to the nature of the self. His memory of a particular encounter is already empirical material, unlike the supposedly pure rational psychology Kant is criticizing. A scene must not pretend that Kant directly decided an experimentally encountered apparent recurrence.

## Dramatic use and objection

Blaise can argue that the same first-person ownership is present in memories of both occasions. He can also have a reason for wanting this to establish something that survives the lost day. Miriam may grant the occurrence and ownership of the recollection while asking what establishes the further claim about a separable enduring bearer. Simon should be permitted to object that ordinary identity is supported by converging embodied evidence without an inspected metaphysical core. That objection prevents the dialogue from making all practical recognition hostage to an impossible proof.

If two bodily continuations with overlapping memories become observable, their co-presence is an empirical finding. Calling one the original soul, the false person, or the unique continuing I would require a further argument. Two people saying “I” is not itself an experimental refutation of the unity of apperception: each speaker's claim concerns that speaker's representations. Conversely, Kant's restriction cannot make an observed second body disappear or settle its treatment by the others.

## Rejected uses

- A memory that survives the represented return does not unlock a noumenal soul.
- An inability to prove an enduring simple substance does not prove that a person is only an illusion.
- Remembering an unchosen earlier action does not supply identical causal conditions for a new choice.
- Literal reverse travel in the same objective time would remain a substantive fictional challenge to the Second Analogy, as recorded in the loop research. The game presently represents later experience followed by an earlier-looking local state, not an omniscient chronology of the universe.

Any extraordinary bodily or material continuation selected for the fiction is an author stipulation, not an event deduced from Kant or Spinoza. The new ending auditions will distinguish what occurs from what their speakers take it to establish.

## Follow-up inspection for L3's eternity exchange, 2026-10-04

Reinspected the retained Elwes *Ethics* extraction `research/philosophy/sources/spinoza-ethics-inspection.txt`, lines 8050–8135 (V.21–26, especially 21–23 and its scholium), 8170–8245 (V.29 scholium through V.33), and 8260–8338 (V.34 scholium through V.37). The distinction is positive as well as restrictive: V.22–23 claims an idea of this body's essence under eternity; V.29–31 connects the mind's understanding of itself and its body to God; V.32–36 makes intellectual love an activity of God's self-love insofar explained through the particular mind. V.34s expressly rejects the ordinary confusion of eternity with surviving memory.

The ending gives Blaise the objection that a truth remaining true does not by itself preserve its knower. Miriam answers with her stronger immanent account of a particular mind's activity, while conceding that the body's co-presence and the wooden strips have not established that account. This is an adaptation of the argument's pressure, not a complete demonstration of V.23 or a settled scholarly account of what “remains.” The hard question about individual eternity remains contested; the scene must not reduce it either to endless duration or to the harmless assertion that facts stay true.

Nothing here identifies one of the two Miriams as the eternal component of the other. Both are embodied contemporary speakers. Neither composure nor willingness to help is a test that she possesses intuitive knowledge.
