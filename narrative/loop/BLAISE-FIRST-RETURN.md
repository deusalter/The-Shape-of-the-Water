# The mouth before the words

Selected male-protagonist scene audition, superseding the Henrietta/workroom direction. Blaise Bloom is an adult man. His given name intentionally refers to Blaise Pascal; the surname to Leopold Bloom. The central stake is whether his continuity of memory belongs to the same life, and whether a changed act is his or merely another part of what repeats. No diagnosis, secret former life or employment problem explains this away. The first-night accident remains fixed before the return point.

This sequence includes its own earlier setup. It does not require an unencountered cuff thread, photograph or confidence. The partial mirror, rabbit float, bucket, saucepan, phone, wet sock and peas retain their established positions at the arrival anchor. More than one interpretation of recurrence remains possible.

## Before the return: two breaths

Entry: late on the next day, after the picture inquiry and before leaving through the already-established service passage. This common setup is encountered on every route that reaches the first return. Ada is waiting for Simon to finish wrapping the saved glass. Miriam has gone to get her coat. The open model is empty.

### Passage

Ada has put two chairs beside the water. She sits on one and sets the saucepan on the other. You stand long enough for her to notice.

“You can move it,” she says.

You put the saucepan on the tiles and sit. The water has stopped reaching the lip of the bath. Each small wave turns back before it gets there. Under the gallery the sounds arrive twice, once from the thing that makes them and once from a direction where there is nothing to see.

Ada breathes in through her nose. There is a faint catch before she speaks.

“I know who's beside me by that,” she says.

“By what?”

“The breath before we come in. At choir. There's a woman I hardly know to talk to. I know when she's going to start.”

“Could you pick her out with your eyes shut?”

“If we were singing.”

“And if you weren't?”

“Then I'd ask her name like anyone else.”

You close your eyes. The dark behind the lids is reddish where the high window was. Ada moves one foot. You can hear the sole separate from the tile, but you cannot tell where she has put it down.

“Blaise.”

You open your eyes. She has moved the saucepan between your shoes.

“You'll kick that if you get up.”

You move it farther away. She watches you do it, then closes her own eyes for a moment. Her face does not look absent. It looks occupied by something to which you have no view.

You ask what she was thinking.

“Whether I put the potatoes away.”

“Did you?”

“Yes.” She opens her eyes. “Asked myself that twice now.”

Miriam calls from the changing passage that she has found her keys. You get up. The chair catches behind your knee; Ada steadies it with her foot. For a moment you are held between the chair, the floor and the hand she puts against your wrist. Then you stand clear and she lets go.

You say thanks. She says, “There you are.”

You have no reason to preserve those three words. You hear them and turn toward the service passage.

## Crossing

Simon has covered the glass with cardboard. He asks whether you can hold it upright while he puts the last strip of tape across. When you take the weight, the reflected room vanishes beneath the paper.

You can see the side yard through the service door. Two bins stand against its far wall. The lid of one is raised by a bag that has been put in sideways. Behind you, Ada counts locker keys into a jar. You have heard the first seven by the time Simon lets go of the parcel.

The edge of the cardboard catches the jamb. You turn it slightly. Your right hand tightens.

The weight is gone.

Your right hand is on the entrance handle. The same hand, the same direction of strain, nothing between the fingers except the painted metal. The room has moved to the other side of you.

At the shallow end the largest piece of mirror rests against the pink rabbit float. Its ears rise on either side of your reflection.

Ada sits on the upturned bucket holding the saucepan. Simon stands by the glass. He turns his phone face down.

You can hear what he is about to say. You hear it in his voice, with the little lift on sight. Then his mouth moves and he says it again.

“The sight worked. Whatever else happened. I need that understood.”

The second voice is in the room. The first had been so close that you thought it was in the room too.

Miriam is on the spectators' bench with the peas around her hand. Her shoes are paired beneath her. One sock is wet. There is no chair beside the water where you have just been sitting.

You look through the entrance. The laundry opposite has white overalls on its line. One sleeve is inside out. You look back at the glass. Your face is where a face should be, moving when you move. The rabbit's ears remain on either side of it.

### Choice B-R01: make a difference

- **“Say ‘Blue’ before anyone asks you a question.”**

  “Blue,” you say.

  Ada looks at the pool. “What is?”

  You do not answer. You remember her holding out the saucepan, saying something about the cord. This is not that sentence. She is waiting for an answer to a word you chose because it belonged to nothing.

  Simon follows her look toward the water. “It's the tiles,” he says.

  “I didn't mean the water,” you tell him.

  Ada brings her gaze back to you. “Then what did you mean?”

- **“Remain silent and deliberately keep your hand on the entrance handle.”**

  The door pulls against your hand. The closer wants it shut. You remember letting it go, but you keep holding it.

  Simon says your name. You watch his lips as if they are part of the latch.

  Ada asks you to close the door. You keep it open another second, then release it. The closing sound comes after you let go. You cannot make it arrive before.

  “Cold coming in,” she says.

  Your palm retains the pressure of the handle. You press it against the other palm, trying to distinguish the two.

### Common passage

You want the changed words to be enough. For what, you cannot yet say. If you can do something different, perhaps you have not been returned in the same way as the glass. But you remember more now. That difference was already here when you came through the door.

“Blaise,” Miriam says.

You know you will turn. You turn anyway. She has not asked you to come closer. You cross the tiles and sit near her, leaving the space she left for the peas.

“I was here,” you say.

“You're here.”

“After this. You were going home.”

She watches your mouth. Now she is the one waiting for it to provide something she can use.

“The cabinet was open. The glass was wrapped. Ada was counting keys.”

Ada looks at the empty jar on the ledge. The keys are still on their hooks.

“I haven't done them,” she says.

“I know.”

“You keep saying that.”

You take out your phone. Saturday. The day has a name and the name is wrong. Miriam shows you her screen without putting it in your hand. The two agree. Simon's does too. The clock above the entrance is still a minute behind his phone.

You tell them you remember Sunday. You use the word remember because it is the same kind of thing as remembering Monday's loan. The second memory has a place in everybody else's account. The first has your insistence and the feeling of cardboard against your fingers.

Miriam asks you to set the phone down. There is a chair beside the bench. You put it there, screen upward. It goes dark. You wake it and read the date again.

### Choice B-R02: give an account of the return

- **“Describe the crossing and the vanished parcel. Distinguish what you saw from what you think it means.”**

  You begin with the seven keys, because the small number gives you somewhere to start. Simon asks whether the glass was covered before you crossed. You say yes. He asks whether you saw the yard change. You say you saw the bins, then the entrance. You cannot provide the event between them. He writes that down without drawing an arrow.

- **“Say you believe the day has returned, and ask them to help you find something your memory can predict.”**

  Simon asks what you expect to happen next. You almost tell him the thing he said on the first occasion. Then you remember saying Blue, or holding the entrance open, and the reply that followed it. You have already interfered with what you were going to use as a test. You ask for something you have not yet changed.

Both routes preserve a current statement of Blaise's recollection, not an accepted deduction that objective time has reversed. Any exact prediction must be earned from the earlier encountered route and lodged before its outcome; no fixed message oracle is assumed after changed outgoing communication.

## Ada has not touched your wrist

Entry: after that first account. Ada is still by the bucket. She has not had Sunday's chair conversation in the currently represented occasion. The earlier memory source remains visible as a remembered event, not as her current knowledge.

### Passage

Ada brings the saucepan to the sink. You follow her. She looks over her shoulder as you enter the workshop.

“Do you need something?”

There is the breath, the slight catch before the words. You have heard it often enough to be ashamed of wanting it to prove anything.

You ask her to say your name.

“Blaise.”

You hear it once, spoken by her. You had expected something to fit when she said it. Instead you notice how tired she sounds.

“Again?”

“No. What is it?”

You take a chair from the table and set it in the space behind your legs. Ada looks at the chair. You sit, then begin to stand, trying to catch the same edge behind your knee.

“What are you doing?”

“Just a moment.”

You rise slowly. The chair shifts. You wait for her hand.

She catches the back of the chair with both hands and moves it away from you.

“It's got a loose leg,” she says. “Use the other one.”

Your wrist hangs in the space where her hand had been. You can remember its exact warmth. At least, you thought you could until you looked at the empty place.

“I wasn't trying to fall.”

“Looked like a useful start.”

You laugh. It comes out as a short breath through your nose. Ada does not.

### Choice B-R03: what you ask of this Ada

- **“Tell her about the touch and the words ‘There you are,’ and ask whether she remembers either.”**

  “Have I said that to you before?” she asks.

  “Yes.”

  “Before today?”

  You cannot answer without choosing which kind of before she means. You tell her about the chairs by the water, the potatoes and the seventh key. She puts the saucepan under the tap. The water begins hitting its bottom, loud enough that you have to raise your voice.

  She turns the tap off.

  “I believe you remember it,” she says. “I don't. Don't make me remember by doing that with the chair.”

- **“Tell her you wanted the touch to happen again, even if she had no memory of the first.”**

  “You could ask me to hold your hand,” she says.

  You look at her hand on the saucepan. “Would you?”

  “If that's what you need.”

  She holds it out. You take it. It is cooler than you remember.

  You wait for the rightness you wanted. Her thumb moves once against yours. You can feel that she is waiting too.

- **“Say you are frightened that the person you remember has gone, and you cannot tell whether Ada is her.”**

  She sits on the chair with the good leg. For a moment you think she is going to ask what would prove it.

  “I've been here all afternoon,” she says.

  “That isn't what I mean.”

  “It's what I can tell you.”

  She places the saucepan between her feet. “What did the other one want?”

  You say she wanted to put the potatoes away.

  “Sounds like me.”

### Common passage

In the doorway, Simon says he has found a spare camera. He can leave it looking at the service passage if you want. Ada asks what it will look at when you are in here. He says nothing, then says that is part of the point.

You picture the side yard, the raised bin lid, the parcel hiding the glass. If you watch a record of yourself going through that door, there will be a man whose face other people recognize as yours. You will still have to say whether what happened to him happened to you.

“Will you come?” you ask Ada.

“To the door?”

You nod.

“I'll stand where you can see me. I haven't said I'm going through it.”

She gets up and places the faulty chair against the wall, back outward, so nobody can sit on it without moving it first.

Editorial consequences: replayed gesture does not summon a fixed response. A requested present touch may be granted without recovering the first relationship. Ada's refusal to repeat a name is practical, not a metaphysical verdict. A companion is offered only up to the door, so this staging does not decide an unperformed simultaneous-crossing trial. Free action is explored through anticipation and intervention, while changed memory remains a changed condition rather than a proof against determinism.
