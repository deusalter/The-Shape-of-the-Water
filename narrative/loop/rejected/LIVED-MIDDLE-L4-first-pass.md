## L4-M01. The dry side

Audience at entry: Blaise and the interior Miriam. Ada brings the sound chair and then returns to the workshop; she hears the first request about its position, not the later private exchange. Simon is in the workshop. Exterior Miriam is near the front with her folded coat, where she cannot hear ordinary speech across the water. No camera or recorder is running.

Miriam's immediate want: spend a few minutes beside the water without answering comparisons about herself. She also wants access to her keys. Her recent confinement remains recent to her; the scene does not make her repeat it for a therapeutic experiment.

### Passage

Miriam asks Ada to put the chair at the shallow end, where the tiles have dried. Ada puts it beside the rail.

“Farther along,” Miriam says.

“Then you won't have the rail.”

“I don't need to get in.”

Ada moves it. Miriam asks for another inch and then apologizes, because the chair is already where she wanted it. Ada sits on it briefly to make sure it does not rock. She gets up and brushes the seat with her palm.

“If he starts moving it again, tell me.”

You say you have understood about the loose leg. Ada looks at you long enough to make clear that she was speaking to Miriam, then goes back to the workshop.

Miriam sits. The water is below her knees, beyond the edge of the tiles. She puts her good hand against the side of the chair and leans forward. From where you stand, it looks as though she means to rise. You reach for the chair.

“Leave it.”

You let go.

She watches a small hair lying on the surface of the water. It moves toward the lip, stops, and begins moving along it. She asks you whether you can see it.

You say yes.

“I keep thinking it's one of mine. As though that would be a reason to get it.”

It is too far from either of you to reach without getting wet. You watch it pass the next tile.

“It probably came off the brush we use for the floor,” she says.

You ask whether that makes it better.

“No. Now I'm thinking of the floor.”

She looks up at you. The small smile stays only until she notices that you are waiting for something else.

“You can sit down too.”

You sit on the low bench behind the chair. Her coat remains on its hook. Her shoes are under the spectators' bench, one loose insole covering the keys she put there before the accident. You know the location only if you have already encountered it; the scene does not make the insole transparent.
