# Two constructions of the central exchange

Provisional writing experiments, 2026-10-03. The selected version is the actual `optics` scene in `src/content/case.json`, written after the ending audition. The rejected version below is not a prior event, alternate world state, hidden memory or playable branch. No owner approval is claimed.

## Version 1, rejected: demand a definition before allowing a trial

Simon puts the camera on the bench but keeps one finger over its switch.

“Say what would count,” he tells Miriam. “Before I show it to you. Otherwise you can always make the sentence smaller afterward.”

Ada has her coat on. She has folded it at the collar so that the damp part does not touch her neck. You have asked her to wait, and she has agreed to wait until the clock above the door reaches the number six.

Miriam looks from the camera to the clock. “Count as what?”

“As something there independently of anyone seeing it.”

“I will grant you the bath.”

“You know what I mean.”

“No. I know one thing you might mean, which is perfectly reasonable. The camera can record while we go out for a cigarette. I don't smoke, but I'll go with you. When we come back there will be an image. Have you proved the image isn't mine? Certainly. Have you proved what a world independent of every possible condition of knowing would be? You haven't even told me what such a proof could look like.”

Simon presses down on the switch without turning it on. “You've made the second question impossible by saying it that way.”

“That may be something to learn about the question.”

You ask whether he is willing to demonstrate the smaller claim.

“Is there a smaller Miriam?” he asks. “One we can observe making her claims, without having to agree that her way of understanding is part of God?”

Ada checks the clock. It has not reached the next number.

Miriam says there is no need to agree with her. Simon asks why she volunteered if she had arrived already certain what the experiment could never show.

“Because I wanted to see it.”

He takes his finger off the switch. He seems to have been expecting something longer.

“I still do,” she says. “But I would like the door off first.”

Ada unbuttons her coat. There is an awkward pause while she thinks of somewhere dry to put it. Simon holds out a chair. She lays it over the back and tests whether the chair will fall. Then she fetches the screwdriver.

You stand at the hinge side. Simon stands at the far edge. Miriam puts a folded towel under the lower corner of the door. As each screw comes free, Ada gives it to you. You hold them in a hand that has begun to sweat. When the last screw is out, Simon is surprised by the weight. He says your name sharply and you take more of it.

The door is laid flat. The image of the high window slides across one surviving piece of glass. Ada puts a towel over it. The camera is still waiting on the bench.

“Now,” Miriam says.

Simon switches it on. The black field appears, with its fine line. He turns the screen toward you. For a moment none of them speaks. You shift the camera. The line changes position within the frame, but persists. Ada covers the slit with the cuff of her coat and it disappears.

“You've covered it,” Simon says.

She removes her arm. It returns.

“You asked what would count.” Miriam has begun to speak before he has finished adjusting the camera. “I think that counts as finding something.”

He switches it off.

“What?”

“A relation. A reproducible one.”

“And you call that smaller.”

“I didn't.”

“You let him call it smaller.” He means you.

You start to say that this is not an argument about the value of his work. He asks what else the four of you have been discussing since you arrived. Ada puts the screws in a jam jar. She screws the lid on, then unscrews it so that the damp screws can dry.

Miriam asks for the camera. Simon gives it to her. She switches it on again, lowers her eye to the screen and asks him to show her where to hold it. He does so from the other end of the bench, avoiding her bandaged hand. Ada puts on her coat. The clock has passed the six.

## Decision against version 1

This version changes the construction substantially: Simon makes advance agreement a condition of access, Miriam treats the question as a problem before the experiment, Ada's threatened departure creates a deadline, and dismantling precedes observation. It is not a synonym pass over the selected scene.

Reject this version for the present build. Miriam's opening wins too cheaply by controlling the terms; Simon's rightful objection to a shrinking claim becomes mostly injured status. The player arbitrates their manners while receiving too little say in the experiment. Ada's clock supplies pressure but turns the shared task into another favor extracted from her. The coat control is physically and emotionally useful, but the line “a smaller Miriam” makes Simon too readily an epigrammatist.

## Version 2, selected: build an open model before disputing what it establishes

The current `optics` scene shares work before argument. Ada supplies a tray and card, Simon supplies a smaller mirror and camera, Miriam can watch without enclosure, and the player asks for a change of condition. The repeated stripe is a joint fact before any participant gets to settle its scope. Simon's independent-reality challenge then receives a fair answer, followed by his challenge to Miriam's own positive account. Her substance premise is incomplete but actually defended; it is not a last-minute speech of consolation. The camera cannot prove either position.

This version was materially revised after philosophical criticism: the lead distinguished the original closed experience from the dimmer open model, let Simon retain his realist objection after his safety failure, and added a premise and objection about self-existing nature. Current prose, its hash and exact successful voice anchors are in VOICE.md and the revision log. It remains a dense scene requiring a human-paced reading.
