## C1-S01: The next morning

Entry: after either first-night ending. The player's reason to return is ownership, transport and repayment, not an invented promise to help. Retain `public_correction`, `private_finding`, `shamed_simon`, `pressed_miriam`, `future_help` independently.

### Passage

The bath opens at nine. At ten past, Ada is still outside with a bottle of milk wedged between her arm and her side. She has got the outer door open but not the inner one. The key turns without moving the lock.

“Don't do anything interesting,” she tells it.

You take the milk. She lifts the door by its handle and turns the key again. This time something scrapes and gives. Inside, the place smells more strongly of yesterday's water than it did when you were standing beside it. The windows are covered with separate drops. You can see the laundry through the clear intervals.

The mirror is where you left it, wrapped on the two benches. Overnight, water from a towel has spread a dark shape across the brown paper. Simon has put another sheet beneath it. He has written your name correctly on that one.

A man has come to remove the old coin box from the lockers. Ada sees him through the glass and holds up one finger. He nods, sits on the low wall and takes a sandwich from his pocket. He has the look of somebody willing to finish a previous breakfast while waiting to begin work.

“It's not ten minutes,” Ada says. “People think one finger means ten minutes. It means wait.”

She takes the milk from you and sets it on a chair. On a sheet beside the mirror she has drawn three rectangles. In each, a dark line shows a possible cut. One would preserve enough glass for a narrow standing mirror. Another would leave two pieces broad enough to be useful over basins. The third leaves very little.

“Those are suggestions,” she says. “I haven't cut anything. He asked me to see what might come out.”

Simon is in the gallery, holding one of the folded chairs at the level of his face. He puts it down when he hears you. His first question is whether the borrowed mirror had a maker's name on the back. You say you never looked.

“Then we should look before somebody says it's irreplaceable,” he says. “I can replace a thing. I can't replace an adjective.”

Ada opens the cupboard under the sink. “He's been here since seven.”

“I couldn't sleep.”

“He could have slept until eight. I still wouldn't have been here.”

There is no expensive mystery on the back of the glass. Most of the original silver has been protected by a dull coat of paint, interrupted at the old damaged corner. The surviving frame has darkened on the side that used to face the wall in your room. You know it better from that side than you expected to.

Simon has brought a folded blanket for your car. You remind him you came on the bus.

“Yes. Sorry. I thought, once we'd sorted out what could be carried...” He looks at the longest piece. “I have a van on Tuesday. A borrowed van. We could wrap it properly today.”

Tuesday is possible for you. It is also possible to ask him to deliver whatever can be saved, rather than return once more to supervise your own loss. He takes out his phone to enter the arrangement. Its screen is open on the visitors' group.

#### If the full correction was public

A woman has replied beneath his correction: “Thank you for explaining. Will there still be something to look at?” Another has sent a photograph of a packet of work gloves. Simon scrolls past the gloves quickly enough that you can tell he has looked at them before.

“A few people still want to come,” he says. “I said the door is off.”

Ada asks whether he also said the pool is open. He has. She nods and goes back to the cupboard.

#### If the finding stayed private

The visitors' group shows a cancellation and five questions. One asks whether Ada is all right. Another asks why she destroyed the demonstration. Ada points at that one with the unopened milk.

“I sent them the account,” she says. “Those two. One of them put it in here.”

Simon says he is trying to get to the question before she answers it again.

“You've had the morning,” she says.

You can see the text Ada forwarded. It is the sequence you gave the four people in the room. Her note above it is short: “I cut it because she asked to get out.”

### Return to common passage

The milk has begun to drip from the dented cap. You put it in the sink. Simon stops using his phone to take a cloth from Ada. For a little while the three of you deal with something that doesn't need a written account afterward.

When the chair is dry, he says that his sister is coming. She has helped him with the invitation. She still has the picture in a form that will print clearly, which several people have requested. He would like to use one of the saved pieces of mirror in the open model.

“The small one works,” Ada says.

“The field is narrow.”

“It works.”

He says he knows. He has asked because the glass belongs to you.

### Decision C1-D01

- **“Arrange Tuesday delivery. Decide about another loan after seeing the new use.”**

  Simon enters the address you give him. He reads it back, including the part he already knows. Ada tells him she will find cardboard corners. You have agreed to a delivery, not a second loan. He draws a line below the address and leaves the space beneath it empty.

- **“Ask him to deliver the saved glass; decline to lend any of it again.”**

  “All right,” he says. Then: “I mean it. The small mirror works.” He crosses out a dimension on his sketch and writes a smaller one. Ada looks over his shoulder. She says the narrower frame will fit on a table without someone holding it. He has to turn the sketch before he can see this. The inquiry can continue; the larger piece will be packed for you.

Exit: the day's arrangement is clear. Refusal alters apparatus size and who does the carrying, never factual truth or access to the later inquiry.

## C1-S02: Beneath the wrapping

Entry: before choosing any new use of the glass. This is examination of the loan and its prior damage, not reopening who cut the cord. A photograph of Monday's loan must be obtained through an explicit request. No pre-existing crack is invented to excuse the binding.

### Passage

The cloth covering the glass has small loops that catch on every sharp edge. Ada suggests leaving it in place and putting paper over the top. Simon wants to examine the corners first. The man waiting outside has finished his sandwich and has opened the newspaper it was wrapped in.

“That's my quarter-hour,” Ada says. “You two can look. Don't slide anything across anything else.”

She puts a box on the floor for the small fragments and goes to the lockers. The man asks about the key. She begins telling him about the wrong door and stops when she realizes he means the coin-box key. Through the open arch you hear both of them laugh, briefly and without satisfaction.

Simon unwraps the damaged corner that was there before he borrowed the mirror. The glass at that corner is intact. What is missing is the dark backing: an irregular patch through which a pale blur of the towel shows. A crescent of silver remains around it. You recognize the patch. There is an unpleasant relief in recognizing precisely what you thought you remembered.

“I said it wouldn't be in the view,” he says.

“It wasn't a crack.”

“No.”

You ask whether the glass could nevertheless have been weak somewhere else. He considers this while holding his hands very still.

“It could have. I don't know how to tell you it wasn't. But it didn't break across this corner.”

The broad line of the new break runs in from the side that struck the pier. Another runs downward from it. You can follow them through the separate pieces without touching their edges. The old patch does not meet either line. Simon lays a ruler on the wrapping beside it, leaving the ruler well clear of the glass.

“Someone may tell you an old mirror was bound to go. Someone who wants to sell you a new one. I wouldn't know what their test was.”

You ask why he has raised the question now.

“Because I have to work out what I owe you.”

“And the answer would change it?”

He looks toward the lockers. Ada has both hands inside a metal box.

“I don't want to pay for a perfect new thing if I borrowed an old damaged one. I also don't want that sentence to sound like I think you did this. Those are both true.”

You stand on opposite sides of the benches, looking at the evidence of a price that hasn't yet been agreed. Last night he offered instalments. This morning an instalment needs an amount.

He has photographed the mirror in your room before they moved it. In the photograph, your reflection is a dark shape near one edge. The old damaged corner shows the wallpaper behind it. A shoe on the floor has kept one end of the frame from slipping while Simon tightened a moving strap. The shoe is yours. You remember being annoyed by how useful it was.

He offers to give you the original picture. You can accept it without asking him to publish it to anyone. It contains part of your room, which has nothing to do with the experiment.

On the back of the surviving frame a pencil line crosses one of the old screw holes. It was there before this loan; Simon's photograph shows it if you enlarge the lower edge. He has added a second line in blue pencil for the new mounting. The two lines are close enough that you could easily mistake one for the other in a smaller reproduction.

“Which line did Ada see?” you ask.

“She saw the plan.”

“Did she see this?”

“Not before I put it on. I don't think so.”

This is a different question from whether she drew the opening arc. When she comes back, she confirms that she drew the arc while the frame was empty. She was checking whether the open door would meet the pier. The mirror's upper frame, and the height at which it would strike, were Simon's next job.

“Then why did you let him put it there?” you ask.

“I didn't let him. I told him to check it.”

“You were responsible for the room.”

“I was. I ought to have come and looked. I didn't. That's the part you can have from me.”

Simon says he had taken the chalk line as agreement. Ada has to go back to the lockers before she can answer. The man is holding up a strip of loose metal and asking whether it should have been attached to anything.

When she returns, she does answer.

“I drew you the problem,” she says. “You thought I'd drawn you the permission. I can see that now. It isn't what I thought I was doing.”

There is no last drawing which contains every change made after it. The existing plan names a check. The installed door and the recording show what was actually built. You can establish the mismatch without deciding that a person's remembered intention is the plan itself.

Simon asks whether you want him to replace the frame as well as the glass. Ada says the frame can still be useful if somebody shortens it. He asks whom she means. She says she is not volunteering before he has decided what he is offering.

### Decision C1-D02: keeping and using the loan photograph

- **“Keep the original photograph privately to document the mirror's earlier condition.”**

  Simon transfers the file to your notebook. Ada looks away while the picture of your room is enlarged. You record the old loss of silver, the intact corner and the visible mounting line. The rest of the room stays out of the account. Simon asks you to tell him if the original fails to arrive; a thumbnail would be no use for the comparison you have just made.

- **“Ask him to show only the damaged corner to Ada, then keep that comparison.”**

  He crops a separate copy while you watch. Ada checks the original on his phone before looking at the crop. “That bit, yes,” she says. “Don't make it look as if that was the whole edge.” He leaves enough surrounding frame to show where the patch sits. The comparison is useful because you know what has been excluded.

### Optional evidence submission at this stage

Claim: **“The old damage caused last night's break.”**

If submitted using only the old-corner picture and the later broken mirror, Simon asks you to put them next to each other. “Here? That corner is still together.” Ada points to the new impact edge. “You can say it was damaged before. We all knew that. You haven't shown that damage making this break.” The supported account of the impact remains available; the submission does not erase the earlier picture or exclude another test.

Claim: **“Ada approved the completed mirrored door.”**

If submitted using the chalk arc or plan alone, Ada lays her hand on the plan without covering the note. “That tells you to check it. Where is the check?” Simon admits the completed mounting came afterward. You can retain the weaker conclusion that she knew a door was being built and failed to revisit the installed version. It is not the same claim.

Exit: new observations concern the loaned mirror's prior appearance and the plan/installation sequence. The established rescue and impact are unchanged. No unsupported material-fatigue finding is awarded.

## C1-S03: Emmy has brought the wrong shoes

Entry: Emmy arrives to collect/meet her son after football and see her brother. She knows the invitation she helped prepare. Her knowledge of last night's event depends on actual public messages or explicit disclosure now. Her son receives no private account by default.

### Passage

The woman at the entrance has a pair of football boots hooked over two fingers. The boy behind her is wearing another pair. Both pairs are muddy.

“They aren't interchangeable,” she tells him. “We've established that. You can stop explaining.”

He begins explaining which sole is for which ground. She listens as far as the word ground, points to the mat and waits. He wipes one foot, then the other, with great care taken over how much he is being made to do.

Simon comes down the gallery stairs too quickly. At the bottom he slows, as if he could revise the whole descent by attending to the last two steps.

“Emmy.”

“I've brought these. They were in the back.”

“They're not mine.”

“I know they're not yours. Find somewhere for them.”

He takes the boots. She kisses his cheek while his hands are occupied, then asks where the kettle is. He points toward the workshop. She heads there without waiting to be introduced. Halfway across the bath she notices the cabinet's detached door on the benches and stops.

“That really is a lot of glass,” she says.

Simon introduces you as the person who lent it. Emmy offers her empty hand and then looks at the dirt left on it by the laces. You take it anyway. She rubs her palm on the side of her coat afterward, more thoroughly than she had before.

#### If she received the public correction

“I read what you sent,” she says to Simon. “All of it, this time. Is Miriam here?”

He says she is expected. Emmy asks Ada whether the hand needs another cold pack. Ada says it needs Miriam to stop using it. Emmy puts a small insulated lunch bag on the bench and says it can be borrowed until the boy gets hungry.

She has read the cut and the request in their stated order. She has not watched the film or heard the private explanations. When her son asks what happened, she tells him a door couldn't open and someone had to cut a cord. She does not supply an intention she has not been told.

#### If she saw only the cancellation

“I got the picture and then the cancellation,” she tells him. “Did somebody try to stop it?”

Ada is standing beside you. She does not answer for him.

“There was a fault in the exit,” Simon says.

“What sort of fault?”

He looks at you, then at the boy, who has approached the largest wrapped piece and is trying to see his face in its exposed corner.

“Come away from that,” Emmy says. He comes away a little. She waits until he has done the rest. “You can tell me. He doesn't have to hear it.”

### Decision C1-D03: what Emmy is told

- **“Tell Emmy the established binding, request, cut and impact, while her son waits with Ada.”**

  Ada offers the boy a soft ball and points to an empty corner where there is nothing to break. He asks whether he can throw it. “At that wall. Not at me.” Emmy listens to the short sequence. When you have finished, she turns to Simon. “Then your first message needs a second message.” He says the cancellation was one. “A different one,” she says. She now knows the account because you have given it to her, not because she entered the room.

- **“Ask Simon to explain the safety failure to his sister himself, without using private conversations.”**

  You say that Miriam's personal history is not part of the explanation. Emmy nods as if this were ordinary. Simon tells her about the extra cord, the inside ring and his answer to the first request. He leaves Ada's cut until the end. She asks him when it happened in relation to the request. He puts it back in its place. When he has finished, she thanks you for standing there, which he finds difficult to look at.

### Common passage

Emmy follows the sound of the kettle into the workshop. She lays out four mugs and then asks how many people actually want tea. Ada says this is a promising change from her brother's approach to numbers. Simon asks what that means. She tells him he brought twenty-four chairs for twelve visitors and one person with a camera.

“People might bring someone.”

“Then they'd have someone to talk to while they waited for a chair.”

Emmy finds the tea bags in a biscuit tin. There are biscuits in the tin marked first aid. She opens that one too and takes the unbroken half of a ginger biscuit. She does not draw attention to the arrangement.

Her brother is still trying to offer her a view of the model from the doorway.

“I've only just arrived,” she says. “Let me have one thing after another.”

This makes him quiet in a way the evidence did not. She sits, touches the table to see whether it is wet, and asks Ada about the choir. She remembers a Christmas concert at which somebody came in one verse late and then continued at the wrong place all the way to the end. Ada remembers who. Emmy tells her not to say. They say the name at the same time, too quietly for you to make it out.

The boy comes in to report that the ball has gone behind the folded mats. Ada gives him a long-handled brush and follows him out. Emmy watches them go.

“He wanted to come,” she says to Simon. “I didn't make him.”

“I know.”

“Do you? Because you have the same face you had when you made those paper theatres. As if we were all going to stand up and say you'd used too much glue.”

“I did use too much glue.”

“You used whatever was there. We spent a month finding bits of the old one on the floor.”

He starts to apologize for asking her to do the invitation. She stops him with the packet of biscuits.

“I said I would. I meant it. I like making something people can read. At work I spend half my day explaining a text message that should have had one more sentence in it.”

You ask where she works. A dental practice, she says. She deals with appointments, receipts and the people who have been given one piece of information by somebody who has forgotten the other piece. She likes the job when the missing sentence can be found.

“The invitation wasn't supposed to be a test,” Simon says.

“No. It was supposed to get people here.”

She looks at the tall window, then at the little camera on the bench. Her expression changes when she notices the dark cloth behind it. You ask whether she has seen the apparatus before.

“In pictures. He sent quite a lot.”

“I sent four.”

“Four pictures and all the versions of four pictures. I made a folder. Otherwise they turn up next to photographs of somebody's molar.”

The folder is on the old laptop she has brought in her bag. She also has the invitation as it went to the printer. Simon asks her to wait before opening it. She asks what she is waiting for. He has no answer that isn't the answer you are all trying to obtain.

### Decision C1-D04

If I don't remember, the files may.” Simon remains standing. He has to decide where to put his hands while she starts.

- **“Ask first what her finished invitation promised a visitor.”**

  Emmy takes a folded paper from a sleeve in the laptop case. “This is the one that went out. Start with what they got.” She gives it to you rather than to her brother. He asks whether there is any point when they will get to the apparatus. She says she imagines there is, after they know what the picture was being used to say.

Exit: both routes reach the actual invitation and preserve access to source files. They differ in who initially controls the description. There is no need to confiscate the laptop or assume a deceit.

## C1-S04: Ruth's print

Entry: Ruth has the public invitation, not the author's underlying history. She has a beginner swimming lesson booked with Miriam. Her attendance remains worthwhile if the art trial is canceled. A later conversation can address whether she received a correction; do not grant her that knowledge automatically.

### Passage

Ruth Garth has put the invitation in a transparent sleeve. The sleeve is almost as old as the bath's admission prices. There are punctures where it has been pinned to something and a white patch where a label was peeled away.

“I brought it in case you said I was on the wrong day,” she tells Ada.

“You're on the right day for the pool.”

“For this as well.” She taps the picture without taking it out.

The print shows a bright, level line in black. Below it is Simon's name, the bath's name, a time, and the sentence: “The same horizon, wherever you stand.” Ruth has underlined the last three words in blue. She says she cannot stand for long. She assumed a chair would not interfere with the thing the picture promised.

Simon says he has plenty of chairs. Emmy looks at him over the top of her laptop.

Ruth has a broad canvas bag containing a towel, a swimming costume and a paperback with a paper cover folded around it. She takes out the towel to reach the paper, then puts the towel back because it is the paper she wanted. No one helps until she asks you to hold the bag open. The sleeve has stuck to the dry fabric on its way down.

Miriam arrives while you are doing this. Her hand is no longer wrapped around peas. Two fingers are loosely taped together. She has a spare sweatshirt over one arm. She hangs it on the nearest chair before Ruth can offer to take it.

Ruth sees the tape and asks if the lesson is off.

“Not if you are content to stay where we can both stand,” Miriam says. “I can't catch you with that hand.”

“I wasn't asking you to catch me.”

“I know. I need to say what I can do.”

Ruth thinks about this and agrees. She can wait until the others have finished with the picture. She would like to know whether there is something to see before she takes off her glasses. Her prescription is expensive enough that she does not trust the little shelf in the changing cubicle.

“You'll be able to see a line,” Simon says.

“Like that one?”

“A line like that one.”

Ruth hears the extra words. She gives him the sleeve.

Emmy has a copy of the same invitation on her screen. The letters and the folded paper agree. Simon says the sentence was an early version. His sister says it was the last version he sent her before she made the layout. They are both speaking mildly. The effort is visible.

“I said I wanted to try it from different positions,” he says.

“You wrote ‘wherever you stand.’”

“I was talking about the plan.”

“Then you needed to tell me that bit was the plan.”

She turns the laptop so you can see the message in the file folder. The sentence is there, before the layout existed. It is not part of a statement from Miriam. It is Simon's own proposed description. He reads it, sitting down at last.

The line in the picture is very straight. A small pale notch interrupts it near its left end. You saw that notch in the early clip, where the slit had a ragged edge. Around the line in the invitation there is nothing you can identify as the rim of a tray, an edge of mirror, a wall or a hand. You do not yet know whether that absence came from the apparatus, the camera, or the preparation of the page.

Ruth asks whether the paper is worth keeping. “I can leave it with you. I've got the date written down.”

You say that the paper matters because it is what a visitor received. Simon says nobody is disputing that. He is disputing what it means to call the picture wrong.

“I haven't called it wrong,” you say.

“No. But we have got there surprisingly quickly without anyone having looked.”

Miriam takes a chair and puts it close to the open model, at a position from which the camera currently looks. She asks Ruth whether that is a comfortable distance from the table. Ruth sits, adjusts the chair once, and says it is. Simon crouches beside her without being asked.

She looks toward the mirror. “I can see the window.”

“Below it.”

“A bit of the window.”

He asks her to lean left. She leans. The line appears, narrow and bright, interrupted by the ragged notch. She draws back. It disappears behind the edge of the opening through which she has been looking.

“There,” she says. “That time.”

Simon begins moving the chair. Miriam puts her foot against one of its legs.

“Ask her first.”

Ruth says he can move it, provided he tells her where it is going. He moves it three inches to the left. From there she can see the line without leaning. She watches it for a while.

“It's pleasant,” she says. “I thought it would be bigger.”

Nobody seems ready for pleasant. Simon thanks her. Emmy writes something on the back of the invitation and shows it to her: “A line of reflected light. Viewing position can be adjusted.” Ruth says that would have got her there too, if they had also mentioned the chair.

Simon asks his sister not to rewrite his work in front of him. She turns the paper over.

### Decision C1-D05: the question for the inquiry

- **“Ask whether the apparatus changed, or the invitation concealed its viewing conditions.”**

  Simon says both are possible. The broken large mirror has been replaced by a smaller one; the opening is narrower. Emmy says the picture was also prepared for printing. Ada brings the large plan and leaves it within reach. You have two changes to distinguish, not a single accusation to prove.

- **“Ask them to specify what ‘the same horizon’ would mean in a test.”**

  Ruth stands to get her bag. “It would mean I knew where to put the chair.” Simon says he meant the line's position, independent of his own presence. Miriam asks: position in the picture, position relative to the bath, or the distance it seems to have? He starts with the picture, changes his mind, and asks for a pencil. All three proposed senses remain written down.

Exit: question can be pursued through the source/provenance route or the open-trial route. The invitation is evidence of a published offer; it is not evidence that every visitor could see the effect from every place, nor that the effect was fabricated.

## C1-S05: The first comparison fails

### Passage

Ada has found two pieces of card large enough to make a background. One has an advertisement for swimming goggles on the other side. The smiling face on it is folded precisely through the nose. She puts the plain side toward the model and fixes it with clothes pegs.

“That's the black,” she says.

  Emmy holds the card against the wall, with an arrow she has drawn pointing up. You turn the camera a little clockwise. In the picture, both arrow and line tilt the other way. ### Common passage after either first control

There is still enough daylight to repeat the other change, if you want it. Ada wants to know whether the water can be emptied before she takes the tray back. Simon asks for another ten minutes. She says she meant now or later, not a prediction.

Emmy places the invitation beside the phone. She has been looking at the position of the ragged notch. She says it is the same notch, but that she cannot identify a single frame from it.
