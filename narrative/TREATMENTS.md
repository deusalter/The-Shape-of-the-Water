# Two treatments, with performed auditions

## A. The Shape of the Water

A single evening at an indoor saltwater bath, closing for winter. You have come to collect a tall mirror lent to Simon Vane for his private trial of a viewing cabinet. It is broken. Ada March admits cutting the cord around the cabinet while Miriam Verney was inside. Simon calls this sabotage; Ada calls it getting somebody out. An appeal to tomorrow's visitors is already drafted on Simon's phone. You can reconstruct a small causal sequence, choose what to tell those visitors and determine whether any of the three will do something together afterward.

Miriam wants to go back to teaching beginners without having the incident converted into her spiritual testimony. Simon wants someone else to see what he saw, and wants the work to remain worth doing after its extravagant claim fails. Ada wants the door made safe and to finish an overdue meal. She also wants Miriam, her former lover, to ask her for something without pretending it is a favor to Ada.

The author's proposed history: the cabinet produces a real reflected stripe, repeatable without a human inside. Simon takes this persistence to warrant a view independent of the conditions of human experience. Miriam disputes his inference but volunteered because she recognizes a demand to understand the world one actually inhabits. To stop light at the door seam, Simon tied an external cord around the cabinet. The inside release still moved the latch, but the cord held the door shut. When Miriam asked to leave, Ada cut the cord. Miriam pushed the freed door into a nearby concrete pier. The mirror shattered. Nobody intended the injury or the break. Simon's improvised blackout made the intended exit impossible.

The player need not determine whether Miriam was having a panic attack or a medical event. A person asking to leave was prevented from leaving; the mechanism and sequence can be established. The return to the cabinet changes what a freely moving release handle means. The original description remains in the transcript.

Metaphysical pressure develops through three distinct encounters: public reproducibility versus the claim to a view outside all conditions; a practical explanation whose completion leaves the demand for a complete explanation of reality open; and Miriam's difficult claim that understanding finite things can deepen understanding of an immanent whole, without a cosmic person or a promise that her dead mother survives. Conduct after the disclosure supplies a further question, not a proof of a doctrine.

### A audition: the first examination

Simon has stood the largest piece of your mirror against a swimming float. The float is pink and shaped like a rabbit. Its ears appear on either side of your reflection.

"It was a clean break," he says.

Ada is eating from a saucepan. "It was about a hundred breaks."

"I meant morally."

She looks into the pan. "Oh."

Miriam sits on the lowest spectators' bench, with a bag of frozen peas folded over her knuckles. A pea escapes through a small hole and rolls down the step. She watches it go. None of them picks it up.

The cabinet stands beside the shallow end. Its black cloth skin is buttoned to a timber frame. At the door, a brass ring hangs from a short cable. Ada points with her spoon.

"You pull that from inside. Try it. Outside, please. Don't go in."

You pull. Something clicks. The door moves a little in its damaged frame.

"Working," Simon says. He says it quickly enough to sound relieved by the discovery.

Miriam takes the peas off her hand. "Yes. That is exactly what I heard."

"You said it hadn't opened."

"It clicked. I could hear perfectly well."

You can ask Simon what held the door after the click, ask Miriam what she did next, or examine the two cut ends of black cord. The scene's difference is between a latch releasing and a person being able to leave. The language should not announce that difference before the player has made it.

### Editorial judgment

Select A. A fixed place lets an unchanged object and exact remembered wording acquire another use. The player has a modest material stake before being asked to hear metaphysics. The risk is a machine built too neatly to exemplify an argument. Countermeasure: keep the actual mechanism simple, let the luminous effect survive investigation, and permit the characters to pursue concerns that the mechanism cannot organize for them. Nobody needs to be a disguised philosopher.

## B. The Voice Carried Home

A moving escort inquiry across one night: a repairer asks the player to carry a sealed portable loudspeaker from a shuttered cinema to a woman dying at home. It has spoken one new sentence in the voice of her long-dead son. The player encounters people sequentially while carrying an object they cannot responsibly destroy or promise to explain. Investigation changes the route and the use of remaining battery. The final encounter takes place at the bedside whether or not the mechanism is solved. Unlike A, locations cannot all be revisited; absence, opportunity cost and an approaching arrival supply the construction.

The repairer wants the machine preserved and a fraud accusation withdrawn. The mother wants to hear the ordinary recording again and fears everyone's curiosity will use up its battery. The dead son's former bandmate wants a private rehearsal tape kept private. Proposed history: a shared wireless receiver picks up an unsorted recording stored on an old phone. The apparently new sentence was an unfinished rehearsal, not a message. A causal solution can identify the recording without establishing what a finite life is or whether understanding it is exhausted by its termination.

The intended metaphysical pressure would be about individuality across changing constituents, eternity versus duration, and whether a recording counts as survival. The extraordinary claim would never be established by authorial fiat. The same recording could be offered honestly as a recording at the end.

### B audition: the bakery stop

The voice comes out while you are paying for bread.

"Leave it, Ma. I'll get the next one."

The baker gives you your change in two handfuls. She has counted it wrong. You wait until she notices.

"Is that on a loop?"

You say you have heard it once before.

"Well. He's still waiting for the next one."

The machine has three buttons. Their arrows have rubbed away. The repairer has covered the middle button with a square of tape and written KEEP on it. The baker sets down the tongs.

"They used to come for six rolls. She would ask him to carry them. He'd say he was carrying his guitar. There's your second thing about him."

She wraps a seventh roll separately and pushes it across. You say you did not order it.

"For his mother. You can't turn up with a voice and nothing to eat."

From the machine comes a scrape, then a long open vowel. The baker's face changes before the vowel becomes a yawn.

"I thought it was going to sing."

You can ask to hear the saved recording, conserve the battery for the mother, or ask where the band kept its rehearsal tapes. Each means spending some part of this stop that another action would have used. The baker goes back to putting cakes into a box. She leaves out the one she has dented with the tongs and eats it standing up.

### Rejection retained

Do not expand B in this revision. The most economical causal solution would turn a strongly charged surviving-voice premise into a wireless explanation. Much of the surviving inquiry would then depend on grief, and the dying mother risks becoming a moral test supplied to certify the player's restraint. A has more room for the independent pleasure and irritation of living together, a stronger repeatable inquiry into objective experience, and revisits that are genuinely playable under the current engine. B is rejected for this build, not pronounced artistically impossible. No material from B enters the selected case.
