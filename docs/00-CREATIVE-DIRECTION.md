# Creative direction
## Literary-first revision, 3 October 2026

This document controls the project's artistic priorities. It replaces the earlier registry-driven case, fixed inventory of 24 clues and 12 deductions, and the idea that a large authoring studio is an equal first-release product. Those are not constraints to preserve or rename.

The working project name remains **The City That Remembers Wrong**. It is a placeholder, not a demand for a supernatural city, literal memory erasure, or a final title. The project is separate from Hêrte. Do not borrow Hêrte's cosmology, characters, symbols, or prose without an explicit request.

## 1. What the owner is asking for

Attempt an original literary game of exceptional quality. Disco Elysium is an ambition and experience reference, not a text to imitate, a setting to reskin, or a checklist of features to reproduce. Restrained visuals are acceptable. The player should encounter compelling people, consequential conversations, an inhabitable place, and discoveries that change the significance of earlier scenes.

The game should withstand close reading. It should also be pleasurable to play before anyone begins analyzing it. A player must be allowed to laugh, misunderstand someone, enjoy an irrelevant anecdote, or feel embarrassed without receiving a paragraph explaining the theme.

"Masterpiece" expresses ambition. It is not an acceptance-test label or a claim that an AI review can certify. Give the work a serious attempt through drafting, discrimination, revision, and play. Do not lower the artistic target merely because it is difficult. Do not announce that it has been achieved because the build passes.

## 2. The main correction to the previous direction

A missing person plus a metaphysical registry is not automatically profound. Replacing ordinary paperwork with enchanted paperwork would preserve the problem. The story must not use a ledger, archive, identity database, authorization system, ministry, or administrative machine as its central image of human existence.

The previous batch records, identity plates, withdrawal permissions, reconciliation procedure, timed pulse, and registrar cover-up are retired. The associated gate checker tested that retired outline only. None of its results apply to a new story.

Keep the useful engineering principles: original observations remain accessible, character belief differs from fact, later understanding changes actions, saves are reliable, and the game does not require a live language model. Rebuild the fiction from human circumstances.

## 3. What to take from the reference

Use dialogue as action. Let the player choose how to approach a person, what to reveal, when to stop, and what uncomfortable possibility to acknowledge. Let the protagonist's habits affect what they notice and how they explain it. Permit abrupt shifts of emotional register when the characters and situation support them.

Do not reproduce a familiar detective's biography, iconic companion, named skills, thought-system terminology, characteristic interface, city history, dialogue, or sentence-level voice. Do not automatically choose amnesia, a hangover opening, or personified psychological faculties. Any interior mechanic needs its own psychological justification.

Our artistic interpretation of the reference is that serious writing can be funny, physically specific, intellectually ambitious, and willing to let the player behave awkwardly. The new game must discover its own reasons for those qualities.

## 4. Character standard

Follow docs/10-NAMING-AND-VOICES.md. Use historical given names plus literary surnames as the working naming method, with recorded provenance and coherent setting fit. Build highly idiosyncratic voices through actual dialogue, varied listeners, pressure, and ordinary life. Preserve exact accepted passages as anchors. The examples are provisional, not the final cast.


A character is not a position in a philosophical debate and not a dossier of contradictory adjectives.

Write them doing something before explaining them. Give each major character immediate work or desire unrelated to delivering clues. Know what they misunderstand about another person and why maintaining that misunderstanding has been useful. Let a relationship contain habits established by actual prior incidents.

A contradiction needs a cause. "Generous but cruel" is not enough. Establish who receives the generosity, who pays for it, and what happens when those people meet. Do not explain a person's entire adult life through one formative wound. They have made choices since then.

Characters may understand themselves accurately in one context and badly in another. Do not give everyone an eloquent private thesis. Some thoughts remain unformed. A character may hold a sophisticated belief and still answer a question badly because they are tired, offended, or trying to finish a task.

Supporting characters must not exist only to validate the protagonist's growth. A person can refuse the protagonist without secretly teaching them a lesson. Reconciliation is not mandatory. A relationship may improve while a moral disagreement persists.

## 5. Philosophy standard

Revision 3 requires substantial Kant/Spinoza investigation under docs/09-PHILOSOPHY-INVESTIGATION.md, with primary-source locations and genuine disagreements preserved. This supplements the scene-first method; it does not authorize eight disconnected textbook summaries or philosophical mascots.


Start from a circumstance that creates pressure. Then identify the difficult question it raises.

For example, "memory and identity" is an area of interest, not a scene. A person asking you to repeat a dead relative's words, then correcting the words they dislike, creates a conflict the player can enter. The intellectual problem can become explicit later, if someone in the scene has reason and ability to articulate it.

For every central thematic question, the writer must privately identify a tempting answer, the experience that makes it attractive, a serious objection, and a later choice that tests it. Do not distribute these answers across a tidy cast of representatives. People can inhabit incompatible answers at once.

Abstract conversation is allowed. A blanket ban on monologues would be another formula. Earn an extended thought through a particular speaker, an immediate need, a responsive listener, and consequences. Remove passages that merely translate the scene into its intended interpretation.

Do not settle the ending with a balanced lecture declaring that every side was partly right. Determine what physically happened where the plot depends on it. Leave genuinely unresolved questions unresolved. Distinguish uncertainty from a missing explanation.

## 6. Symbolism and recontextualization

Objects must exist usefully before they become interpretively interesting. A coat keeps someone warm. A missing button can be annoying. A song may be enjoyable. None needs an explanatory caption about what it represents.

Repeated material should return under changed circumstances. Its meaning may differ between characters and need not converge into one final definition. Do not build a numerical quota of motifs or turn every object into a clue. Some ordinary details should remain ordinary.

A strong revelation changes the player's understanding of something already encountered and makes that earlier material worth revisiting. Preserve the original wording. Show how the new information supports the changed reading. Let that change affect an available question, action, or relationship when appropriate.

Do not reduce recontextualization to "the person was secretly evil," "it was a simulation," "the narrator forgot everything," or "the literal symbol meant the opposite." These devices are not universally forbidden, but none is a default solution, and a surprise alone does not satisfy the requirement.

## 7. Language rules

The baseline is precise and alive. Elevated language is welcome when the perception or thought earns it. Plain speech is not a lesser register. Avoid making every sentence audition as the most important sentence in the game.

### Hard restrictions for original narrative prose

No em dashes. No habitual contrastive "not X but Y" constructions. No bureaucratic or academic vocabulary used to manufacture emotional grandeur. No copied distinctive prose from the reference game or another author. No invented quotations attributed to real writers.

The project rejects the familiar metaphysical uses of ledgers, registries, archives, dossiers, audits, protocols, administrative erasure, and systems that formally process human souls or memories. Do not evade that rejection by renaming the same mechanism.

The technical PRD, diagnostics, and data schemas may use accurate technical language. These restrictions govern fiction and player-facing literary narration, not ordinary software documentation.

### Patterns that require editorial inspection, not blind deletion

Repeated three-part crescendos; abstract noun stacks; universal claims about humanity after small actions; symmetrical "X is Y" aphorisms; a concluding sentence that explains the emotional meaning of the paragraph; overuse of "something," "somehow," "a kind of," and "as though" to avoid identifying a perception; generic personified weather; identical sentence rhythms across characters.

Stock phrases about the weight of memory, the architecture of grief, a tapestry of lives, a city holding its breath, and a silence louder than words are warnings about an underlying failure of perception. A thesaurus substitution does not repair them.

Colons are acceptable where ordinary grammar needs them, but fiction must not repeatedly use a colon to introduce its own explanation. Avoid decorative ellipses and italicized profundity. Mechanical warnings are not evidence that text was written by AI.

### Preserve what a simplistic cleanup would destroy

Do not normalize every voice into polite contemporary conversational English. Do not eliminate deliberate repetitions that have dramatic force. Do not remove difficult ideas merely because they are abstract. Do not make every metaphor literal or every sentence short. Do not make crude humor, bodily discomfort, vanity, desire, or cruelty disappear in the name of polish.

A good edit may make a passage stranger, sharper, or less immediately comforting. It need not make it smoother.

## 8. Anti-pattern examples

**Rejected:** "The city kept a ledger of its grief, and every citizen was an unpaid debt."

This uses a grand administrative metaphor to announce a theme before providing a person or an event. The repair is not to replace ledger with tapestry.

**More promising, not final canon:** "She had taken his boots to the repairer twice since he died. The second time she brought them back herself. He had polished them. She had only asked him to mend the soles."

The second example gives a relationship to an object through a specific objection. It still needs a surrounding scene, a distinctive person, and a reason to be there. Do not reuse it mechanically or treat bereavement as the default topic of every scene.

**Rejected character description:** "He is a compassionate cynic whose loyalty masks his fear of abandonment."

**Required development:** Write an encounter in which his loyalties come into conflict. Show whom he disappoints, the explanation he offers, and what he does after the disappointed person leaves. The description can be useful private shorthand only after the scene earns it.

## 9. Scene requirements

A scene should contain someone trying to do something, resistance that arises from another person's circumstances or the material situation, and an observable outcome. Quiet observation scenes are valid when the act of attending changes what the player can understand or do. Do not force an argument into every room.

Before drafting, record only the inputs the scene actually needs: entry knowledge, present characters, immediate pressure, permissible disclosures, relevant earlier choices, and possible exit changes. Do not produce a page of theoretical analysis for every three lines of dialogue.

After drafting, inspect what a player can infer without the design notes. A character's identity should be legible through their attention and behavior, not just their nameplate. A choice should be a thing the protagonist could actually say or do, not an authorial label such as "choose existentialism."

At least some scenes should be allowed to end awkwardly. A failed attempt can produce new material without secretly rewarding every approach equally. Essential plot access must remain possible, while optional intimacy and reconciliation may be lost through a knowingly consequential choice.

## 10. Player interiority

Begin with a small, authored interior point of view. A remembered humiliation, a bodily reaction, and a professional habit may disagree without becoming separate talking mascots. Internal language must remain distinguishable from an external observation.

Do not display an omniscient tooltip stating what a character really feels. A player's reading is a reading, even when it is perceptive. Acceptance of factual deductions must never certify the moral interpretation of a person.

A replay may change what the protagonist attends to because of prior choices, while the observed event remains stable. Once displayed, a line must remain in the transcript with its original context. Do not stealth-edit the player's record to simulate unreliability.

Build additional interior mechanics only after a prototype scene demonstrates their value. No large psychological skill tree by default.

## 11. Presentation

Use readable text, static illustrations or deliberately limited environmental imagery, a clear sense of place, and restrained sound where original or appropriately licensed assets are available. No mandatory voice acting, elaborate animation, 3D movement, or cinematic pipeline.

The design must not resemble an administrative dashboard. No large stats grid, kanban-style evidence interface as the default screen, constant notifications, floating productivity cards, or decorative charts. The notebook is a personal reading aid, not the visual identity of the game.

Protect reading rhythm. Allow instant text, adjustable text size, transcript access, persistent mute, visible keyboard focus, and no mandatory countdown. Sound can establish a room without carrying a necessary clue by itself.

## 12. Editorial authority and review

One lead writer owns the integrated prose and story canon. Other agents can develop alternatives, investigate a character, test a scene, or challenge a reading. They do not each write a chapter and concatenate the results. Technical parallelism does not justify splitting artistic authority arbitrarily.

Separate reviews of causal coherence from reviews of prose and dramatic effect. A correction to a clue must trigger a check of the scenes that depend on it. A technically valid scene may still be boring. A beautiful scene may still make the mystery impossible.

Reviewers identify exact passages and consequences. They may say a passage works. They do not manufacture a flaw quota or assign "masterpiece scores." The lead records substantive disagreements and decides rather than averaging all feedback into bland prose.

The owner's taste remains authoritative. Lack of a live owner response is not permission to fabricate approval. Continue under a clearly labeled provisional editorial decision when the task permits autonomous work.

## 13. First artistic milestone

Before generating a full game, create a playable or minimally interactive 15–25 minute section with a specific relationship, a mundane interruption, one meaningful interpretive change, and consequences that survive into a revisit. Duration is a design target to measure, not a promise or word-count equation.

Develop two genuinely different openings for the same human conflict. Use different approaches to viewpoint and dramatic pressure, not synonym swaps. The lead chooses and revises one, with an independent review. Preserve the rejected version for comparison without merging every attractive sentence into the winner.

The result must be worth reading without portraits, music, or claims about its themes. Then use presentation to support it.

## 14. Resource use

Follow docs/11-CONTINUOUS-REVISION-AND-HANDOFF.md across sessions. Keep one editorial owner, protect accepted work, and use new contexts for specific research or critique rather than repeatedly reinventing the project. Continue concrete improvement during active authorized execution, then leave a truthful handoff.


Use the available development capacity for actual alternatives, difficult scene revision, route-aware dialogue, integrated testing, and comparative review. Do not spend it generating a hundred-character encyclopedia, an enormous framework, or endless automated self-praise.

The first complete game should be compact enough to revise thoroughly. A larger work can grow from it after the opening and relationships have demonstrated that they deserve expansion.
