## What the encounter changes

Initial plan: record a complete gesture, open the two individually responsive new paths and close the old fold so the tree can pass. Blaise hopes enough recorded and understood detail could preserve a person. Noor has let her growing grasp of a particular organization slide into confidence that the diagram is complete enough to authorize the combined transfer.

The argument separates those errors. A lasting record does not constitute eternity or continuing personal activity. Knowing a real relation, even adequately, does not mean knowing every current bodily condition. A positive claim about immanent dependence does not license an untested material generalization.

Revised operation: retain the old support; either test both auxiliary connections together while Dora directs a reversible change, or postpone that test and keep the already tested single reach. The first route actually exposes the shared neck. The second acquires only the reason for further inspection, never the unperformed result. Both give Dora a smaller current activity she chooses and leave the broader relocation open. Delay affects the proposed journey and Noor's appointment; it is not a final sacrifice, good ending or punishment for doubt.

The disclosure choice is separate. On one branch Blaise tells both listeners his preservation wish; on the other he keeps it private and asks the philosophical question. The later conversation does not announce that hidden reason. Neither route chooses Spinoza or Kant as a state flag. No correctness meter grants the leaf view, bodily success or blessedness.

## Literary risks for the lead's decision

The explicit argument occupies substantial space. Its reason is Blaise's wish to keep a person and Noor's changed relation to understanding, while a real relocation is being prepared. Keep interruptions and ordinary aims, but do not make Dora exist only to puncture thought with jokes. Her resistance to another repeated trace and request for the smaller available reach are her material contributions.

The principal risk is making Noor's overconfident transfer an allegory that punishes positive metaphysics while the skeptic alone keeps everyone safe. The intended correction concerns an exhaustive operational drawing, not immanence itself. Preserve the distinct positive change: Blaise can let Dora develop without first securing her in a complete record, and Noor's desire to understand generates a new activity worth pursuing. If the final rewrite makes her only retreat, the scene has failed its assignment.

The opposite risk is letting lyrical first-person certainty stand in for the missing argument. Preserve Blaise's conditions-of-cognition challenge and the individuation question. They affect the action and leave a specific later disagreement; they should not become a brief disclaimer after an authorially guaranteed revelation.

The limited alternative is deliberate. Keeping the old support today remains a workable path, with further inquiry owed. The scene does not choose the whole-game ending or write the protected person's response by proxy. It also does not tell us whether the other Dora would have made either choice.
