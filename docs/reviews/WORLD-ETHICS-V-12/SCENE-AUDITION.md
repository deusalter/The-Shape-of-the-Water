# Later encounter proposal: when the washing is dry

2026-10-04. Actual prose audition for the lead writer, not selected canon. Read the dependency and philosophical-boundary notes beside this file before adaptation. This is a later encounter with the continuing orchard Dora, Noor and Blaise. The other Dora is elsewhere on her own established route; the scene invents no change in her fate. It supplies neither the final ending nor an identity for the person René protects.

## Before the work

Noor has spread her coat across two chairs. She is sewing the little pockets back into its lining. The stones are in a bowl on the floor, with a spoon nobody has explained.

You take the spoon out before sitting down.

“I was using that,” she says.

“For what?”

“Keeping you out of the chair.”

You sit on the edge. She moves your knee with the back of her hand and goes on sewing.

The drying passage is open on one side. Beyond it, the orchard rises toward a narrow break in the white ridge. Washing hangs between the trees. A man's trousers have been arranged with the legs pointing in different directions, and whenever the wind gets into them they appear to be making an important decision.

Dora has been watching them through the little opening beside her speaking piece. The piece is coupled to the root under the sill. You can see the wet tissue hold its edges whenever she speaks.

“Whose are those?” she asks.

“No idea.”

“They should go where the trousers go. It looks more interesting.”

Noor bites the thread. She folds the coat with the new seam outside so she can find it again.

“The game begins when the washing comes down. I intend to arrive before everybody has decided which stones belong to me.”

She offers you a needle with a small square of cloth stuck through it.

“Keep that where nobody will sit.”

You put it beside the bowl.

“Nobody but you,” she says, and retrieves it.

There are two newly grown strips beneath the window. Dora has been learning to move pressure through them while the old root carries the greater part of the work. The new strips go round the narrow break. The old root goes through it. As long as that root stays where it is, the tree cannot move farther up the ridge.

Noor has opened each new fold separately. You have watched Dora answer at its far end. Today the three of you mean to let her use both together, then release the old support. She wants to reach the upper slope before the trees turn their leaves away from the light.

“You said there were red ones,” she tells Noor.

“I said some of them looked red from here.”

“I would like to make the investigation myself.”

You have brought a board and charcoal. On the board are the pressure marks you took while Dora learned the first strip. Each time she pressed, you moved your thumb beside the mark and drew the next. The sequence runs nearly to the edge.

“We ought to take the second one before we change anything,” you say.

Noor looks at the board.

“We have its answering test.”

“I want the whole movement. We might need it later.”

“Then turn the board over.”

You don't turn it over. There is a grease stain on the back. You had been keeping the clean side for this.

The mouth in the root opens.

“What do you want me to do?”

“Exactly what you did before.”

“I couldn't reach the bottom before.”

“As much of it as you can.”

Dora begins. Under your other hand, pressure travels through the wall. You follow it with the charcoal until the line leaves the first sequence and bends toward the grease stain you have been trying to avoid.

She stops.

“Was that it?”

You study the two lines. She has done something the first one has no room for.

“Again,” you say.

“Blaise.”

“So I can get it right.”

“I want to see the leaves.”

You lower the board. Noor has begun to put the stones into her repaired pockets. Each settles with a small knock against the next.

## What Blaise means to keep

**Choice: Tell them why you want the complete trace.**

“I keep thinking there must be enough of it somewhere. Enough of the way someone is, to keep them.”

Dora is quiet. You can feel her holding a little pressure against your hand.

“In the board?” Noor asks.

“If I understood everything it records.”

She takes one of the stones out again. A green one, too round to remain where she puts it. She catches it against the bowl.

“You could learn a great deal. You could keep this for a long time. Someone could still be reading it when none of us is here.”

“That sounds like very little, when you say it.”

“It isn't little. It is a different wish from the one you told me about.”

**Alternative choice: Ask whether understanding can preserve anything that memory cannot. Keep your reason private.**

“You said understanding could reach more than appearances. Can it keep anything of us when no one remembers? I want you to explain that before we take this apart.”

Noor puts the green stone down and catches it as it rolls.

“All right. But we are taking a support apart. I haven't promised to show you eternity inside it.”

“I know.”

“I couldn't tell.”

She puts the stone into her pocket. The cloth pulls the coat askew.

*Both approaches continue here. The private reason is not spoken or recorded on the second route.*

Noor takes the board by a clean corner.

“I can follow these marks. One pressure, then another. I can remember how she made them. But suppose I understand why this particular arrangement can answer and change, why its parts continue to make this life together. The answer doesn't consist of all the times I watched it happen.”

“It still happened at those times.”

“Yes. And it will stop. If the activity ends, a perfect account of it won't put her back at the window.”

“I'm still at the window,” Dora says.

“I know. I'm trying to explain why you don't belong to his board.”

“I can do that.”

The pressure leaves your palm. A moment later it returns at your wrist.

Noor waits until Dora has settled it.

“I used to start with the marks and imagine some last mark that would finish them. Now I begin with the existence we talked about. Bodies and thinking express it. This life is a particular way it acts. Her dependence belongs to what she is. It isn't something added afterward because she was unlucky.”

You look at the green stone's bulge in her coat. She is standing with her weight against it and has not noticed.

“You get from that to her?”

“That's the difficult part. A general argument that everything depends on Nature never did much for me. It could have been about anybody. Understanding this particular life through that dependence is different. I can see why the changes that frightened me needn't take her away, and why preserving her outline can fail her completely. She follows from the same existence I do. There isn't another kind of stuff inside her, waiting to be rescued from the rest.”

“You know some of what keeps her going.”

“I think I know something of what she is. And something of myself in knowing her. The understanding is an activity of that same Nature. It has not come from outside to look in.”

“Whose activity?”

“Mine. I'm still the person with a stone pressing into my hip.”

She shifts the coat. The stone falls out and hits your shoe. You pick it up for her.

“Then you're glad because you can save her?”

“I'm glad I can help. But I was enjoying finding this out before we knew what to do with it. I wanted to go on understanding. For once I wasn't waiting to be told that I had done enough.”

She looks toward the hanging trousers.

“I'm enjoying it now. I'm also going to be late.”

The wind lifts one empty leg. It falls against the other.

“And this is the part that lasts?” you ask.

“You keep moving it to an evening after my death. I mean my understanding now, so far as it knows itself and this body through their necessary dependence on Nature. Its truth isn't another event waiting at the end of this one. I don't remember being alive before my body. I don't expect you to find me afterward by taking the right passage.”

“You call that part of you eternal.”

“I do.”

“I still don't know how it is yours.”

Noor looks down at the green stone in your hand.

“Nor do I know how to explain everything I mean by that. It is this mind doing the understanding. I don't have to become somebody larger to take pleasure in it.”

“Does Nature take pleasure in you?”

“It isn't someone watching to see how well I manage. Understanding this gives me pleasure, and I understand the existence it depends on as its cause. That's what I mean by love. I think it is one finite way Nature understands and loves itself. My own activity. That's what makes it worth having now. I don't have to finish being good before it begins.”

Dora gives a short, involuntary laugh. The sound catches in the speaking piece.

“What?” Noor asks.

“You still haven't given me back the blue bowl.”

“I brought the other one.”

“It has a crack.”

“So does the blue one.”

“Yes. Mine.”

Noor closes her eyes. You return the stone to the proper pocket.

## The claim has to reach the work

Noor takes the board. With the side of the charcoal she joins the two pressure sequences at the place they share.

“Here. She can continue through these together. We can let the old fold close once the two new ones are answering.”

You put your thumb over the join she has drawn.

“We tried each while the old one was carrying her.”

“And she answered from each end.”

“With the old one carrying her.”

Noor looks at the marks again.

“We can keep it open while we begin.”

“Then we've changed the test.”

“Yes.”

You keep your thumb on the charcoal. There is more you want her to change, and you know the test won't accomplish it.

“I believe you know a real relation. You and I can stand on opposite sides and follow the same pressure. But wherever we stand, the body is given to us in space. Its changes come in an order of time. Those conditions let it be an object for us at all. Even the necessity we understand belongs to an object we can experience under them. You keep going from that to what this person is through Nature.”

“And you think understanding ends there.”

“I think you've given me no reason it can make those conditions disappear. Calling the result her essence doesn't show that you know her apart from them.”

Noor puts the charcoal down.

“I don't think I have made the conditions disappear. I think I understand a dependence that they don't create. Being unable to look at it from nowhere doesn't make it unknowable.”

“I haven't asked for another place to look from. I'm asking what warrants the further claim. You haven't got a different kind of intuition because you understand the wall very well.”

The mouth in the root stays open. Dora has been listening.

Noor rubs charcoal off the edge of her thumb. It spreads into the skin.

“I still take myself to know more than the marks,” she says. “But knowing this relation doesn't tell me that our drawing contains everything her body is doing. I treated it as if it did.”

“Then don't close the old fold on the strength of it.”

“I won't.”

You lift your thumb. The join has smudged. Noor draws a short break through it, leaving the rest of the observations legible.

“And will you let her move without getting the complete trace?” she asks.

Dora presses at your wrist again, waiting for your answer.

## A different intervention

**Choice: Put the board aside. Keep the old fold open while Dora loads both new connections together.**

You lay the board on Noor's empty chair. She takes the two new folds. You put your palm against the old one, where she shows you, and wait for Dora to say she is ready.

“First one,” Dora says.

Noor eases it open. A small movement runs beneath your hand.

“The other.”

The second movement comes back stronger. You had expected the first to become less noticeable. Instead both press toward the same narrow place beside your thumb.

The speaking piece makes a dry sound.

“Enough,” Dora says.

Noor closes the second fold to its former position. The pressure settles. Your hand has hardly moved. You are sweating as though you have been carrying something heavy.

“They meet before they get to me,” Dora says. “I can do either. Not both like that.”

Noor kneels beside the old root. She follows the two new strips with her fingers until she finds where their living tissue shares a narrow neck. It is below the visible fork. You hold the folds in their resting positions while she brings the lamp down.

The neck can be widened here. It cannot be made ready by merely changing the drawing. It needs an overlapping growth from the broad lower root, which is still farther down the passage.

“We stay here tonight,” Noor says.

Dora is silent.

“Unless,” she says at last, “I can go as far as the fork without taking the tree.”

Noor studies the rooted strip already in use. She asks Dora to try a smaller movement. This time you feel only the first line answer. It holds. Dora draws back before you tell her to.

“I could have the upper light there,” she says.

You look at the washing. One of the sheets is between her and the ridge.

“I'll take that down.”

“Fold it properly. It's still wet at the hem.”

**Alternative choice: Leave the existing support in place today. Help Dora take the already tested reach as far as the fork, and postpone the combined transfer.**

“I want another look below it before we try both,” you say.

Noor lets the two new folds rest separately. She shows Dora where they are, then asks her to take only the reach she has already used.

Dora presses to the fork and draws back. The old root remains where it is.

“I can wait here,” she says. “Move the washing.”

You put the board on the chair and begin with the largest sheet. Noor follows the new strips below the fork. She cannot yet see enough to call them independent. She leaves them joined to the old support and arranges a return visit from the lower passage. You have acquired no result from a test you declined.

“Come to the game,” she says, getting up. “You'll only keep touching things if I leave you here.”

“After I've folded this.”

“She'll make you do the corners again.”

“I can hear you,” Dora says.

*Both routes continue with the present support intact. Only the first has exposed the shared neck under combined load. Neither settles the argument about knowledge of Nature.*

You take the sheet by its two dry corners. The wet hem touches your trousers. You hold it away from yourself and make it worse.

Dora waits until you have unfolded it a second time.

“Bring that end over here.”

You do. She holds the corner against the wall with the reach she has already learned. You walk the length of the sheet back toward her. For once it comes together without dragging on the floor.

Beyond the white ridge, the upper leaves catch the light. A few look red. You begin to tell Dora where to attend, then find she has already turned toward them.

Noor takes the pressure board off her chair. She puts it under the coat so the charcoal will stay dry.

“Still want it?” she asks.

“Yes.”

She hands it to you. The two sequences are there, and the join you changed, and a clean rectangular patch where your thumb had covered the wood.

Noor goes to get her blue bowl.
