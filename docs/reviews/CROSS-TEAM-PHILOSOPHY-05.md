## Source fidelity and arguments worth retaining

**Kant.** LP-R06's appearance exchange is a useful positive argument, not merely a refusal to know. Simon can press, measure and separately handle both objects while rejecting the inference from “appearance” to “unreal.” Blaise's question about removing every condition of givenness is distinguishable from changing a lamp. The Aesthetic's empirical reality of space and its explicit warning against illustrating transcendental ideality merely by colour/taste support this distinction. Stang's discussion of competing two-aspect readings and the triviality objection supports keeping Simon's further realist challenge open. An experiment requiring spatial relations does not by itself settle their status when things are considered independently of our sensibility.

Neither bodily Miriam is a noumenal counterpart of the other. Nor do observed recurrence and changed calendar labels demonstrate literal backward objective time. The Second Analogy explicitly rejects a phenomenon's moving from a succeeding to a preceding time. The packet correctly keeps encounter order distinct from a demonstrated universal chronology. This is compatible with continuing empirical investigation; it is not permission to call a contradiction in custody “transcendental.” The Paralogisms source and Grier also support preserving B-E01's earlier correction: ordinary recognition and particular recollections do not already assert a separable simple soul. No new packet reinstates that error.

**Spinoza.** LP-E04's positive answer about an activity of the same Nature (`PERFORMED-REVISIONS.md:257`) is faithful within the fuller earlier argument. I18 supplies immanent causation, while I28 still demands conditioned finite causes. The sentence cannot explain the recurrence's particular mechanism, and it does not pretend to do so. Her simultaneous wish to preserve the account and hear the other woman's answer is not a refutation of immanence. It preserves a finite want that metaphysics does not satisfy by renaming it.

I D8, V21–23 and V34s distinguish eternity from continued autobiographical memory. V30–36 give the stronger positive position: understanding the particular mind/body through God, and intellectual love as an activity of divine self-love, not merely an assertion that past propositions stay true. V36s itself distinguishes a general demonstration of dependence from understanding a particular essence. Do not let the accessible LP-E04 paraphrase replace the fuller positive exchange if the latter is removed from a route advertised as developing intellectual love. The lead is retaining the God argument. No added terminology is needed to protect it.

The paper proposal would be an ordinary finite means of preserving a statement through an extraordinary event; it is neither Spinoza's eternal aspect nor a restoration of Ada. II7, II13 and II18 do not support a memory that changes while a completely identical bodily state is certified. The fiction has not certified that identity. IV39s makes bodily organization and radical change consequential but does not license “amnesia proves a new person.” V41–42 do not award blessedness for choosing the considerate branch. The packets maintain these distinctions.

Keep Blaise's objection in BLAISE-ALLOWED that giving need not seek repayment or remedy a lack. The Appendix's criticism of divine ends is historical doctrine; its sufficiency can be contested. Miriam properly admits that simply saying “outside Nature” has not answered his claim. Likewise, Simon's post-injury-anchor objection challenges a story of a gift without logically disproving every possible intention. These are substantive disagreements, not mistakes to erase through the evidence engine.
