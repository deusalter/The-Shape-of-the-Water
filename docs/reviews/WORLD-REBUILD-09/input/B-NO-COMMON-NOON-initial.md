# B. No Common Noon

Second incompatible treatment, first saved 2026-10-04. Provisional audition, not selected or owner-approved canon. The passages below are actual scene experiments. Rules following them constrain this candidate; details explicitly left open must be resolved before playable integration. This is a new world, not the bath with different scenery.

## Ending written first: the place at the table

The woman on the far landing has brought a cake. She holds it flat against her chest, underneath her chin. From here you can see one thumb making a dent in the icing.

Noor has never met her. She has described this woman to you three times, always beginning with the things she could not have inherited. A taste for swimming. Excellent teeth. The inability to leave a drawer half open. All this came in letters carried through three different shores.

Now there is a landing between them. Noor cannot make the last step alone.

Behind you the supper is beginning again. You hear Dora asking who has put a knife in the jam. You know whose knife. You know that, in a moment, the younger Noor will say the jam deserved it.

The long bridge has room for two returns. One brings the far landing into contact with this one. The other keeps the supper reachable. Both have held separately. When you joined them on the small frame, the returning movement of one broke the other. There is no third attachment hidden under your hand. You have taken the frame apart yourself.

Noor leans her weight on the rail. Her knuckles are the size of the hard sweets she used to steal from Dora's kitchen.

“Tell me what she's wearing.”

“Blue.”

“That isn't a garment, Blaise.”

You describe the coat, the cake, the thumb. Noor listens with the impatience she usually saves for a door that opens inward.

“She'll drop it. Do it now.”

You can release the supper's attachment. You can keep it and take Noor back down the steps. Neither movement needs an answer about the whole of Nature. It needs your hand.

**Choice: Release the supper attachment and join Noor's landing to her daughter's.**

The voice behind you reaches *the jam* and stops. The opening narrows to a line of lamplight, then the grain of the closed wall.

Noor has already begun to cross. You help with the first step. On the second she shakes your hand off so she can take her daughter's arm.

The woman begins a speech she has plainly rehearsed. Noor gets the cake out of her hands.

“Where can I put this?”

The woman starts to cry. Noor starts to cry too, though she keeps asking for a flat surface. You find one. It is too small for the cake and you have to stand there with two fingers under its edge while they embrace.

Later, when you put your fingers in your mouth, the icing tastes of salt. You cannot tell whether it was made that way.

**Alternative choice: Keep the supper reachable. Tell Noor you will not make this joining.**

You say it before touching the rail.

Noor looks at you. The woman across the gap cannot hear the answer, only the voice in which you give it.

“Then take your hand off me.”

You do. Noor sits on the step. After a while she raises her arm and waves to the woman with the cake. The woman waves back. It takes both of them some time to stop.

You can still get Noor down safely. She tells you where to put the chair, which stair will catch its foot, and when to rest. She gives you nothing else to do.

Downstairs, Dora asks who has put a knife in the jam.

You used to be able to say the next line with her.

**Ending design limit:** this excerpt tests the intimate cost of a physically established route incompatibility. The whole ending must also resolve the saved child's future, the culprit's exposure and Blaise's own access to recurrence. They cannot be compressed into this reunion or left in a closing summary. The refusal above is an actual costly alternative, not a secretly wrong philosophy answer. The younger Noor is a local recurrent continuation with her own standing; closing access is not yet authored as killing her. That distinction must be confronted before the decision, not concealed in author notes.

## Opening audition: a knife in the jam

You have come back for the sentence after the joke.

Dora Helmer has put six places on the table and food for eleven. She will count the empty chairs before she lets anyone begin. You have watched her do it twice. Tonight you sit where the long side of the table almost touches the wall, so she will have to ask someone else to move.

Above the open arch, the sea stands on its edge. A boat comes down it bow first. Its sail hangs toward you, full of the wind from the kitchen. The man aboard is shelling peas into his hat. He gives the table a casual wave as he passes the height of your head.

Noor Brangwen waves back with a fork.

“That's your boat.”

“It knows the way.”

“So did the last one.”

The last one is visible through the other arch, sticking out of the white shore like a tooth. Children climb into it at low water and shout orders at the sand. You used to correct their terminology. You have stopped.

You carry people between the shores. Most of the work is waiting for someone at the other end to answer. People believe the difficult part is stepping out over nothing. That is usually easy. They watch your feet and put theirs where yours have been. The difficult part is persuading someone to stay behind, with a hand on the answering line, until everybody has crossed.

There is a loop of that line around your ankle now.

It runs from the door under the stairs to a little iron jaw beside your chair. If you keep your heel down, the jaw cannot shut. If you lift it, the route behind the stairs will return to the supper's beginning. It has done so twice. The nick you made in the outside rail is still there when you pass it. The nick on this table is not.

Dora arrives with a dish wrapped in damp cloth. She wears two aprons, one backward, because Noor has been spitting cherry stones at her whenever she bends over.

“Nobody spit in this.”

Noor puts her fork down with exaggerated care.

“I was about to give you a compliment.”

“Give me the stones. Compliments in the empty bowl.”

You know the joke about the jam is coming. You have your answer ready. Last time it made Noor laugh so hard that the spoon fell out of her mouth. She was still laughing when she asked you about the far shore.

You had not prepared for that part.

Dora unwraps the dish. Bread, dark at the edges, with a hot seam of cheese running down the middle. You tear off the end before she can hand you a knife.

“That is everybody's,” she says.

“Everybody can have the middle.”

“The man has a political system.” Noor tears the other end off.

For a moment you get what you came for. Heat against your tongue. Noor trying to talk with her mouth full. The boat's shadow slipping over Dora's hands. Nothing asks you to understand more than this.

Then Dora takes the lid off the jam.

“Who put a knife in here?”

You give the line you have kept for yourself.

“It had a weapon.”

Noor laughs. Not quite as much as before. You had allowed no place for that.

She wipes her mouth on the back of her wrist. She looks at the empty chair beside you.

“Did you find René?”

You press your heel harder into the floor. There is time. You can still choose a sentence that makes the next one easier.

René Dedalus left from the far shore with you. You came back alone. You have told Dora the crossing failed. That is true of the route you meant to take. There was another route. You saw René take it, carrying the child wrapped in his coat.

Noor knows there was a child. She does not know you held the answering line for him.

**Choice: Tell Noor that René crossed with the child and you held his line.**

“He got across,” you say. “There was someone with him. I held the line.”

“Then why did you tell her it failed?”

Dora stops laying out the bread.

“I said our crossing failed.”

Noor looks at your foot.

“Lift it.”

“That will send the passage back.”

“I know. Lift your foot off the line and put the peg through. Then tell us.”

The peg is hanging from the jaw on a cord. It will keep the connection open when you let go. You put it through. Your heel comes up without anything happening.

Noor has seen you decide to stay.

**Choice: Say René stayed behind, and lift your heel before Noor can ask where.**

“He stayed.”

Your heel rises. The little iron jaw closes with a click.

Through the door under the stairs comes the sound of Dora unwrapping the dish. In this room her hands are still resting on the bread.

Noor turns toward the sound. She looks back at you.

“Blaise.”

You have already got up. The route has opened. It will take you round the outside of the room and back to the moment before you sat down. You will have carried this conversation with you. They will begin it again.

You do not have to go. The sound under the stairs continues while you stand beside your chair.

**Follow-up: Take the return passage.**

The door is low enough that you have to bow your head. Halfway round, there is a gap between the outer wall and the white sea. You check the nick in the rail with your thumbnail. It is still there.

On the other side of the wall Noor says your name again.

You stop.

She should be asking whether that is your boat. The question comes before you sit down. You have heard it twice.

Instead she is saying your name slowly, as if calling you out of a very deep sleep.

The return door is open. You can see the table through it, the six places, the bread under its cloth. Dora's hands are reaching for the cloth's corners.

Beyond the opposite arch there is no white shore.

There are houses. So many that some have been built on the slopes of others. Their upper rooms lean over the arch without touching it. A tree has grown through the hull of your wrecked boat. A child's shirt is hanging from one of its branches. The wood around the trunk is black and soft.

A woman stands on the narrow landing between the arch and the first house. She has one hand on the answering line.

She is Noor.

Her hair is pale. Her nose seems larger in her face. You know her first by the way she holds the line, looped over two fingers, saving her palm from the friction.

“Don't come out yet,” she says. “Get the peg.”

You turn toward the table. Noor is sitting there, licking jam off the back of her spoon.

“What?” she says.

The woman on the landing stamps her foot.

“The peg, Blaise. For once in your life, while somebody is asking.”

You cross the room. Dora slaps your hand because she thinks you are taking the bread again. You pull the peg from its cord and bring it to the arch.

There is a receiving hole on this side too. You have never used it. You fit the peg. The woman lets go of the line one finger at a time.

For several breaths she does nothing. Then she laughs, a hoarse little noise you do not recognize.

“I was very good at that by the end.”

You reach for her. She lets you take her elbow, but keeps her other hand raised behind her. Someone you cannot see passes a stick into it.

“How long?” you say.

“Help me sit.”

You fetch your chair. By the time you bring it back she has begun arguing with the person behind her about a bag of onions. She will not take them. They are thrust at her. She pushes them back hard enough to make them knock against the door frame.

“I grew them,” she says. “I know what they taste like.”

The bag disappears. She sits.

The younger Noor has stood up. Dora has taken the hot dish off the table and set it on the floor, as though there will be room for what has happened if she moves enough things aside.

You keep looking at the tree through the boat.

“Was it me?” you ask.

The older woman closes her eyes.

“I don't know what you've done.”

You begin to tell her. She opens her eyes at once.

“Water first.”

**Follow-up: Bring water; ask which events she actually witnessed.**

**Follow-up: Ask the younger Noor to help her while you examine the arch and answering line.**

**Follow-up: Tell both Noors, in Dora's hearing, exactly which line you held for René.**

All three continue to the same first investigative interval. They change audience, immediacy and trust, not the observed tree, simultaneous embodied women or the fixed act at the far shore.

**Unperformed alternative at the peg:** the truthful stay route must also reach the changed coast through an actual joint inspection after the supper conversation. It must not secretly operate the return or acquire a memory of the return passage. Its scene remains to be written before this can be called a playable branch.

## Fixed history and rules to test

This revision rejects the research proposal's unexplained export of accumulated age. No substance called time is pumped into a harbor. The shore has sustained ordinary births, eating, repairs and deaths during successive local days. The supper room is a small recurrent enclosure, not a whole basin in which every otherwise isolated place must reset. The larger country has no universal reset rule.

1. Local order is real. The nick, the tree's growth and Noor's years must arise through located continuous processes. Calendar labels are not evidence of a shared present.
2. The supper enclosure has a physical return route. Its exterior passage can carry a traveler and marks beyond the local reinstatement. The earlier configuration has Blaise outside the room, so entry does not create a second resident Blaise by administrative deletion. Other people can use the route. It is not his supernatural privilege.
3. Two one-way lines make one reciprocal joining. Each line has a witnessed physical action at its own end. Opening only one can convey a voice or object without establishing a traversable round trip. Ordinary local sequence continues behind an unjoined opening. Rejoining is a new event and retains already encountered effects.
4. A peg preserves an existing reciprocal connection while the person holding its line moves away. It does not select a date. Blaise cannot dial a preferred younger person. The opening's changed far side needs its own material causal history, discovered in the first act.
5. René intentionally removed the far shore's return attachment after Blaise held its answering line. The action left a passage to the recurring enclosure usable in one direction while depriving the shore of the means to return a traveler. René carried a child into an independently continuing shelter accessible from that passage. His alibi, that he was at the supper when the shore's inhabitants say their bridge was cut, relies on an assumed common noon that those severed connections never supplied.
6. Noor's older continuation began when she stepped through the shore arch during an earlier supper traversal. How the scene's younger resident coexists with that continuation must be established through a witnessed repeat with a consenting traveler. Neither can be dismissed as a counterfeit; both can change and refuse. The continuing Noor helped build a society and has relations Blaise has never possessed. She was not standing at the line for decades. Other inhabitants took turns; she returned to it for the eventual contact seen here.
7. Blaise's heel click operates the near return, not René's earlier cut. It exposes an already existing consequence through a new conjunction. His concealment and aid to René are actual culpable acts; the author will not invent an automatic punishment for telling a lie.

**Unresolved construction point before selection:** the exact graph and sequence relating René's one-way exit, the older Noor's departure, and the changed arch must be drawn without a covert master clock. A fixed partial-order ledger is required. This candidate is not ready for a causal lock merely because the opening image is strong.

## Long-form construction

Six movements, provisionally 68,000–88,000 encountered words on one substantial route. At 180–240 words per minute, reading alone spans roughly 4.7–8.1 hours. Exploration and deliberation may add time; no duration is measured. Exclusive alternatives are excluded from these route estimates. The six movements change the work the player can do, rather than extending the same interrogation.

- **Supper / 8–10k:** learn ordinary return use, commit or resist Blaise's concealment, meet the inhabited changed coast, establish the two Noors' independent histories and solve how a person left the recurring room. First bounded causal finding is reachable without judging which person is real.
- **The built shore / 12–15k:** explore the town through three residents' incompatible needs. Noor's daughter has gone to another shore. A public play ridicules the old visitors; Blaise finds himself represented in it as a fool, not its secret hero. Establish the sequence of the broken connection through growth, repair and eyewitness action. Some local losses predate his help to René, preventing convenient total guilt.
- **The narrow country / 12–16k:** travel by a reciprocal joining the player helps perform. Enter a region where rooms are carried between households and dinner invitations reserve physical space. Find the child René saved as an active person whose wants contradict his preservation plan. Blaise can now carry other people's effects through recurrence; consent becomes concrete because they can also act without him.
- **The divided voyage / 13–17k:** two reachable routes preserve different witnesses and lead to substantially different encounters before reconvergence. Trial joins on a small inhabited scale settle what can coexist. A Spinozist character defends one intelligible reality across the joins; a Kantian argument attacks the unwarranted inference from navigable experience to a completed object. The argument alters the proposed intervention and leaves real positive commitments in play.
- **The cut / 13–17k:** independent routes establish René's act, the warning he had, and Blaise's exact assistance. Confrontation occurs where the act can be reconstructed. No final room presents all answers on a tablet. The changed country participates in deciding access; the child and both Noors can reject Blaise's plan.
- **Two landings / 10–13k:** perform the final local configuration, settle its reachable lives, and inhabit its aftermath. Develop separately the fate of the recurring child, the younger Noor and Blaise's own return. The excerpt above is one encounter in this movement, not an adequate ending by itself.

Before selection this structure needs one further ordinary scene independent of the return machinery, a complete causal graph, and a serious alternative ending that does more than withhold the attractive reunion. Its current strengths are immediate agency, spatial investigation and a society that has acquired its own history. Its dangers are explanatory machinery, multiplying continuations too easily, and letting an old woman's independent life collapse back into Blaise's loss.
