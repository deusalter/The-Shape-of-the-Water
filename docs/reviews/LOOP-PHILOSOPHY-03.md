## LP-01: narrow bodily restoration or confront the philosophical departure

**Concrete passage:** proposal Memory table row, line 39 in reviewed version: autobiographical memory/affect persist, while “Her bodily condition returns to the anchor's condition.”

This may mean the visible bruise and fatigue disappear, as its examples suggest. If it means exact identity of the whole body, it places new episodic memories in a body with no corresponding difference. That strains the Spinozist account the game is developing: II7s, II13, II18s and III2 connect thought/extension as one order, and memory with bodily affections. V21–23 does not license explaining the exception as eternal memory.

**Priority before rule lock:** either scope restoration to what the fiction actually establishes, leaving hidden bodily conditions unknown, or explicitly record the stronger commitment as original fiction and give Miriam the challenge. This is a fidelity/evidence precision problem, not a request to remove the extraordinary premise or manufacture a physiological explanation. Sent to lead and root.

## LP-02: literal backward travel would challenge Kant, not exemplify him

**Concrete passages:** proposal lines 23, 35–39 and 77. The manuscript properly says Kant does not explain the recurrence, and leaves the actual wider history unsettled. Add the sharper boundary if literal return becomes certified: Second Analogy, retained lines 6020–6028, excludes a phenomenon's passage from a later objective time to an earlier one. Aesthetic §5 treats different times as successive parts of one time.

Henrietta's sequence of recollections and present perceptions is not the same question as the calendar's repeated labels. Keeping this distinction in an actual inquiry is warranted. It should not become a narrator's effortless reconciliation of the fiction with Kant. A restored-state interpretation and literal backward travel impose different commitments. Neither presently follows from the proposed successful prediction.

**Experiment:** after a successful prediction, let a participant ask what established the direction of objective time rather than merely the date. A reply may justify action while leaving that question open. If Henrietta eventually learns that it was literal return, retain the resulting philosophical pressure. No “time is subjective, so reverse it” unlock.

## LP-03: use the Ada replay to preserve a real disagreement about freedom

**Concrete passages:** proposal lines 48–54, 79; C1-S08 lines 335–337. The proposal already recognizes that changed memory means changed starting conditions. Keep it. A new choice after a remembered failure neither refutes necessity nor demonstrates Kantian transcendental freedom. Nor does a repeatable reply prove an absence of agency.

Miriam's answer that she becomes less at the mercy of her first idea is a defensible Spinozist claim about increased activity. It is not a full shared answer with Kant. The newly inspected Third Antinomy solution preserves empirical determination and distinguishes intelligible causality; its conclusion expressly withholds proof of freedom's actuality or real possibility. Grier and Rohlf expose, rather than erase, the interpretive difficulties. Kant's practical account is larger than this theoretical limit; this wave did not newly inspect the later primary practical works.

**Experiment:** Henrietta can obtain the reply she intended by selecting a remembered cue. Allow her to call that freedom. Let someone ask whether the greater control explains her own ends, and whether it amounts to understanding Ada. Miriam need not condemn the useful knowledge; another person can still object that her account gives too little agency. No doctrine is certified by whichever conversation sounds kinder.
