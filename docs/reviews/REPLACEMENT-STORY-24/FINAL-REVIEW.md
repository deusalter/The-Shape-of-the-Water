## Promise and remaining dramatic risks

The promise is legible. Erasmus's restraint applies while Blaise remains within Aubade (line 49). Refusal secures the present unrepeated interval; it does not abolish the power (line 115). Blaise matters because Erasmus wants this particular informed person's acknowledgment. The story does not assign him an unexplained civic right to decide for everyone. Other residents can still value returns, and future disagreement survives the resolved departure.

Three execution risks remain, rather than requests for another broad synopsis iteration:

1. The canceled tribute is now an actual earlier cost, but the eventual scene must make Erasmus's attachment strong enough to withstand the much greater final refusal. His disappointment and affection must carry that choice; the promise alone cannot.
2. The substantial later arguments have a real object in Blaise's lost interval. Their full scenes still need the sample's interruptions, appetites and changing proposed acts. Vera and Erasmus have not yet received equally substantial performed auditions.
3. Possessive recurrence romance and hospitable false divinity are familiar structures. The proposal's distinction lies in reciprocal rescue, the deliberately surrendered witness position and the relationship retained with a finite patron. The outside company and residents must acquire particular lives in the manuscript rather than serving as agreeable evidence for departure. The synopsis offers those lives; the sample demonstrates its method locally.
