The comparison establishes material provenance in the continuous history being investigated. It does not detect a soul or identify the cutter. Basil's witnessed removal by René remains a separate source.

2. **Compare the cradle with P.** The recommended authored resolution is that the fresh blade groove and retained fibres were made while René supported P for the cut. Establish this with the actual damaged strands and their torn connection, not matching colour. Alma identifies the earlier groove as her own ordinary use. Her account of René's borrowing, alteration and access is separately retained. A borrowed implement alone is not an exclusive-access argument.

3. **Meet D doing something chosen.** My preferred role is an adult former collaborator of René's, still capable of leaving the repeated support for bounded intervals. This is a proposal for the lead to accept or reject, not a selected identity or a euphemism for an established child. First encounter: D is trying to open the low house's window far enough to pass a large, badly shaped plant outside. The plant is a gift D dislikes but has kept alive. The clean strips brace the moving frame as well as the support inside. They are ordinary supplies with several uses. Let D refuse being introduced solely through the failing body.

4. **Separate preservation from substitution.** D has consented to maintenance through a supported local return. An outside living continuation carries their acquired experience through replacement of the failing part. This is materially different from interrupting both of Dora's paths and reinstating an unsupported occupied arrangement. Show the support while it is working; do not kill or replace D to prove the distinction. A later small test or an already encountered natural failure can establish the dependence.

5. **Give D a consequential account of the preparation.** Stronger proposed history: D accompanied René partway to the supper room and knowingly asked him to keep the old support working. D saw him cut the direct braid while the second path was still active. D did not see the installed older body arrangement or the delayed wedge. Their first account must state the witnessed cut and their own request directly, rather than withhold an eyewitness solely for a last-page surprise. They may defend preserving their life while condemning what he concealed. This supplies a witness against interest, corroborated by the tool and material histories, and gives D responsibility without making them the secret mastermind.

6. **Confront René at the actual shared support.** The reconstructed sequence, observed consequences, acquisition/access accounts and D's limited eyewitness answer who did what before any confession. René can then explain why he expected O to contract and why he called reinstatement recovery. He is wrong about the possibility the player and Dora have already performed: O can develop a new relation without returning to the old circuit. He need not confess that his love was false or accept Blaise's theory of God. The practical consequence is that he loses sole control of the support. He shows its operation to D and the others, removes his remaining prepared interference in their presence, and cannot privately restore the old arrangement after the final choice.

This is one coherent proposed disclosure construction. Its eyewitness scene and comparisons must be written before authorship is marked proved in content. If the lead rejects D's direct observation, the material and access findings still support a strong inference, but a replacement authorship route needs explicit drafting. Do not pretend a tool with René's name on it or his being the only absent named man is sufficient.

## Two concrete ending paths under one history

Both paths preserve the completed culprit finding. Neither asks the player whether to believe Kant or Spinoza. D must be encountered before the final support decision, and their preference must be heard. The failure of the original R branch remains irreversible on either path. No return recovers the unshared interval as if it had never happened.

### Path 1: the first unreturned supper

Proposed additional commitment: D's failing organization can be carried into a changed, nonrepeating support, but only through an overlap using the external arrangement that currently carries Blaise through his returns. Its viability is established by a modest, consented preliminary transfer well before the ending. The preliminary result does not promise a familiar silhouette or every old capacity. D chooses this future with those specific limits known. Blaise can offer the supporting arrangement; he cannot order D to enter it.

The last operation ends the shared local return and gives its tissue to O's developing relation. Blaise also turns his outside carrier over to D's transition. D remains active through the overlap and survives in a changed bodily arrangement. O survives and continues the countryward change. R2 leaves the room before the final fold and keeps a present life of her own. Blaise remains alive, remembers the events normally, and loses the ability to carry himself through this return. The actually regained double whistle is lost with its sustaining arrangement. Ordinary memory does not disappear to make the sacrifice look greater.

This route has the more positive outcome. It need not be disguised as morally equivalent to refusing help. Its requirements are physical feasibility, consent and a materially developed plan, not a hidden score for kind dialogue. If the premise cannot honestly support D's transition without a late exception, reject this path instead of making Nature reward the preferred attitude.

Ending encounter to write after the existing wall-and-pear scene: D tries to move the disliked plant through the now-open window. Their new reach is awkward. René reaches to help and is told which end to take. He is useful on someone else's instruction; there is no embrace that acquits him. At the next supper Alma arrives late from the wedding with the unserved bitter cakes. Basil asks R2 where she wants her painting hung for the performance. These people continue their plans. When Blaise tests his whistle, someone asks him to stop because they are trying to hear a speech. The cessation of recurrence has made this particular next event possible; it has not made the country perfect.

**Determinate fates:** D lives through the transition; O lives in an altered organization; R2 continues outside the room; Blaise loses this return and its regained skill; René is exposed and no longer controls the support alone. No murdered or interrupted life is restored by the result.

**Setup debt:** show Blaise's carrier physically, make his recovered whistle real before losing it, test D's proposed overlap, explain why a replacement carrier cannot simply be grown before the decisive transition, and write D's freely chosen participation. Alma's party and Basil's performance must remain genuine events with their own disappointments, not applause for the protagonist.

### Path 2: the maintained room

D does not consent to the proposed transformation, or Blaise declines to surrender his carrier. The existing repeated support remains. This is a local practical choice with identifiable beneficiaries; it does not demonstrate that separate souls exist or that God prefers recognizable bodies.

O receives a known finite interval before the new countryward organization can no longer be sustained. Her remaining choices are staged during that interval. She decides where to move, whom to speak to and which capacities to spend. A proposed final place is the orchard court after the wedding tables have been cleared. Alma has left the stained yellow cloth hanging to dry. Dora asks for the rain ribbon to be moved within reach. When its activity stops, the speaking piece ceases because the current living organization can no longer articulate through it. Later attachment to inactive tissue produces no farewell message. This death is performed and its scope is established; no closing sentence leaves her secretly dispersed elsewhere.

R2 leaves the recurrent room before the final configuration and goes to the performance with her picture. She is not required to become O's replacement companion, keep the same household or forgive Blaise. D continues living through the maintained support, with an actual shared arrangement for its operation and with full knowledge of O's death. René's preparations remain exposed. D can reject his deception while still wanting the life it sought to preserve. Blaise retains the local return and regained whistle but cannot revisit an event in which the now-lost O acquired her new capacities, since that interval was carried outside the limited fold. The prepared square removed in movement one stays out of contact. Keeping the support does not secretly reinstall it or summon another R2.

Ending encounter: D has moved the disliked plant outside and is trying to wash the mark it left on the sill. The supporting return restores a worn small part of D's body; their outside continuation carries this day's experience through it. Blaise's two-note whistle works. D asks how the orchard woman died, and he takes his fingers out of his mouth. The player may tell the truth, give a smaller account or stop. Operating the support cannot make this question never have been asked in the continuing house. D's next response belongs to the person who heard his answer. There is no repeated voice from an absent Dora and no last-minute resurrection to soften the choice.

**Determinate fates:** D remains alive through supported recurrence; O dies after her performed final interval; R2 continues elsewhere; Blaise retains a bounded return with a loss it cannot repair; René is exposed and has to share control. This is an ethically costly ending, not a diagnosis that the player chose the wrong metaphysics.

**Setup debt:** establish O's finite remaining interval without a last-minute countdown; show why another active support cannot be supplied in that interval; stage R2's departure; specify the retained support and bodily fold's reach before the ending, retaining the performed removal of the dangerous occupied-place cast; distinguish the continuing house from the returned enclosure; and let D bear knowledge of what has been maintained for them. If those constraints cannot be made coherent, the lead should reject this ending rather than claim an elegant ambiguity.

## Alternatives to preserve but reject in this wave

- **D is secretly the original Dora.** Reject. It collapses the new country back into identity sorting and makes every new person another instrument of the first tableau.
- **D has been dying unnoticed on every return.** Reject unless the entire maintenance history is deliberately rewritten and reviewed. The proposed distinction depends on real supported continuity; a late revelation that all care was mass replacement would invalidate the meaningful difference from René's cut.
- **A new perfect carrier saves everyone and preserves every return.** Reject. It removes the finite conflict by authorial favor. The positive path above must consume an already demonstrated possibility that Blaise values, and preserve the prior loss.
- **René is punished by being put through his own unsupported return.** Reject as a default. It would use R2's involuntary creation as a device for revenge and evade the harder task of ending his control while treating every resulting person as alive.
- **The last wall speaks as God.** Reject. An organized part may speak through a present person. Its size or strangeness does not turn it into substance, a divine witness to the crime, or an authority that certifies the ending.
