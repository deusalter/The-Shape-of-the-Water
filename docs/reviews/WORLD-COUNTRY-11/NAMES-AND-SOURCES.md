## Alma Garth

**Historical given name: Alma Thomas (1891–1978).** Read the Smithsonian American Art Museum's [Alma Thomas artist page](https://americanart.si.edu/artist/alma-thomas-4778), including title/dates and Katherine Markoski's 2024 biography. The inspected biography identifies Thomas as a teacher and artist whose later abstract painting drew strongly on colour and the natural world. Short identification excerpt: “Alma Thomas was a teacher and artist.” Inspected extracted lines 132–147; older appended biographies were not needed for the relied-on claims. Retrieval returned HTTP 200. Full-page SHA-256 and URL are retained in `sources/alma-thomas-retrieval.json`; the complete contemporary text is not republished.

**Literary surname: Caleb Garth in George Eliot's Middlemarch.** Read the existing public-domain local source `narrative/sources/middlemarch.txt`, corresponding to [Project Gutenberg ebook 145](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/145). This was an inherited local source, not a new network retrieval. Title/author and Garth passages were inspected. In Chapter XXIV, the passage beginning “Caleb Garth often shook his head in meditation” develops his imaginative investment in skilled activity that feeds, clothes and houses a society. The relevant passage begins near character offset 527,615 in the repository's single-line extracted text. File SHA-256: `68d72777c012fa0eb92cc9e32fce801be60d8a58c788a6be734f1d6e7ddc62eb`. Selected earlier passages at offsets 483,115–488,605 also establish the named character; no full-novel reading is claimed.

**Intent in this proposed person.** Alma takes pleasure in colour and makes something excessive because she wants a celebration to be beautiful. She also knows the material effects of her work and will not let a visitor's inquiry casually consume the tool or labour she needs. The two sources support that conjunction of pleasure, making and material responsibility. The audition deliberately lets her produce bitter cakes, count a lift badly, and value an accidental stain. She is not a flawless artisan or a disguised philosopher. Thomas's biography and Garth's financial plot are not imported.

## Basil Boffin

**Historical given name: Basil Bunting (1900–1985).** Read [the Poetry Foundation's Basil Bunting biography](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/basil-bunting), title/dates and selected biography/performance paragraphs, extracted lines 22–29. It identifies the historical poet and discusses the importance of sound and performed poetry in accounts of his work. The attribution to different critics in the article remains theirs; this proposal does not pretend to have independently read their original reviews. No poem, distinctive phrase or literary voice is copied. Retrieval returned HTTP 200. Full-page hash and URL are in `sources/basil-bunting-retrieval.json`. Only a short date excerpt is retained there.

**Literary surname: Nicodemus, or Noddy, Boffin in Charles Dickens's Our Mutual Friend.** Retrieved and inspected the public-domain [Project Gutenberg text, ebook 883](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/883/pg883.txt). Title and author were checked. In the early encounter with Silas Wegg, the stranger identifies himself as Boffin and gives Nicodemus/Noddy as his name; local extracted lines 1766–1800. Lines 3325–3365 discuss the Boffins' work among dust. Lines 11164–11185 explicitly call Nicodemus Boffin the Golden Dustman. The retrieval record is `sources/our-mutual-friend-retrieval.json`; full response SHA-256 `0ef01467f55bdb988048e6dbf3459ce3e9dd1733f84227ceb59036685d80c88e`. Selected passages only, not a claimed full-novel reading.

**Intent in this proposed person.** Basil wants an audience to hear a storm at the right moment. He uses discarded material to make a present performance, treating old rooms as useful and sometimes beautiful without believing their former occupants are contained inside them. Bunting supplies the concern with audible form; Boffin supplies a connection between apparently worthless matter and human value. Neither supplies the character's speech. The audition gives him failed timing, an incomplete cast, generosity with a noisy instrument and a real claim on his own scenery. It does not borrow hidden riches, a pretended personality, or Dickens's comic dialect.

## Limits and alternatives

Both full names are inventions assembled from verified naming sources. Name symbolism is author-facing and should not be explained by a narrator. If the lead dislikes their sound, replace them through another checked historical/literary pair while preserving the ordinary circumstances that made them useful. Do not make a character's goodness or credibility depend on recognizing the allusion.

No name or identity is proposed as accepted for René's dependent. The auditions' low house and clean strips establish a route to inquiry, not a family relation or a diagnosis. The ending proposals use the author-only placeholder D until the lead writes and selects that person.
