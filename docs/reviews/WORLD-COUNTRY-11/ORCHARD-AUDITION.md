# Orchard continuation: the tables under the rain

Proposal to the sole lead writer, 2026-10-04. This follows the orchard departure in `A-FIRST-INVESTIGATION.md`. It is an alternative continuation for selection and revision, not installed canon. New proposed person: Alma Garth; provenance and intentional significance are in `NAMES-AND-SOURCES.md`. No identity or age for René's dependent is selected here.

Author staging: Blaise accompanies O. Noor and R2 have taken the dry route. The existing speaking piece remains seated in O's root. It has no voice when detached. Alma is a current embodied grower, not an earlier restored instance. The ridge path reaches an occupied orchard court with a rain-catching trough. No return is operated here. The court and its living supports are outside the supper fold. Proposals introduced here are a public feast, Alma's borrowed root-cradle, and her bounded recollection of René's access. They require lead acceptance.

## Performed scene

Rain is falling on the other side of the ridge. You put your hand out before stepping into it. A large drop lands on your smallest finger and travels under your sleeve.

“There,” says Dora, from the root beside your foot.

She sounds pleased. You had been ready for her to sound right.

Below you, three tables stand under a roof made from the underside of an enormous leaf. Rain runs down its veins and falls through a hole in the middle. Somebody has put a bowl beneath the hole. The bowl has overflowed.

A woman is lying on the nearest table. Only her legs show beyond a heap of yellow cloth. One leg moves while she works, making a little impatient circle in the air.

“Alma?” you call.

The leg stops.

“If you're bringing chairs, put them on the stones.”

“I haven't brought chairs.”

“Well, what have you brought?”

You look at the root. Dora says, “Me.”

Alma Garth lifts the cloth off her head. A needle is held between her lips. She takes it out, looks past you for a familiar body, then crouches to the root.

“Oh. You can get down here now.”

“Apparently.”

Alma presses the speaking piece more firmly into its seat. Dora tells her to leave it alone.

“It's always loose when you bring it.”

“Leave it.”

Alma takes her hand away. She does not apologize. She picks a strand of cloth off her sleeve and holds it between her finger and thumb until she finds somewhere dry to put it.

The table beneath the yellow heap has begun to sink at one corner. Alma goes back to it, puts her shoulder under the edge and straightens her knees. The corner rises. A row of little cakes slides toward her.

You catch the nearest plate.

“That one is for my sister.”

“The whole plate?”

“The one with the flower.”

There are flowers on all of them.

Alma blows a loose hair away from her mouth.

“I know. I got carried away.”

You move the plate to the higher table. Under the cloth is a dress with a great quantity of yellow skirt and two very small sleeves. Alma has been sewing something into the hem.

“Wedding?” you ask.

“Tomorrow. If she gets him to stand still long enough.”

“Does she need all these tables?”

“I need them. She would have everyone standing in a field eating fruit out of their hands. She thinks that makes the marriage more serious.”

She says this through the effort of holding the table level. You take the weight beside her. The ground is soft, but the flat stones by the path have not moved. Together you carry the table onto them.

Alma tests each corner. Only when none rocks does she look properly at the root again.

“Have you come to stay?”

“I don't know,” Dora says. “I wanted to see the rain.”

Alma points with the needle. At the low side of the roof, a pale ribbon of tissue hangs into a trough. The moving root passes under it. Rain lands on the ribbon and makes shallow dents that fill immediately.

“You've got hold of my gutter.”

The ribbon rises. The water it held slides into the trough all at once. You hear Dora laugh.

Alma snatches up an empty dish and catches the next spill before it reaches the dress.

“Do the laughing toward the ditch.”

Dora lowers the ribbon. It meets the water again.

You crouch beside the trough. You can follow the wet tissue from the ribbon to the root without a gap. At the join, small movements pass in both directions. When the root shifts, the ribbon lifts; when Alma tips a little water onto the ribbon, a movement runs back under its skin.

“Again,” Dora says.

Alma pours from a lower height. Dora answers before the last drop lands.

“That one was nearer.”

“It was on the same bit.”

“Nearer to me.”

Alma considers this, then sets the dish down.

You ask Dora whether you can hold the roof away from the ribbon for a moment.

“Why?”

“To see whether it's the drops you're feeling.”

She asks you to do it slowly. You lift the lip of the leaf. Rain continues striking your neck, but the stream passes beside the ribbon into the trough.

“It's stopped,” she says.

Alma looks up into the rain. You keep the leaf where it is.

“Here?” you ask.

“Where I was doing it.”

You lower the leaf. The ribbon dimples again. Dora does not need you to ask this time.

“Yes. Leave that.”

You sit back on your heels. You had imagined bringing her something from this side of the ridge: a wet leaf, a report that she had been right. She is lifting part of the gutter to keep water off Alma's dress. You are the one in need of a dry place.

Alma offers you a cake without a plate. It has a flower with one petal missing. The icing cracks against your teeth. Beneath it is a sharp, unpleasant fruit.

You try not to let your mouth change shape.

“Too much peel,” Alma says. “I've made forty.”

“Forty plates?”

“Don't be a bastard.”

She takes a bite from another cake, thinks, and eats the rest with evident resentment.

You ask where she keeps the cradle for lifting a root. You have noticed her using her own shoulder where she used to fit its broad curved crosspiece.

“He brought it back wrong.”

“Who did?”

“René. Shortened the loop, put the pin in backward. Said he was sorry before I saw it. That's always expensive.”

She points beneath the far table. A curved wooden frame leans against its leg. Its sling has been unthreaded and wound around the crosspiece.

You want to carry it straight back to the room. Something with René's name attached to it. Something you can set beside the wedge while the others look.

Alma is watching the puddle spread toward her cakes.

“Can I look at it?” you ask.

“Look as much as you like. Then help me put it right.”

You bring the frame onto the dry stones. The speaking piece remains by the trough, several tables away. Rain on the roof covers what you and Alma say here; Dora continues trying to turn the water toward the ditch.

The cradle normally takes the weight of a branch in its wide sling. Alma shows you the old rubbed places where the cord ran. The shortened loop will not reach them. Threaded through the inner holes, it instead holds a narrow braid flat against the wooden crosspiece.

Two shallow blade marks cross the wood at that narrow position. One ends before the other. Fine green fibres cling to the second groove.

You do not touch them. You put your own hand beside the crosspiece and remember the width of the cut braid beneath the window.

“Was this here when you lent it?”

Alma sits on the stones. She takes the frame from you and holds it sideways.

“The long mark was. I cut a root on it last change. The short one wasn't.”

“You're sure?”

“Look at the ends.”

The long groove has darkened. Its edges are rounded. The shorter groove has lifted a pale splinter that still bends. Both could have been made by a blade. Neither carries its maker's name.

“What did he want it for?”

“Lifting something under your north window. I asked why he didn't use a strap. He said he needed both hands free.”

“Did you see him there?”

“I took the other cakes up. The first lot. I had to step over him to get to the sill.”

She draws her knees up, making room for the frame between them.

“He asked me to wait with the plate. Something had to begin answering through the room before he could let go. So I waited. Then I passed the plate in. Dora said they were too sour.”

Alma looks toward the root with the expression of someone remembering that an old grievance can now be delivered in person.

“Did you see him cut anything?”

“No. I saw his back.”

You tell her that the direct braid was cut and an old arrangement had been installed beneath Dora's place. You keep your account to what you and Noor actually found. You do not tell her René made the cut.

For a while Alma holds the sling without threading it. Then she puts the frame on the stones.

“I lent him a thing to hold a weight. I didn't lend him whatever you're looking at.”

You say you would like to take the frame for comparison.

She looks at the two tables still standing in the mud.

**Choice: Leave the frame here. Make a rubbing of the two grooves with Alma watching, then help her move the tables.**

Alma fetches the paper in which the cakes were wrapped. You use its dry side. The rubbing catches both grooves and the torn edge of the new splinter. You write which mark she says was already present. She reads that sentence twice and makes you add that she did not see the new cut being made.

Then you lift. Your hands slide under the damp wood. Alma counts badly, beginning with two, and you both have to lower the table and start again.

**Alternative choice: Ask to borrow the cradle until you can compare it with the cut braid. Promise to bring it back before the wedding tables are set.**

“You can have it after these are on the stones.”

You help her lift both tables by hand. Afterward she threads the old length of sling back through its wide holes but leaves the new splinter untouched. She shows you where to carry it without rubbing the grooves against your coat.

“If you find out he didn't do it, tell me that too.”

You say you will.

## Shared exit

Dora has got the gutter to fall in a thin stream beyond the dress. Alma examines her hem. It has picked up a pale pink stain from the table.

“Better,” she says unexpectedly.

She holds the cloth against herself. The stain sits where one of the bride's knees will be. She turns it around to see if the other knee would look stranger without one.

Dora asks you to bring the speaking piece closer before you tell her what you found. She does not know the conversation on the stones. You move with the root until you can kneel beside it, and tell her about the new groove, the changed loop and Alma's account. If you have taken the cradle, you let her feel its crosspiece through the active tissue; a rubbing must be described, not silently read through your eyes.

“He knew the room was answering,” she says.

“According to Alma. She didn't see the blade.”

Dora moves the gutter again. The response comes promptly.

Alma returns with two wrapped cakes.

“There's a low house beyond the split trees. Two roofs, one almost on the other. That's where he takes the clean strips. If he's there, tell him I haven't forgiven the loop.”

You ask whose house it is.

“Ask whoever opens it.”

She puts one cake in your hand and wedges the other beside the speaking piece.

“For later. If you hate it, eat it somewhere I can't hear you.”

## What this scene actually establishes

- **Observed:** current rain beyond the ridge; a continuous local tissue path; a reversible relation between the rain reaching the ribbon and Dora's reports; a current movement Dora can perform through it. This tests the bounded report and new capacity. It does not establish perception of all rain, all roots, private thought, or Nature as a local body.
- **Observed:** the cradle can be rethreaded to hold a narrow braid; a relatively fresh groove and an older groove differ visibly; retained fibres are available for later comparison. The scene does not identify their source by colour or declare the tool unique.
- **Testimony:** Alma lent the cradle to René; he returned it altered; she saw him beneath the north window and recalls him waiting for an answering relation through the room. She did not see a cut. Her account opens an access/knowledge inquiry; it is not a culprit proof.
- **Changed desire:** Blaise begins wanting an object he can display as an accusation and an account he can bring back on Dora's behalf. He leaves with evidence that must remain qualified, a possible obligation to Alma, and Dora already acting somewhere he had imagined himself merely reporting from. His competence can become participation without the scene declaring him cured of the wish for an unborrowed self.

The lead must choose whether the cradle is ever genuinely connected to P's cut. My recommended later resolution is that the new groove and retained fibres came from supporting P during René's preparation, but that conclusion needs physical comparison plus independently encountered history. Do not promote it to an automatic clue conclusion here. The older groove is deliberate nonincriminating context, not a second secret crime.
