## Judgment

Retain the country visits as distinct development scenes. The world has acquired life outside the case. Alma's sister wants an orchard wedding while Alma wants tables, cakes and a speech. The cakes are bad; the stained dress becomes a new possibility. Basil needs a storm at the right moment, a missing performer has kept the coat, and R2 wants a place to show a painting. These pursuits survive their usefulness as clues. The smaller houses inside former houses and the leaf's inconvenient drainage are inhabited consequences of the setting, not a sequence of ornamental rooms.

The macro alternatives materially differ. The orchard route gives a witnessed new bodily activity and a tested relation to the rain report, followed by a qualified access witness. The dry route gives a source-material hypothesis, a witness to acquisition and a different social future for R2. Their evidence and audiences remain distinct. The smaller choices also preserve real possession differences: a rubbing is not the cradle, and arranging a panel comparison is not performing it. Those are worthwhile beginnings of consequence. The later cost of borrowing, delivery and timing still needs an actual return scene; its absence from these first visits is an ordinary drafting obligation, not an installed bug.

## 1. Earn the finite choice before treating the death as unavoidable

**High-value later-draft obligation, not an installed defect.** Ending line 9 explicitly identifies the support incompatibility and exhausted alternatives as setup debt. The two country visits do not pay that debt: they establish a new activity, access and provenance, not the limits of sustaining O alongside Emil. At line 101, the performed ending then says there is “no unvisited second orchard being kept in reserve.” Without the intervening work, that assurance asks the narrator to close the rescue question on the player's behalf.

Write a bounded investigation of the actual candidate supports the player can reach. Use two materially different attempts or comparisons, with their terms established before failure: what each arrangement presently sustains, what changing it would interrupt, and why the available overlap does or does not last. Tie the remaining interval to observed activity, rather than adding a countdown. The final claim can then be precise about these reachable possibilities; it need not claim an exhaustive survey of everything Nature could ever permit.

Emil's refusal needs a performed scene within that inquiry. Let him understand a particular proposed transformation, try or inspect what can safely be tried, and refuse for reasons this person can own. His wish to continue is already treated seriously here. Do not replace it with an abstract refusal of change or make an unearned refusal do all the work of creating scarcity. If the intervening scenes cannot support the stated incompatibility, revise the outcome instead of strengthening the author note.

## 2. Carry Blaise's new want into an initiated act and a later consequence

**High-value psychological revision across these drafts.** The pipe passage is the strongest new Blaise material: he briefly wants to conceal the instrument, knows where the extra tone comes from, and nevertheless tries it again because he likes the sound (visits 257–267). This puts enjoyment into conflict with his wish for an unborrowed capacity. But the next action is summarized as “You may ask to borrow the pipe or put it down” (269), and the ending returns him mainly to holding bowls, supporting hands, holding a picture and carrying the seed tray Basil gives him (ending 133). His restored whistle occurs “almost without meaning to” (141). The people have become more independent while his initiated project is thinner on these pages.

Perform a concrete decision after Basil's invitation to use the noise in the play. Blaise might ask for a part and choose to make this externally supported sound before an audience, or decline because he still wants the recovered bodily act to be what people hear. Give the decision an actual later encounter: he arrives to do what he asked for, renegotiates it, or fails someone who expected him. Preserve the pipe's merely acoustic status and the separate later recovery of his cheek movement. On a route that has not encountered the invitation, do not import it silently.

This small project should expose the larger inquiry into what Blaise thinks must belong exclusively to him; it is not a replacement for that metaphysical trajectory or a lesson in becoming confident onstage. The maintained ending can then make his whistle, silence or performed sound a legible continuation of something he pursued. He need not override either Dora or Emil, and he need not become a better man. He does need a present appetite that survives his usefulness to them.

## What I would not weaken

Keep Alma's and Basil's unglamorous projects, the precise limits on witnesses, the pipe's pleasure, and the adult's refusal to perform gratitude. Keep the death sequence free of a final disembodied message. Keep R2's separate social life: the unavailable chair beside her is more effective than announcing that Blaise has learned to respect autonomy. These choices give the country and ending resistance to his needs. The next work is to give his needs equal dramatic particularity and to perform the finite causal alternatives the ending currently presupposes.
