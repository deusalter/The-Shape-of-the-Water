# The Second Mouth: provisional causal ledger

Selected for development by the lead writer, 2026-10-04. This ledger supersedes the ambiguous use of “last active connection” in A's first saved audition. It is a fictional law set, not a claim about real physiology or Spinoza's sufficient criterion of numerical identity. Still subject to independent review; no owner approval or complete story lock.

## The room before the interruption

Four material arrangements matter in this local case:

- **R:** Dora's room-side bodily organization, including her familiar head and torso. Her left hand is currently engaged with the orchard.
- **O:** the orchard-side continuation of her organization. The visible hand is one part of a larger responsive network already recruited through the roots. It is not an isolated ordinary human hand containing a detachable soul. This country's people can distribute their bodily activities in this way.
- **P:** the direct responsive braid between R and O, running under the window. René cuts this before supper. The cut leaves the room-side end hidden behind a facing; the orchard-side end travels with the tree.
- **Q:** a second responsive connection through the room's own tissue. It passes through the wooden mouth beneath Blaise's chair. It remains active after P is cut. Q also helps carry Blaise's learned cheek movement through the room's ordinary return. Sharing tissue does not merge the two people's minds or give either access to the other's private memories.

```text
             P, direct braid, deliberately cut before supper
     R  ------------------------------------------------  O
      \                                                  /
       \ Q, through responsive room tissue and mouth     /
        ------------------------------------------------

     O remains active in the moving orchard throughout the event.
     Q alone keeps R's current activity connected after the cut in P.
```

The country's normal exchanges preserve a living individual's activity by overlapping old and new relations before old material is shed. A lent hand has the recipient's larger responsive tissues available to it; it can enact and receive far more than the isolated visible appendage would permit. This is ordinary physiology here. People can lend support without becoming one undifferentiated consciousness. Cooperation, privacy and withdrawal remain meaningful.

The room's recurrence can reinstate an earlier local arrangement where current organization has ceased to be carried through an outside connection. It does not erase every organism merely because someone closes a door. Its exact reach is a physical fold in the room, inspectable from the lower passage. Ordinary use is prepared while the room is empty or its occupants have continuing support. Applying an old fold to an unsupported occupied place is dangerous.

## René's preparation and intended act

René has seated an old cast of the room beneath Dora's place. It is part of an earlier arrangement in which her left hand was attached. The cast is not a picture or a recorded mind. It is a physical earlier configuration that the country's local recurrence can reinstate. The old cast has a repaired notch in its edge; the matching notch and earlier repair are inspectable outside the room's immediate return reach.

His preparations, in causal order:

1. Position the old cast under Dora's place, narrowing the imminent return's reach to that occupied portion of the room.
2. Cut P. Q continues Dora's current organization across the room and orchard, so the cut alone does not yet perform the substitution.
3. Insert a shaped wedge that will draw Q out of the wooden mouth as the new table seam hardens. The wedge's broad end is caught in the seam; its narrowed, freshly cut end holds the cord. The table's material change supplies the pull. It is a finite delayed mechanism, not a curse or a special reaction to Blaise's choice.
4. Leave before supper. The ordinary change proceeds whether Blaise interferes or not.

When both P and Q are inactive, the prepared local fold reinstates the earlier room-side body. That reinstatement interrupts the prior R's activity. The orchard-side continuation O remains active. The new room-side woman, R2, immediately has her own continuing activity and claims; she is not an illusion or an actor impersonating the missing woman. Her older bodily arrangement includes the left hand and an earlier experiential history. She does not know facts that happened only to O or R after that earlier arrangement.

The loss is the forced destruction of Dora's ongoing room-side branch, its capacities and its relations, while her larger ongoing organization survives in O. Do not call this the total murder of Dora or describe the continuing woman as resurrected. R2 is newly active through reinstatement, with a prior configuration and experiential history; her standing does not depend on whether Blaise prefers her. The first case establishes the interventions and differing causal histories, not a final answer to every question of personal identity.

The author commits to the difference between maintaining an organization through change and interrupting it to reinstate an earlier configuration. The game will establish operational consequences and a physical causal history. It will not offer a numerical “soul test” or claim that a philosopher supplied an empirical identity detector. How much later change a person can survive is itself a sustained plot question, not a universal formula solved by the first incident.

## Two opening routes

**Blaise keeps his promise:** P is already cut. Blaise leaves the mouth open. The hardening table draws the wedge and releases Q. R2 appears while O persists. Blaise and Noor witness the release and can state he did not touch the mouth. The player does not acquire a memory of closing it.

**Blaise breaks his promise:** P is already cut. Blaise closes the mouth to begin his attempted repair; closing it dislodges the cord from its shallow responsive seat and interrupts Q before the wedge's release. Springing the lips open does not reinsert the displaced cord. R2 appears while O persists. His act advances the interruption. It does not create René's cast, cut P, or excuse those preparations. He can see the dark wedge still caught in the seam when the new hand appears. The wedge subsequently comes free as the seam hardens. If the player wishes to use it as evidence, its later discovery must be performed and distinguished from the directly witnessed release on the other route.

On both routes the immediate conclusion is narrower than “René did it.” The first movement can prove deliberate preparation, the pair of interruptions and the selected earlier cast. Establishing René's authorship and knowledge requires independently encountered later material from the lower country and the dependent person's support. No first-act accusation should be accepted solely because he is the only named absent man.

## Why the act was instrumentally useful to René

The orchard is taking on a continuing bodily organization beyond its former repeating circuit. Dora has agreed to assist that transition. Its new connection, if completed, will cease to return the shared support to the earlier condition on which a dependent person's failing body currently relies. René has been maintaining that person's life through repetition. He cannot indefinitely keep the same stretch both fixed in its return and developing in the orchard's new relation.

René's substitution was intended to make Dora appear safely recovered in the room while preventing her ongoing orchard-side activity from completing that change. It is an attempt to stop an authorized transformation without obtaining the agreement of the people whose bodies make it possible. He expects O's unsupported connections to contract back into the old circuit. The extra independent support the player can help create defeats that expectation.

This dependence must be encountered before the culprit confrontation. The person he protects is not a silent justification. They can want the maintained life, reject it, or disagree with Dora while also opposing René's deception. Exact identity, name, relationship and clinical-like condition remain unselected; do not call the person a child, son or patient in player text yet.

## Evidence and consequences inside movement one

1. The simultaneous left hands are an observation requiring investigation, not proof of which person is real.
2. The two cord ends, still-responsive Q, severed P and the wedge establish distinct interventions. The clean cut must be compared with a naturally shed braid encountered in the same room; “it looks cut” alone is insufficient for the complete conclusion.
3. The lower passage reveals the old cast's actual extent beneath Dora's place and its repaired notch. The relevant relation is seen from two positions; a drawing supplied by an author cannot substitute for access.
4. A consented transfer of support sustains O through an exchange and gives her a new capacity. It also changes her practical dependence on the room. This is a consequential performed intervention, not a doctrine reward.
5. A harmless empty-place return demonstrates the local fold's reach and its restoration of an earlier arrangement. It must not reproduce a personal death as a tutorial. The player can solve the mechanical preparation with complementary physical evidence if they decline the test.
6. The accepted finding is that someone prepared an earlier local restoration and arranged the interruption of both supporting paths. Optional Blaise responsibility is recorded separately. Motive, full authorship and God/Nature remain distinct inquiries.
7. The first movement ends with both present women secure enough to choose their next movement. One elects to travel with the orchard. The other wants to inspect the older arrangement from which her body returned. Blaise cannot both escort them on the same immediate route; the alternative route changes actual scenes before reconvergence.

## Continuity controls

- The speaking piece articulates activity only while physically coupled to O's responsive tissue. Detached, it is warm material with no voice, memory or authority. It never stores or certifies a person.
- The room-side woman lacks the post-cast experience of the current orchard hand. Her unsolicited offer to fetch rope is a new act she performs now.
- The ongoing orchard woman can hear through the active speaking piece and feel through participating tissue; she does not automatically hear all dialogue in the room.
- Noor's private knowledge of the dependency is initially partial. She knows the two support paths and the agreement to let the tree pass; she does not initially know P is cut, the wedge exists, or René has installed a cast.
- After the interruption, the rope holds the tree in place but carries no memory or bodily activity. O sustains itself through the orchard while people inspect the room. Both P and Q remain interrupted. The early transfer creates a new responsive connection to a fresh part of the wall; it does not silently reconnect R2 to O or merge their private experience. No earlier scene may describe Q as still carrying the root's response after its release.
- Blaise's initial knowledge of recurrence and his own mouth is genuine practical knowledge. He does not know every path using that tissue. This does not excuse breaking his explicit undertaking on the covert route.
- The first movement does not say the entire country or every object is conscious. Organization and capacities differ. A living wall is not God, and a large living arrangement is still finite.
- Whistle sequence: lost before opening; optional unsuccessful covert repair; actual recovery offered and performed in a later movement; final route may surrender the regained capacity with this local return. No ending calls the first loss new.
