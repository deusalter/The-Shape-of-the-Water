## Noor: a reference working through conduct

In B Noor asks Blaise to insert the peg before telling his story, understands the labor of holding contact, and has participated in a society beyond his recurring supper. At the ending she directs the practical help she will accept and can withdraw personal trust after a refusal. These are specific actions, not a generic claim that she is brave.

The historical source supports the limited contribution: maintaining communication for others when leaving would strand them. The inspected biography reproduces the George Cross citation's account of Inayat Khan refusing to leave her comrades without communications. This supplies a real relation to Noor's answering-line work. It does not make her a spy, import occupied France, or forecast martyrdom. The draft's clarification that other people took turns at the line is especially valuable: it prevents an inflated saintly vigil from replacing a social life. [Source record](noor-wikipedia.json).

The literary contribution is stronger than “generations” or “new growth.” Near the end of *The Rainbow*, Ursula can feel affection for a past relation without placing that person in her future. The passage then asks, “Who was she to have a man according to her own desire?” and opposes creating the desired man to recognizing a person beyond that desire. This is useful pressure on Blaise's attempt to recover a predictable Noor. Her daughter, bodily age, onions, and refusal of his touch make her more than the effect his remembered supper produced. Both Noors must retain that standing. [Primary record](brangwen-primary.json), final chapter, exact retained needles and offsets.

The novel's pregnancy, illness, religious language and account of an essential self are not this world's ontology. Do not cite Ursula as proof that a preserved inner core decides which continuation is real. The allusion's useful limit is recognition beyond personal desire.

In A, Noor checks an active connection before interrupting it, asks Dora what she is still using, and physically helps preserve contact. The historical component remains apt. Her own desires and changing life are much less developed there, so the Brangwen contribution currently describes the surrounding problem more than her particular role. This is an audition limit, not grounds to add an artificial subplot merely to earn a surname.

## Dora: a good ordinary sound is not yet a completed rationale

“Dora Helmer” is readable and easy to say. But Dora is also one consonant away from Nora, making the combination liable to feel like a lightly altered source name. That cost needs something more precise than a woman in a house.

In B Dora feeds people, counts places, objects to theft from the communal bread and makes room for the older Noor. She has authority and humor, but no independent want has yet collided with the household role others assign her. The ending returns her to the jam line. The inspected Ibsen passage is specifically about refusing to remain a “doll-wife,” educating oneself, and treating oneself as a reasonable human being beyond assigned domestic duties. Calling any domestic labor Helmer would flatten that contribution. An intentional inversion is possible; it has not yet been performed. [Primary record](helmer-primary.json), Acts I and III. “Mrs Nora Helmer” occurs in the primary text itself, so the surname does not rest merely on inferred married usage.

A improves the case. Dora asks Blaise to stop proving the woman at the table through familiar preferences; that woman also acts before being asked. The distributed Dora continues a life Blaise cannot simply reinstall. Helmer can contribute resistance to being preserved as somebody else's familiar domestic performance. That is a substantive tension even though the continuation remains within a house in an extraordinary sense. The name should not turn “a doll's house” into a body-parts pun or demand a repeated Ibsen departure scene.

Carrington is the remaining gap. The retrieved source verifies the exact historical name, her painting and decorative art, and the shared Ham Spray household. None of that establishes that this Dora's cooking or distributed hand carries Carrington's particular artistic contribution. Nor does living in an unconventional household alone justify the selection. “Dora” was a real given name despite Carrington preferring her surname; that biographical preference neither invalidates the name nor supplies a reason to use it. [Source record](carrington-wikipedia.json). Preserve Dora as a working address if it helps the writing, but let her ordinary life determine whether this historical source belongs. Do not graft an artist occupation onto her to rescue a naming note.

## René: correct spelling, excessive borrowed weight

Laennec's mediated listening has a precise source: he describes using a paper cylinder to hear the heart more distinctly. That is more useful than “inventor of the stethoscope,” but no performed René encounter yet shows how he attends, tests, or fails to hear another person. B establishes a rescue and severed return in author history. A establishes deliberate bodily replacement called a rescue. Neither presently earns a character-specific listening rationale. [Source record](laennec-wikipedia.json). The accent in René is correct and should remain consistent if the name survives.

Stephen Dedalus is directly verified in the retained *Ulysses*. I inspected the history/nightmare exchange, the paternity discussion, and the “Non serviam” scene, as well as the opening name occurrence. Escape from imposed histories and refusal of authority could put pressure on a self-authorized rescuer. These are not distinctive enough here to outweigh the surname's prominence. A claim about a brilliant engineer making a labyrinth would instead be importing the mythical Daedalus without doing the specified Stephen Dedalus work. [Primary record](dedalus-primary.json).

More decisively, Blaise Bloom is fixed. Pairing him with Dedalus advertises a second central *Ulysses* name and invites expectations about the Bloom/Stephen relationship before this game's own relation exists on the page. “René Dedalus” also sounds more conspicuously assembled than the other candidates. That is an ensemble cost, not a rule against literary recognition. Reject this pairing now; do not write a source-shaped René to justify having chosen it.

## One considered alternative: Lydgate

**René Lydgate** is a materially stronger surname audition for A. Exact “Tertius Lydgate” is present in *Middlemarch*, chapter XVI. Chapter XV joins his ambition to understand “the more intimate relations of living structure” with the narrator's diagnosis that his intellectual distinction does not penetrate all his judgments about other people. The passage even marks his research question as not quite the one the eventual answer requires. [Primary record](lydgate-primary.json), chapters XV–XVI, exact needles, offsets and retained-file hash.

The contribution would be serious beneficent inquiry whose reach does not authorize every intervention. That bears directly on A's René knowing a living connection matters while deciding its replacement is a rescue. It allows competence and human error to coexist without treating either “good intentions” or “scientific knowledge” as an acquittal. Lydgate is not evidence of the fictional culprit's guilt, and the source's marriage, debts and later plot are not imported.

The sound is comparatively unobtrusive: two syllables in Lydgate, clear consonants, no second Joyce protagonist. Both halves pointing toward medicine could still be too schematic if René has no independent ordinary life. Therefore audition the surname; do not lock the full pair or appoint him a physician merely to match it. B's child rescue gives a thinner connection to Lydgate's particular inquiry, so this is not a blanket replacement across both treatments. A short actual encounter in which René helps somebody and is answerable to them would resolve more than another source biography.

## Cohesion, evidence and limits

First-name address works: Blaise, Noor, Dora and René are short and distinct in rhythm. Noor and Dora share a vowel/r sound, but their syllable counts and on-page shapes are sufficiently different in these scenes. Brangwen is less familiar than Helmer, yet readable; consistent first-name use handles it. No artificial single nationality is required for this original world. The current danger is an anthology of readily legible references, particularly Bloom plus Dedalus and Dora plus Nora, not merely mixed linguistic origins.

All six proposed source components have verifiable names. Verification does not certify their artistic necessity. These judgments distinguish a performed action, author-side construction, and a proposed future contribution. A and B remain unselected, and no full cast audit or name-stripped voice test was performed.
