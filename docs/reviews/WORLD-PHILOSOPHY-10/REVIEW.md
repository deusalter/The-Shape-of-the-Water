## Attribution: no demonstrated doctrinal error in the initial prose

Both treatments distinguish their executable laws from the historical arguments. There is no need to make their worlds less strange to protect Kant or Spinoza. The following are **limits on attribution**, not defects forbidding the fiction:

- Kant's Third Analogy argues for reciprocal determination as a condition of objective coexistence. It does not teach that pulling two physical lines creates time. It permits mediate interaction, too. B's particular bridge operations and absence of a global clock belong to B. They can remain hard truths of that world.
- The *Ethics* physical interlude gives preservation of organization through certain changes. It does not define A's exact branching, interruption or replacement rule as a numerical identity algorithm. A explicitly invents such a rule and must make it observable. That is legitimate fictional construction.
- II.7 neither turns thought into an external controller of matter nor erases distinctions among finite people. A's speaking piece can articulate an embodied person through a physical connection. It would become misleading only if the eventual explanation quietly used a portable immaterial mind token while calling that Spinoza.
- I.18 concerns immanent causal dependence. A's finite country, however large or alive, does not thereby equal God. The initial notes correctly distinguish them. Likewise, disproving a master clock in B would not disprove a complete intelligible order conceived through substance.
- Eternity in I.D8 and V is not an indefinitely repeated supper or an infinitely preserved memory. No initial passage makes that equation. The crucial future task is to give the positive eternal claim substance, rather than merely denouncing survival fantasies.

Initial B line 29, “Neither movement needs an answer about the whole of Nature,” was an editorial risk rather than an error about Spinoza. V.41 itself preserves the value of ethical life without knowledge of eternity. The risk was cumulative dramatic closure: the only actual ending preemptively made the unperformed positive inquiry dispensable. The lead has accepted this distinction and is revising. Do not replace the line with an opposite proclamation that correct metaphysics is required before helping Noor.

## Where deeper inquiry is still missing

**A could settle into a morally careful identity mystery while its cosmology stays in notes.** The duplicate, causal cut and duty of care are compelling, but resolving which organization continues does not yet address what kind of reality these organizations express. The promised “substantial positive argument for one immanent reality” (initial A line 249), attributes in movement III, and intellectual love in movement IV cannot count as performed material. This is the main remaining philosophical weakness, not insufficient disclaimers.

The source argument is stronger than useful cooperation. V.24 says the more we understand particular things, the more we understand God; V.25 connects the third kind to adequate knowledge through attributes; V.29s distinguishes temporal location from necessary dependence on God. V.32–36 connects that understanding with active intellectual love. The relevant question is not only whether understanding keeps Dora alive, but what Blaise or another thinker claims to understand **of her particular existence**, why that claim is warranted, and how it changes the activity from which their joy arises. A quiet, grieving, funny or irritable person may defend this; serenity is not the test.

**The historical disagreement must concern knowledge, not split jobs into science and comforting belief.** A Spinozist claim cannot retreat to “this interpretation helped me cope.” Its ambition is adequate cognition of dependence on ultimate reality. A Kantian challenge cannot win simply by repeating that nothing is proven; it must expose the inference from an objective empirical rule to cognition of things apart from the conditions under which they are given. A successful transfer can establish a fictional law with real stakes while the argument over the further claim remains live. The author can positively commit to an immanent ontology without pretending the characters have demonstrated it merely by discovering plumbing.

**Blaise's hunger for understanding needs equal dramatic dignity with his desire to repeat intimacy.** Both beginnings make his wish to manage another person's response concrete. That is psychologically promising. It should not make every wish for a unified explanation another form of selfish control. Let systematic inquiry first find a real relation, repair a mistaken rescue and enlarge others' options. Then make him face his own dependence within the reality he hoped to observe from outside. His demand to know whether this life is intelligible can be passionate and transformative without the country existing for his lesson.

## Concrete encounters to test, not prose to install

1. **An ordinary transfer that produces a wanted new capacity.** The lead is now auditioning this for A. Let Dora direct an actual operation, with a capacity she values and a loss Blaise finds unwelcome. His attempt to restore a familiar arrangement should erase or prevent that capacity for an intelligible material reason. The player must choose how to help, not which metaphysical sentence to endorse. Both her thought and the bodily arrangement must matter in the account; no mental command travels across an attribute gap. This scene supplies a practical consequence of understanding, but must not label the procedure itself third knowledge.
2. **A source of change that is not the source of existence.** During the case, investigate the junction the culprit mistook for the country's ultimate origin. Show that it really causes the replacement and that it itself requires continuing support. Changing it solves a concrete problem. The consequential disagreement then asks whether the indefinitely traceable finite conditions exhaust explanation, whether a complete series has been illegitimately assumed as an object, and whether immanent dependence on what is conceived through itself is genuinely knowable. The Spinozist answer concerns the dependence of these particular finite activities, not a hidden first creature. Blaise can need that answer without receiving a god who addresses him. This widens the story beyond two-continuation identity.
3. **A late action that cannot retrieve a reply.** Return to a particular activity of someone definitively dead in the causal history. Knowledge of how that singular life depended on others and acted can allow a new, chosen activity among living people. Let a character make and defend the stronger claim that understanding this dependence is participation in Nature's self-understanding; let another challenge the warrant. The practical result must neither resurrect the dead nor reduce eternity to their legacy. Its joy need not erase grief. This is a candidate for the currently promised Ethics V encounter, not a second afterlife mechanic.

For B if retained as the alternative, the equivalent pressure belongs with the saved child. A proposal to preserve the child's body in a repeated state while letting an independently stored mind acquire new experience should meet a concrete problem in that world's authored bodily continuity. Preserved instructions may teach a later learner; they are not themselves that learner. The child can positively seek activities and understanding beyond mere duration. This would develop the body/mind question and give the eternity/duration distinction something to act against, rather than adding a speech to the landing.

These are alternatives and experiments, not additional required side quests or a new quota of concepts. A single well-developed encounter may satisfy several pressures.
