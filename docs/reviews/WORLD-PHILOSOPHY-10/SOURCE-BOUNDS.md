## Kant

Immanuel Kant, *The Critique of Pure Reason*, J. M. D. Meiklejohn translation, [Gutenberg 4280](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/4280/4280-h/4280-h.htm). Existing translator/title record inspected in `research/philosophy/sources/kant-critique-gutenberg.json`. Underlying print impression unverified. Local text SHA-256: `642bd845dd0315ac76399d5d1663e7ed75247e065251833bd47ef051fd997ebe`. All ranges below are extracted-text lines in `research/philosophy/sources/kant-critique-gutenberg.txt`, not standard A/B pagination.

- **Conjunction and original unity of apperception, lines 3905–4013.** The passage explains why representations belonging to one consciousness presuppose their possible synthesis. Its qualification, “even although I am not conscious of them as such,” blocks treating the argument as a requirement for continuous introspective narration. This supports distinguishing the conditions of an objective experience from a person's confidence or recollection. The critic did not re-read the whole Deduction.
- **Third Analogy and immediately preceding close, lines 6350–6486.** “the coexistence of substances in space cannot be cognized in experience otherwise than under the precondition of their reciprocal action.” Kant distinguishes the order of our apprehension from objective coexistence: successive perceptions alone cannot supply the latter; reciprocal determination supplies the required relation. He explicitly allows **mediate** reciprocal connection. The proof concerns a condition of experience, not a mechanical rule that two handles manufacture simultaneity. It therefore inspires B's operations without historically establishing them.
- **Phenomena/Noumena, lines 7190–7305 and 7335–7435.** “the logical possibility of the conception” is distinguished from “the transcendental possibility of things”; a concept's consistency supplies no intuition corresponding to it. The second range includes the negative/positive noumenon distinction and the “merely limitative conception” conclusion. The positive sense requires a nonsensible intuition that is not ours. This argument does not turn unexplored coasts or an unusual distributed body into noumena, and it does not make empirical objects unreal. The second range begins inside the negative definition; no missing introductory words are quoted as if inspected there.
- **Critical Solution of the Cosmological Problem, lines 11393–11473 and 11528–11555.** “If the conditioned is given, a regress in the series of all its conditions is thereby imperatively required.” Kant accepts that requirement while attacking the inference that a completed phenomenal series has thereby been given as a thing in itself. This is not a prohibition on looking for the culprit or building systematic explanations. It is also not a proof that a game's map must have no global clock.
- **Paralogisms, lines 9220–9306, re-read after the new A encounter.** “The logical exposition of thought in general is mistaken for a metaphysical determination of the object.” The passage distinguishes the identity of the subject in consciousness from cognition of its substance, and identifies an equivocation in moving from the logical subject to a self-subsistent object. This supports the encounter's challenge to Blaise's inference that remembering through changing conditions establishes an independent owner. It does not prove that no person persists. Only this selected range was inspected, not the complete chapter or A/B textual variants.

## Spinoza

Benedict de Spinoza, *The Ethics*, R. H. M. Elwes translation, [Gutenberg 3800](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm). Title matter and translator directly inspected. No printed impression or Latin comparison verified. Local text SHA-256: `6d9c85ac448e0f6acfbdce43715e18c50ac5ef78c2634cb3e43f84f0ef8a0b55`. Ranges refer to `research/philosophy/sources/spinoza-ethics-inspection.txt`.

- **I definitions/axioms, lines 1–85; I.17s ending and I.18–20, 655–732.** Substance is conceived through itself; modes through another. I.18 calls God “the indwelling and not the transient cause of all things.” Elwes's “indwelling” means immanent. This does not mean a country-sized creature hosts God or that shared physical pipes establish substance monism. I.D8 says eternity “cannot be explained by means of continuance or time.”
- **II.4–8 selected, lines 1530–1638.** II.5–6 restrict explanation to the relevant attribute; II.7s calls a mode of extension and its idea “one and the same thing, though expressed in two ways.” Thought is not a spirit issuing efficient commands into otherwise inert extension. Nor does this rule prohibit ordinary embodied speech and action. A speaking piece can be an organ or transmitter within extension without becoming a mind parcel. II.8s is outside this range.
- **II.13 and physical interlude, lines 1790–2005.** The definition constitutes an individual through bodies whose movements preserve a fixed relation. Lemmas IV–VII show ways material replacement and change can preserve that organization. Postulate IV emphasizes the body's ongoing dependence on other bodies. A's hand/root/swallow operations are fruitful fictional adaptations. The text does not give an executable criterion for modern numerical personal identity, let alone the exact threshold for either Dora.
- **IV.38–39 and adjacent passages, lines 6424–6510.** IV.38 connects bodily capacities with the mind's capacities. IV.39s refuses to identify death solely with becoming a corpse and considers radical transformation, including the Spanish poet and infancy. Neither familiar appearance nor heartbeat alone settles its question. The examples remain interpretively difficult; the passage is not a clinical diagnostic standard.
- **V.21–36, lines 8060–8330.** V.21 ties memory/imagination to bodily endurance. V.22–23 argues for an eternal aspect through the body's essence. V.24 says, “The more we understand particular things, the more do we understand God.” Its argumentative context matters: V.25 traces the third kind from adequate knowledge of attributes to adequate knowledge of essences, while V.29s distinguishes temporal location from necessary dependence on God. Therefore successful practical troubleshooting is not automatically third knowledge. V.32–36 makes understanding, intellectual love and God's love of himself one positive connected claim, not merely an agreeable attitude toward interconnection. V.34s warns that people “confuse eternity with duration.”
- **V.39–42, lines 8368–8520.** V.39 connects bodily activity and understanding; V.41 preserves the importance of ethical life even without knowledge of eternity. This is why B's initial claim that a choice need not settle all Nature was not a historical error. V.42 says, “Blessedness is not the reward of virtue, but virtue itself.” The understanding is the activity and source of increased freedom, not a correct doctrine answer unlocking a reward. V.33s, in the preceding range, also acknowledges the difficulty of calling an eternal perfection a transition to joy.
