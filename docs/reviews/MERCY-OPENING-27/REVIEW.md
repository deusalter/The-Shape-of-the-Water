# Blind opening review

**Verdict:** This opening earns further reading and play without requiring a hidden explanation. Its strongest hook is not the reset mechanism itself: it is watching a perceptive, frightened man use a defensible account of love to make another person's refusal provisional. Preserve that conflict. The principal local repair is a speaker attribution that briefly obscures who concealed a return.

**Scope:** AI close reading, not a human playtest or an executed-engine witness. I read only `docs/reviews/MERCY-OPENING-27/input/reading.txt`: the supplied authored sequence, available actions, and selected actions. I have not read narrative summaries, other reviews, future modules, or author explanations. Conclusions about interaction concern this packet alone.

## What Blaise wants and cannot bear

Blaise desperately wants to be wanted as this particular person, with enough necessity that the wanting cannot simply expire. He wants gratitude to establish his purpose and affection to establish his irreplaceability. The public miracle and private kiss expose the same wound: "It would have been pleasant to stand before a room of people and discover that gratitude had made him important" becomes the fear that "A funnier, more useful man could then stand in his place."

He also wants innocence in how he secures love. He wants his knowledge of Julian to count as intimacy while remaining exempt from the power that knowledge gives him. The small staged proximity before the kiss makes this genuinely uncomfortable; almost anyone can recognize arranging an encounter without thinking of it as coercion.

He does acknowledge his motives, often with painful accuracy. What he refuses is their practical consequence: Julian can retain a real history of love and still give an answer Blaise cannot accept. His wish for "an arrangement of events which would let him say no without making another person experience refusal" establishes the reciprocal problem early. He wants his own refusal to cost Erasmus nothing and Julian's refusal to leave him a remedy. Moving his chair closer to Erasmus enacts what his self-knowledge cannot stop.

## What I understand happened

Erasmus can return Aubade to an earlier state. Julian once requested a return after a bridge collapse gravely injured or killed Blaise, and that intervention is an actual good, not merely the origin of a trap. Erasmus and Blaise can remember experiences absent from the restored present; Erasmus remembers more. Erasmus has promised not to return the city without Blaise's request while Blaise remains there.

There have been further returns. Blaise carries a shared evening with Julian that Julian no longer remembers. His premature use of their improved line exposes a return concealed from Julian. Julian had already been considering an outside theatre company, invites Blaise, and says he may go without him. Following the discovery, he leaves with scenery, saying he is going to Vera. Blaise sees the cart take the west road, suspects departure, and seeks Erasmus instead of following. He obtains the morning, retaining memory.

I do not know exactly what prompted the previous concealed return, how memory retention is assigned, or whether Julian has actually departed. Those uncertainties sustain interest; they do not prevent me from understanding the immediate stakes. The final empty theatre makes me want the next scene.

## Voices and philosophical force

**Julian is the most immediately alive.** "I checked my confidence" is both a joke and an account of his work. The missing punchline, glue, measurements, and fallible props give his independence a body. "I want to see what happens to the play after somebody sees it" makes departure a positive desire, not just flight from Blaise. Their performance is indispensable: I have experienced something worth losing before the dispute asks me to judge its preservation.

**Erasmus is compelling because his power has ordinary needs.** Carrying the cups, tearing the cloth, wanting bread, and liking company at the moment of return prevent him from becoming only a metaphysical spokesman. "I don't yet know" gives his anger an honest limit. His final offer is disturbing precisely because it contains truth and kindness.

**Blaise's thought is genuine argument, not decorative profundity.** The claim that later wishes are not automatically better—"Time did not adjudicate between them"—has force. "The hope did not make the argument false" refuses the easy move of disproving an argument by discovering its interested motive. His objection to an uncaused kiss is equally substantive: affection stripped of personal history would not obviously become freer or more Julian's.

The unresolved step is also productive: even if an earlier desire was better, who thereby acquires authority to restore its owner against his current answer? The prose lets Blaise discover the authority he is assigning Erasmus without disposing of the temporal argument. Preserve that distinction. Do not replace it with a lesson that the latest desire is always the authentic one.

The argument from usefulness to substitutability is less secure: being loved partly because of qualities does not establish that any bearer of those qualities is interchangeable. It works as Blaise's frightening inference. It should remain recognizable as his inference rather than settle into the story's uncontested metaphysics.

## Where understanding or emotional interest weakens

1. **Speaker attribution at the disclosure needs repair.** After Julian's "I know it's funnier," a new quoted paragraph begins, "You told me there hadn't been another return while we were writing this." Ordinary alternation initially assigns this to Blaise, although the ensuing scene requires Julian to be accusing Blaise. Add an explicit Julian attribution here. This is the one confusion that damages understanding of the central injury.

2. **Blaise occasionally interprets himself before the reader can.** "He noticed that he had given Erasmus the position from which Julian could be overruled" repeats an insight already stated through "which Julian was entitled to answer." Both are good sentences individually. Across this short packet, repeated exact diagnoses slightly diminish the frightening discovery available in his behavior. Consider cutting or delaying one repetition, keeping the bodily refusal to stand up or the movement of the chair. Preserve the arguments themselves.

3. **Julian's change of destination creates a small competing mystery.** He says Vera, then the cart takes the west road. That may be a deliberate act of concealment or a change of intention. The packet correctly leaves Blaise's conclusion uncertain—"Then he may be"—but I begin asking whether Julian lied instead of staying entirely with Blaise's concealed return. If that question is intended, keep it. If not, clarify the observed change locally; no explanatory history is needed.

4. **The surreal world is clear enough already.** The bent street, rising dust, edible stars, and healing bowl progress from domestic strangeness to invasive power. The reset's "row of teeth closing" is the sharpest destabilizing image. This reads more as intimate, unsettling fantasy than relentless disorientation, which serves the relationship. I would not add cosmological exposition to make it comprehensible.

## What the choices promise

I found no substantial contradiction between the selected reset action and its consequence: the morning returns and Blaise retains memory. The opening choice to thank Erasmus without claiming existential knowledge also delivers precisely that distinction. I cannot assess the unselected branches.

There is one small textual mismatch: **"Ask about the folded letter beside the paint"** leads into Julian volunteering news of the roof, rather than Blaise initiating that inquiry. Either give Blaise the opening question or relabel the transition to match attending to the letter.

The larger issue is agency rather than a literal contradiction. The narration repeatedly establishes that Blaise could follow Julian; the action list never offers that route. At the decisive moment, **"Ask Erasmus to restore the dedication morning, keeping your memory as witness"** is the sole action. As a reader I accept this terrible decision as Blaise's. As a player, I am being asked to click his decision after seeing an alternative withheld.

That can be an intentional fixed-character opening. If so, make the interaction's framing consistently clear about its role in following and interpreting Blaise. If this moment is intended as the player's moral decision, it needs a meaningful alternative. Adding a token refusal that immediately forces the same request would not solve the problem. This finding does not require a new branch if the authored decision is the intended form.

## Bounded recommendation

Fix the disclosure's attribution and the letter action's wording. Consider one small reduction in repeated self-diagnosis. Resolve whether the Vera-to-west discrepancy is meant to attract suspicion, and decide what the sole reset action asks the player to own. Preserve the comic work, physical affection, Erasmus's imperfect hospitality, and the unrefuted philosophical arguments. They make the final request an injury to something I already care about.
