## What now works

The opening meets the crucial correction. Blaise is hungry for a life he truly remembers and has not lived; the corpse makes the threatened loss both ontological and personal. The scene's “Why must I pretend the pleasure was a warning?” is particularly valuable. It refuses to make immense experience merely a warning against ordinary bad behavior. The recognition of having enjoyed the older man's life is more disturbing than an externally delivered prophecy.

The visual events have different functions: upward water disorients spatial order, the perpendicular table reveals an independently continuing street, the remembered scar destabilizes identification, and the corpse's voice makes event order a practical investigative problem. Faceless presentation does not prevent recognition through hands, habit, voice and memory. The sock and cup interrupt wonder without making the whole experience a joke. Isambard is impatient with a thing in his hand, Hypatia controls and withholds information, and Blaise develops an appetite for explanation. Their voices are differentiated.

The construction also gives the murder a determinate actor, weapon and intention, with material traces and independent correction of the killer's claimed justification. The orchard people predate Blaise's access and want things he cannot encompass. Temporal revision does not erase their reality or the encountered evidence. The two endings already propose material differences in continuation and personhood, rather than two attitudes toward an identical fixed loop.

The positive philosophical positions are substantially stronger than stock skepticism versus connectedness. The treatment distinguishes finite productive power from unconditioned substance, identifies inquiry's objective orders, and retains the demanding adequacy/essence/intellectual-love claims. Its explicit departures from Kant and Spinoza are appropriately fictional. Those achievements should survive the following repairs.

## Three material faults and strongest repairs

### 1. Give the arriving enlargement an actual source stage consistent with the stated law

**Passages:** treatment, “More history than one order can contain” and lines 43, 57, 75; “The murder has an answer,” “The investigator discovers who has been cultivating him,” and “The final return.”

The governing law says backward effects must come from actual events. Hypatia reaches the moment *before* the enlargement and kills its author; the climax nevertheless has the unfinished enlargement arriving along surviving connections. The text does not yet identify what was actually performed before interruption that can produce this later force. “Turning its terminal act back” also names the decisive revision without showing which act or relation undergoes that change. The remembered life can survive by the established rule, but an unperformed future command cannot acquire effects merely because the climax needs it.

The letter adds a related ordering question: before his death, when and through what evidence did the maker know to cultivate the earlier Blaise specifically for his own restoration? Sending recollections to induce his original development and sending them to defeat a later severance are different causal projects.

**Repair:** Place the voluntary makerhood, memory transmission, first performed stage of coercive enlargement, Hypatia's arrival, killing, revision, corpse's transport and surviving transmission in one recoverable event order. This can stay concise and need not become machinery in the prose. One strong construction is an actual begun enlargement interrupted before completion: identify the component it already sent, what death stops, and what later participants can still complete or transform. Give the letter a stated position and knowledge basis in that order. Demonstrate the revision's relevant consequence in an encountered event, so the reader can reconstruct the cause rather than merely accept the term. Do not restore an immutable timeline to avoid the problem; the revision itself is permitted.

### 2. Make accumulated decisions determine more than the final selection

**Passages:** treatment lines 77–89, especially the final sentence naming earlier choices about disclosure, trust and participation.

The two terminal acts alter reality, but the treatment has not yet made earlier choices causally consequential. No specified earlier decision changes who can participate, what each knows, which relation survives, or the shape of the resulting continuation. At present all the actual difference can be produced by selecting the last binary. That is weaker than the owner's request for a paradox and metaphysical endings arising from Blaise's choices across the story.

**Repair:** Specify two or three actual decisions in the shared investigation and carry their consequences into the finale. For example, how Blaise handles the maker's letter can change a named person's informed willingness to complete a crossing; an earlier act of entering, interrupting or preserving a shared performance can leave a particular relation available in a different form. Show which capacities or personal continuities those deeds make possible and which they foreclose. The final act should transform that accumulated situation, not reset it. This need not multiply routes, create virtue points, or require a universal veto procedure. Consequences can change the concrete scope and costs of either broad ending.

### 3. Let the existential and philosophical temptation survive the ending's moral contrast

**Passages:** treatment lines 33, 71, 85–93; scene lines 57, 61 and 91.

The scene makes remembered divine life powerfully attractive. The ending then simplifies the contest: the maker grants greater access and healing at the price of permanent coercion; plurality preserves the others' freedom and costs Blaise loneliness, fewer journeys and some pleasures. Those are real costs, but the structure risks settling into the conspicuously correct choice against universal domination. The orchard's “difficult movement in the water” remains unspecified, while the strongest claim about adequate knowledge of essence is described as a future argument rather than materially affecting the final alternatives. The intrinsically metaphysical question of which Blaise owns or continues the remembered life risks shrinking into a lesson about allowing others to leave.

**Repair:** Carry the opening's problem of genuine first-person continuity into the ending as an actual difference, not only a feeling. Determine what kind of identity or intelligibility the restored continuation achieves that the plurality really cannot, and what the latter positively achieves beyond revocability. Give one concrete orchard discovery enough substance that characters can argue over what they have understood and what their final act would preserve or destroy. A serious claim about one necessary order, a particular essence or the identity of understanding with the activity understood should encounter a specific success and a specific unresolved objection. Larger powers and more beneficiaries alone will not supply that philosophical pressure.

This does **not** require making forced universal consciousness Spinozist or pretending domination and refusal are morally indistinguishable. One substance is not one commanding person. Erasmus can misidentify a real achievement, Hypatia can make a strong immanent claim that Blaise has grounds to contest, and Blaise can value an existence without having proved its adequacy. The ending can remain ethically unequal while presenting an irreducible metaphysical loss and a difficult positive alternative. No cosmic discovery needs to become a relationship lesson.
