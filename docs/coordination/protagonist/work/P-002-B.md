# B. Before he gets here

P-002 proposal, not canon. This is a different connected two-scene construction for the same gap as A, after BLAISE-ALLOWED and before B-E01. It lets Blaise pursue a second self as a possible witness, then discover that he wants an unequal encounter. It introduces no actual second Blaise and no new return rule. Do not install A and B together.

## B1. The chair between them

The woman by the bench has taken her dry shoe off again. Something inside it is folded under her heel. She puts a finger beneath the insole, pulls it straight, and tries the shoe once more.

The other Miriam is watching from the chair beside her coat.

“It does that,” she says.

“Yes.”

“Push it down at the back.”

“I am.”

“All right.”

You want the exchange to go on. There is no question you need answered by it. They have come to a place where one of them is annoyed about a shoe, and the other has made the ordinary mistake of helping before being asked.

You move the sound chair closer to hear. One leg catches in the grout. Both women look at you.

“Sorry.”

“Who is that for?” asks the woman with the coat.

“Me.”

She looks at the space you have chosen, between their chairs.

You leave it farther back and sit. The front of the seat presses behind your knees. You put both feet on the floor. There is the room, with two places where the same person ought to be, and each has room to lift a cup without striking the other.

You close your eyes for a moment. You can hear a sleeve move, water in the pan from the workshop, Simon opening and shutting a drawer. Neither woman speaks. You do not ask her to.

When you open your eyes, the Miriam without the coat has got her shoe on. The other has finished the water left in her cup. Both things happened while you were occupied with something else. You feel an unreasonable affection for the room.

“I want him to come,” you say.

The woman by the bench waits for the rest.

“Another one of me.”

Simon has come from the workshop with a ruler. He stops beside the end of the bench.

“There isn't evidence that...”

“I know. I didn't say he would.”

You look at the empty space beside your chair. Your other foot could be there. You could hear your own account without having to provide the voice.

“For once somebody would know what I'm talking about before I tried to explain it.”

The Miriam by the bench takes her foot out of the shoe she has just adjusted.

“What if he doesn't want to talk about it?”

“He would.”

She looks up.

“I would,” you say.

“You'd be very busy deciding which question came first,” says the woman beside the coat.

The warmth you felt goes out of the room. She has said it with an ease that makes the next thing you say feel used already.

“You don't know that.”

“No.”

You get up. If you stay in the chair, she will be able to read the rest of you from the way you are sitting.

“I only thought,” she says, then stops.

“What?”

“I was going to explain why I thought it. I don't know whether you want that.”

### B1 choice: what to do with her anticipation

**B1a. “Ask her how she reached the thought, and sit down to hear it.”**

“You kept looking at my hands,” she says. “After I took my coat off. I asked you to stop.”

“I was trying to understand what had happened.”

“Yes. I think you wanted the woman on the bench to fit beside me.”

“I haven't asked you to be the same.”

“I didn't say you did. I think you'd ask him where he had been. Before asking what he wanted.”

The Miriam by the bench puts her shoe down.

“I would too,” she says. “I did.”

“And did I want to answer?”

“You did answer.”

“That's true.”

They leave the difference there. You sit again, farther back than before. It is pleasant to have somewhere to put your weight. You dislike that she can see how relieved you are to stop standing.

“You might have guessed right,” you say.

She takes the empty cup between her palms. “I might.”

**B1b. “Ask her to let you finish before she tells you what you would do.”**

“All right.”

She waits. The first sentence you meant to say has gone. In its place is a choice between producing what she expects and producing something else for that reason.

“I might ask him whether he's hungry.”

The other Miriam says, “I am.”

You look at her. She has one shoe on and one beside her, and has apparently grown tired of discussing an absent man.

“I'll get something,” you say.

“Ask Ada what's there. Don't bring the pan out again.”

You go to the workshop. There is cheese. Ada puts it on a plate while you cut bread. She asks whether Simon has found the ruler. You say he has. She asks you to tell him not to leave it on the wet part of the bench.

You return carrying enough for everyone. Simon takes his piece and puts the ruler in his pocket. For a while the conversation cannot happen as you expected because two people are chewing.

### Common

“Would he remember wanting to meet me?” you ask Simon.

“If the memories extended that far.”

“Then he might know why I'd be frightened.”

“Yes.”

“Before I frightened him.”

Simon looks at the two women. “That wouldn't tell us how either of you would behave.”

The Miriam beside the coat starts folding one sleeve inside the other. She stops when the dressing catches in the cloth and frees it with her thumb. You bend toward her without deciding to. She lifts the sleeve clear before you reach it.

“I can do this bit.”

You draw your hand back. She finishes folding.

“You both want to stay,” you say.

“Here?”

“Alive.”

“Yes,” says the woman by the bench, sharply enough to startle Simon.

“I wasn't asking you to...”

“I know. Yes.”

The other woman has put the coat down. She says yes too, without looking at either of you. You cannot make the word into an explanation of which one should have arrived.

### B2 choice: the request he could regret

**B2a. “Ask the woman who remembers Sunday to speak to you first if another Blaise ever arrives.”**

You ask her plainly. You hear the request reach Simon before it reaches her. He looks away.

“First about what?” she asks.

“Before you decide what to do.”

“I can speak to whoever asks me something.”

“I mean about him.”

She puts both feet beneath her chair. “Would he have to ask you before speaking to me?”

“No.”

“Then don't ask me to wait for you.”

The woman by the bench has stopped working at her shoe. She is looking at the space where you first tried to put your chair.

You can withdraw the request. It will still be something you asked in front of her.

“I wanted to be sure there was somebody I could come back to,” you say.

“You can come and talk to me,” the woman beside the coat answers. “I won't help you get in front of somebody else.”

You nod. She does not offer to make the refusal smaller.

**B2b. “Say you want another man to remember with you, and are afraid you would ask everyone to prefer you.”**

“I don't want him to be the better one,” you say.

Simon looks at you. “Better how?”

You had hoped everyone would understand without asking.

“Easier to be with. Glad to be here. Not asking this.”

The Miriam by the bench looks toward the other woman's coat. “I thought she'd have it worked out because she'd had longer.”

“I was under some wet laundry,” the other says. “I wasn't improving.”

You laugh too loudly. It sounds as though she has been waiting to rescue the conversation for you.

“Sorry.”

“It was funny.”

“I know.”

She rubs at the damp place on her sleeve. You can see her attending to where the cloth touches her arm. The other woman looks down at her own sleeve, then stops herself. You have no claim to either gesture.

### Common

Simon asks whether he may take the wooden strips back to the workshop. Both women tell him he can do what he likes with the wood. The second answer catches the first. He smiles without asking them to do it again.

The Miriam with the coat follows him to ask for a clean cloth. The other stays beside the bench. You reach for your chair.

“Leave that,” she says. “I've got somewhere I want to put my foot.”

You leave it. She puts her dry heel on the front edge of the seat. The shoe is finally staying on. The woman with the coat comes back carrying a clean cloth. You pass her on your way into the workshop and shut the door behind you.

## B3. The man who would ask

Ada is looking for the end of the tape. The roll turns in her hand, then back again. She presses her thumbnail across it without looking at the service door.

“Do you think I'm going to try it again?” you ask.

“I think you're going to ask everyone until somebody says what you want.”

The answer is so quick you want to take something off the table and drop it, just to make her stop knowing what comes next. You put both hands into your pockets.

“And what do I want?”

“Couldn't tell you. You'd be finished asking.”

She finds the end of the tape and holds it free with a finger. You have been given somewhere to laugh, but you do not take it.

“I want somebody to know I was there even if I can't tell them.”

“You've got her. She remembers.”

“Some of it. I haven't asked her about the chair.”

Ada looks up. “You could.”

“I was hoping you would ask.”

Ada puts the tape down. “Why me?”

You had wanted the question to matter to her before you explained it again. You look at her wrist, then at the roll of tape.

“If God knew,” you say. “All of it. If I couldn't get in front of what he knew and spoil it.”

She waits.

“Then I wouldn't have to go looking for a man who was me.”

“Would you like being known like that?”

“I don't know.”

You can still hear Miriam guessing the order of your questions. You had been ready to leave the room to get ahead of her sentence.

### B3 choice: what to do with the chair witness

**B3a. “Bring the woman who saw the contact, and let her tell Ada without supplying the words.”**

You ask Miriam whether she will come back to the workshop for that. She asks what you want her to say. You say only what she saw from the entrance.

She describes the chair catching your knee, Ada stopping it, the hand against your wrist. She says she could not clearly hear the words. You do not tell her what they were.

Ada rubs the side of her own wrist.

“I don't remember doing that,” she says.

“I know,” Miriam answers.

You wait. Ada looks at you; you are waiting for her to do something to make the account hers.

“Thank you,” you tell Miriam.

She nods and returns to the concourse. You shut the door after her. Ada takes up the tape again. You want to ask her to put it down. You let her finish finding the edge a second time.

**B3b. “Leave the witness account for the table; tell Ada what you said to the woman who waited outside.”**

If B2a was chosen:

You tell her you asked Miriam to speak to you first if another man came. You repeat the refusal without improving your request.

“You're allowed to be frightened of him,” Ada says.

“I know.”

“Do you?”

You take your hands out of your pockets. You had made fists without noticing.

If B2b was chosen:

You tell her you admitted wanting people to prefer you. You do not repeat what either woman said afterward.

“To a man you haven't met,” Ada says.

“Who might have everything I have.”

“He hasn't got this conversation.”

You look toward the entrance. “He could.”

“He hasn't.”

### Common

You lean beside the washbasin. Ada has the tape on the table, with its end turned over so it will be easy to find. Through the closed door you can hear Simon's voice. You can't make out the words. Another voice interrupts, and the two go on at once.

“You don't have to predict what I decide,” you tell Ada.

“I wasn't trying to. I wanted the questions to stop long enough to think.”

“About what?”

She looks at you.

“Sorry.”

She leans back against the table. You keep your place beside the basin. For a while you hear only the conversation in the other room.

You had wanted another man because he would be able to begin where your explanation ended. Ada has stopped answering you and remained in the room. You do not know what she is thinking. You are glad she is here. Then you want to ask whether she is glad too.

You can still ask. You let the wish stay with you until Simon comes to say they have cleared places at the table.

## Authored later consequences for B

### B-E01-A, immediately after the hypothetical-other-man question and Ada's “Don't”

If B2a was chosen:

The woman at the sink looks at you. “I meant what I said before. You can talk to me. So could he.”

The other Miriam moves the peas farther from the table's edge. You had asked for a place ahead of an absent man while sitting between two women who could hear you. You pull your chair in so she can pass it.

If B2b was chosen:

“I know you might not want him here,” Simon says. “You've told us.”

You are grateful, then annoyed that your fear has become something he can recognize without asking. You leave both feelings there while the conversation continues.

### B-E02, after “I think it's the reason you have.”

You say you had also wanted to find another man who remembered the crossing. Simon waits while you correct that. You wanted him to come here, where the people you know could tell you what they saw. Nothing you have done shows that walking into the yard will produce him.

“I know,” you say. “I still want to see what happens.”

Ada says she wants to hear your decision, then goes back to the table. You let her go. The two route choices remain.

### B-E03, after “The hand giving it is no steadier.”

The Miriam who waited outside asks you to take her coat while she helps with the dressing. You hold it over your arm. There is a private pocket against your sleeve. You leave it closed.

When she asks for the coat back you give it to her. You would have liked her to need you for longer. She puts it on and asks the other woman whether that strip of tape is pulling.

### B-E04, before the exterior woman says where she will wait

If B2a was chosen:

“I'll tell whoever comes to the door what I saw,” she says. “I haven't agreed to choose between people.”

You say yes. Simon puts the camera down while she decides where she will stand.

If B2b was chosen:

You ask her whether she is willing to give the account again if someone comes to the entrance asking for it.

“I can tell him what I saw. I don't know what else he'll need.”

You say yes. Simon puts the camera down while she decides where she will stand.

### B-E05, after she reports the disclosed risks and before the inherited optional-offer callback

“Did you expect it to be me?” you ask.

She looks at the entrance handle, then at your other hand.

“I didn't know who would open it.”

“Are you going to ask?”

“You've told me you went through.”

You want her to have recognized you before you spoke. You want what you have said to matter. She waits while you decide which question to ask next.

You look at the room behind you. There is only the one man you can see by the glass, and he is Simon. You have come back without the companion you imagined.
