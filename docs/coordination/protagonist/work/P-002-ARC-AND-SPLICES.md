## The weakness and proposed change

The strongest existing turn is the first return: Blaise tries an irrelevant word, a held handle, and the chair again. His body makes the inquiries inseparable from his fear. B-R04's two-person encounter then offers both the support he wanted and the prospect that first-person continuity cannot reserve a unique place for him. But between that discovery and B-E02, he largely requests distinctions from other people. The plot acquires a second Miriam more decisively than Blaise acquires something new to lose.

B-E02 currently asks whether he will risk the present for the woman at the earlier chair. Give him a particular present encounter he has caused, enjoyed, and partly spoiled first. His choice then concerns an experienced attachment, not only the sensible proposition that present people matter. An uncaused act, a soul count, complete memory, another person's testimony, and being loved remain different things. The proposed arc does not solve them by making him courteous.

There is a second weakness worth challenging: “There was someone who knew what I meant” overclaims the original chair episode. Its actual words are “There you are”; neither the scene nor Miriam's distant observation establishes uniquely intimate understanding. A now makes Blaise confront that inflation. The loss made the ordinary kindness seem more complete than he knew it to be. This is one interpretation he acknowledges, not a diagnosis or a declaration that the encounter was unreal. The independent witness still corroborates it.
