# L-002 motif, name and voice map

Companion to `L002-CRITIQUE.md`, same input commit/hashes. Author-facing proposals only. The map identifies repetitions worth developing, not instructions to give every object symbolic significance.

## Consequential repetitions

| Motif | First actual encounter | Later transformation | Revision limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Anticipated sound | Arrival: Blaise knows the room by wet feet, chairs and returned voices. Recording: Simon answers the request he expected. Sunday's choir breath lets Ada recognize a particular singer in a shared activity. | First return separates the inward familiar voice from Simon's located utterance. B-R04 supplies simultaneous replies; B-E01 makes the second breath visible. Proposed singer scene changes Blaise's listening from familiarity to attempted monitoring. | Distinguish remembered, expected, recorded and currently heard sound. Do not make all anticipations supernatural. The others sometimes mishear because water is loud or a wall intervenes. |
| Touch and refusal | Ada removes Miriam's sock; the women steady a bench. Sunday's chair catches Blaise, Ada steadies him and says “There you are.” | B-R03's manufactured fall fails to reproduce contact; requested touch can occur without the desired recognition. B-E02-B offers company rather than reenactment. The two Miriams later help with a dressing. | The temperature difference is experienced, not a thermometer of identity or affection. Preserve Ada's purposes and refusals. No required romance or healing arc. |
| Mirror and water | The mirror is Blaise's borrowed object, broken by a soluble chain of physical causes. A stripe persists in the open optical model under discoverable conditions. The rabbit makes the image ungainly. | Cardboard removes the reflection just before carried weight disappears. After return the same frame cannot authenticate a person. Co-presence eventually gives the identification problem a form that is not an image at all. | Do not make the optical effect secretly magical, or the broken mirror the proved recurrence cause. Water can just be water in a pan or on a sock. “The self is a reflection” would close down the harder problem. |
| Weight and resistance | `private_account`: the door is heavier when carried than it looked while moving. `release`: the broom bends against an obstruction that the working latch cannot remove. | The parcel's absent weight and the entrance closer's continuing pull localize the return. The sound chair, bodily pressure and exterior step in B-E03 make the later choice something a body does. | Do not claim these forces prove an immaterial bearer. The earlier door-weight line is route-dependent; the common crossing cannot say every player remembers it. |
| Position, address and being seen | Simon puts another person in the arrangement intended to produce his sight. His childhood chair story exposes the difference between making and presenting. | Blaise's chair experiment repeats the wish to arrange someone else's response. Co-presence makes where he stands determine whom a name can address. Coat removal defeats a convenient marker. B-E03 ends with making room for a passerby. | Keep the parallel implicit. Blaise is not simply “the new Simon,” and Simon's preventable injury is not equated with every anxious request. The final movement is valuable precisely because nobody explains it as ethical progress. |

The image system is strongest when a detail has a current use, then changes the reader's account of an earlier action. The tap once exposed a listener; in the proposal Blaise wants it quiet enough to preserve a voice. The chairs once let people sit and sing; he later turns one into an instrument for recovering contact. These transformations do not require a narrator to announce that memory is a room, love is a door or life is water.

## Ensemble differentiation by action and listener

| Voice | What the strong passages actually do | Pressure at this revision | Specific next handling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Blaise | Notices perceptual lag, strain, mouth, wrist, where someone stands. Under metaphysical pressure his words expose desire before he can defend it: “allowed,” “the one I'm looking for.” He can be embarrassed, sharp and unfair. | Early investigation repeatedly uses impersonal qualifying language. Later, “I know” can become his universal exit from discomfort. | Carry a few bodily attentions through first-night scenes; let him ask an unsatisfactory question and receive an inconvenient answer. Vary between speaking and withheld speech, without assigning a diagnosis. |
| Ada | Sees whether an action works; admits her own omission. With Miriam she can speak at length about forty minutes each way and a kindness that may expire. Choir is a pleasure of arriving among people. | The recurrence risks making her a dispenser of sane boundaries or a woman available to keep Blaise coherent. | Give her unfinished activity, appetite and a mediocre stretch of singing. Let a practical answer miss Blaise's intended question. Preserve her long intimate sentence, not only “For doing what?” |
| Simon | Defends the part of his work he thinks others will dismiss; narrows a term, watches his listener, tries a joke with Ada and waits for it to land. His challenge to Miriam is serious. | Too many correct cautions about experimental limits can turn him into the author's method supervisor. B-E02's “the reason you have” risks sounding like a therapist. | Keep his wish to find out and his disappointment at not knowing. Let him be competent at a small task and unhelpfully specific at another. A failed suggestion need not become a maxim. The proposed bread instruction gives him a modest instance of both. |
| Miriam before recurrence | Controls the invitation to her experience, pursues a substantial argument, takes pleasure in making Ada snort, remembers absurdly persistent lemon. Her pressed variant closes plainly. | Eloquence becomes too continuously available. Long reasoning and the ability to care are at risk of being treated as a single settled temperament. | Preserve all three voice anchors. Alternate argued conviction with reluctance to speak, annoyance, enjoyment, or an incomplete offer, as the relationship warrants. |
| Miriam retained from outside | Has had the later day; can corroborate the gesture but not its exact words. Rejects recording and refuses to give her day away. | Being the useful memory-bearing woman can become her whole dramatic role. | Let her want bread, quiet, space, or help that does not advance Blaise's inquiry. In the proposal she gives unwanted sock advice and then honors a request to sit close. Her confidence is situated, not a permanent superior trait. |
| Miriam renewed inside | Has the fresh injury and her own unborrowed first-person present. Wants to call somebody and resists being examined as an exhibit. | Too easily assigned the status of the weaker, ignorant one who must accept the other's account. | Let her know how her sock comes off, be annoyed when answered for, initiate proximity and withhold the call for her own unspoken reason. She can understand the same arguments without having the same later day. |

The two women should often be difficult to distinguish **by syntax alone**. They share a substantial history. Location, listener, remembered occasion and present desire should do much of the differentiating work. That is a designed reading difficulty; giving one a new verbal tic would evade it. Keep physical staging clear enough that the reader can recover who spoke.

Transfer check, performed by this reviewer with attribution already known: “I don't have an honest way of measuring the difference now” could be Miriam's qualification in isolation; as Simon's answer about his own diverted attention it protects a different vulnerability. “I don't want a lift because you nearly got hurt” would require changing Simon's relation to the listener and his experience of temporary care before it fit him. The proposed “I was telling him” belongs plausibly to both Miriams; the force changes because one is helping and one is currently being helped. These are contextual judgments, not a blind identification score.

## Deliberate naming: test the performed work, keep the names

The following are the selected intentions recorded in `NAMES.md`; no new provenance claim or name change is proposed.

| Name/source pair | Work it currently does | Where it can become too explicit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blaise Pascal + Leopold Bloom | A vulnerable embodied consciousness wants an opportunity to be addressed to him; ordinary sensation resists turning him into an abstract spectator. | Pascal is more audible than Bloom once the God argument begins. Correct by giving Blaise a continuous bodily day, not by adding Joyce imitation, biography or a wager. |
| Ada Lovelace + Jo March | Technical competence coexists with musical pleasure and a life not exhausted by keeping other people's projects viable. | An endless sequence of firm, practical refusals would shrink the intended double source into a type. The choir breath must remain hers before it becomes Blaise's test. |
| Simon Stevin + Sibyl Vane | Water/light inquiry and the desire to control an audience collide with a living person's request. The sister/chair scene gives that failure an intimate parallel. | Avoid making his surname a verdict of vanity or a fate borrowed from Wilde. Let his investigation survive the public correction. |
| Miriam Makeba + Lionel Verney | Publicly heard voice can still withhold private life; retained witness does not restore every lost attachment. Two bearers of the same name complicate address. | “Survivor” must not certify the exterior woman's sole authenticity. Neither literary survival nor historical public voice explains duplication. The renewed woman may insist on the same name. |
| Emmy Noether + Sibyl Vane; Ruth Asawa + Mary Garth | The naming sheet promises invariants/changed presentation, and embodied participation/refusal. | Their expansion scenes were outside this bounded route review. These intentions are not proof of performed ensemble differentiation. Do not use this report to certify those scenes or import their jobs as Blaise's central stakes. |

Historical naming and philosophical sources can deepen a second reading. The protagonist's fear, the material case and the women's right to answer must work for a player who recognizes none of them.
