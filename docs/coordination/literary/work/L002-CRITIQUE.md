## Prioritized findings and performed alternatives

### L-01. Give Blaise a continued body during the first-night inquiry

**High priority.** `MANUSCRIPT.md:31–35` gives him an excellent entrance: his attention remains with his mouth in the rabbit-framed mirror, familiar sounds establish a personal relation to the bath, and he checks his empty hands. In `recording` at line 386, `release` at 354, `finding_notebook` at 1081 and `report` at 802–804, the narration repeatedly becomes an account of what the evidence does and does not establish. Individually these limitations matter. Read in sequence, their speaker could be the notebook rather than this man. The consequence is an abrupt shift from an almost impersonal investigator to the distressed first-person ownership of the loop.

Keep the distinctions in the sources/proof feedback, and keep one necessary statement in the scene. Replace selected repetitions with Blaise's attention doing something. For `recording`, after “I answered the thing I expected,” try this replacement for the two expository closing paragraphs:

> You move the clip back to the first request. Before the sound begins, Simon draws breath beside you. On the screen he is still standing with his back to the cabinet.
>
> “Leave it there a moment,” you say.
>
> He takes his hand away from the phone. You can see the cord across the door and the cloth moving. Her face is hidden. You had been waiting to see whether she looked frightened.
>
> You play it. The request is clear. Simon is quiet beside you while his recorded voice asks her to wait.

This preserves the distinction between an audible request and an unseen face, seeds anticipation without an impossible event, and allows discomfort to remain unannounced. The film already shows cloth movement; the proposal adds no image of Miriam inside. Do not acquire new factual evidence from Blaise's imagined view of her fear.

For `report`, retain the actual selected witness set and the bounded causal account. Cut the sentence “You do not add another source merely because it would make the account sound more complete” from spoken-scene narration. It explains implementation discipline, not an action Blaise has been tempted to take. Do not cut the distinction between acknowledged intention and demonstrated action.
