An alternative second-traversal middle scene, not canon. Intended placement: after B-R06 and BLAISE-ALLOWED, before B-E01. It develops the missing interval in which Blaise lives with co-presence rather than immediately discussing its ultimate meaning. The exterior Miriam starts on the sound chair near the bench, coat folded over her knees. The interior Miriam is on the bench, with the peas and wet sock. Simon and Blaise enter from the workshop; Ada is at the workshop sink. The marked/unmarked strips and prediction remain on the workbench. The camera stays off and acquires nothing. ## Proposed passage

Miriam has put her phone on the bench beside the peas. She picks it up, sees the damp rectangle beneath it, and moves it a little farther away.

“I want to make that call,” she says.

The woman with the coat looks toward the changing passage.

“It's quiet down there.”

“I know.”

“Of course you do.”

Neither moves. Simon is carrying a piece of cardboard with a ragged edge. He has been trying to fold the edge over so it will lie flat. He looks at the phone, then at you.

“I'll go in here,” he says. He means the workshop. You follow him as far as the doorway.

The woman on the bench turns the phone over in her hand.

“Not yet. In a bit.”

The other woman adjusts the coat on her knees. “I could stay out of the way.”

“I don't want you to.”

She says it quickly enough that you look back. The phone is dark. She is rubbing its edge with her thumb, trying to get the damp out of the case.

From the workshop comes the sound of water striking the saucepan. Ada has something stuck to the bottom. She loosens it with the edge of a spoon, stops, and holds it under the tap again. You can see her forearm and the back of her head. Beyond her, Simon clears a place for the cardboard.

“Is there any bread?” asks the woman with the coat.

Ada calls that there is, if Simon hasn't put glass on it.

“I haven't put glass on the bread.”

“You've put it on everything else.”

He lifts a clean towel from the far end of the workbench. The loaf is beneath it. He looks pleased to have found it exactly where it should be.

You offer to cut some. He gives you the knife handle first.

The bench is still visible through the doorway, but the woman with the folded coat is behind the jamb. You move a little to the left. Now you can see her knee. You have brought the knife with you.

“Here,” Simon says, tapping the board.

You go back. The first slice is too thick. He puts his palm against the cut face to steady the loaf and takes it away when you lower the knife.

“Saw it.”

“I am.”

He leaves you to it. The knife has entered at an angle; by the time you reach the bottom, the slice has almost disappeared. Simon eats the thin end that falls off. He does not offer another instruction.

You put two pieces on a plate. When you carry it out, both women are where you left them. You notice this before you notice that the one on the bench has put her phone away.

“Thank you,” says the woman with the coat. She takes the thick slice.

The other shakes her head. “Not with this taste in my mouth.”

“Water?”

“No. Thank you.”

You stand holding the plate. The woman eating looks at the piece you have left.

“I'll have that as well, if nobody wants it.”

You let her take it. For a little while she is occupied with keeping crumbs off the coat. When one falls into a fold she picks it out and eats it.

The woman on the bench has begun pulling at the heel of her wet sock. It gives a little and stops. She cannot keep her foot raised and catch the heel at the same time.

“Could you?” she asks you.

You put the plate down. She lifts her foot toward you. You take hold of the sock at the heel, careful to use both hands. The elastic rolls beneath your fingers.

“Not like that. It'll go tight.”

You loosen your grip. She lowers her foot.

The other Miriam has leaned forward. “Pull it down first.”

“I know how it comes off.”

“I was telling him.”

“I was telling him.”

The woman with the coat sits back. She folds the remaining bread in half and takes a bite too large to answer through.

You kneel again. This time you put your fingers inside the wet heel and ease it down before pulling. Miriam's foot comes free. There is a pale ridge around her ankle. You hold the sock loosely, as if she might still feel what you do to it.

“Over the rail,” she says.

You hang it there. She puts her bare foot on the dry part of the towel beside her shoe. The other woman swallows.

“I hate somebody doing it for me too.”

“It wasn't that.”

“All right.”

She eats the rest of the bread. The woman on the bench watches her for a moment, then puts out her good hand for the empty plate.

Ada has turned the tap down. You can hear her singing through the remaining water, a few notes that do not seem to be the beginning of anything. She stops to work the spoon against the pan. Simon asks whether she wants him to hold it. She says it needs soaking and puts it down.

She begins again. You wait for the catch in her breath. The water makes it difficult to hear.

### If the earlier Ada–Miriam towel conversation was actually encountered

You remember Miriam saying she liked hearing Ada from the kitchen. Ada had known because the tap stopped. You look toward the sink, expecting the same small silence to come from it. The water continues.

### Common

The woman who ate the bread closes her eyes. The other Miriam watches the workshop door.

“She'll stop if you go in,” says the woman with her eyes closed.

“I'm not going in.”

“I meant him.”

You have moved without deciding where you were going. You stop with one foot beside the plate. From here you can hear Ada and see both women. Ada herself is behind the wall.

The voice rises, becomes uncertain, tries the note again. Simon says something you cannot distinguish. Ada laughs and the singing breaks off.

You want to know what he said. You want to have been in the room before he said it. The laugh is already something they have had without you.

### Choice L-002-S1

- **Go to the doorway and ask Ada to keep talking while you stand outside it.**

  She looks up from the pan.

  “About what?”

  “Anything. You were singing.”

  “Not very well.”

  You tell her you want to hear her while you cannot see her.

  She puts the spoon down. Simon is beside the board with the knife in his hand. He lays it flat.

  “Are you going somewhere?” she asks.

  “Just here.”

  You step back until the jamb hides her face. You can still see the hand resting beside the saucepan.

  “Blaise, come back a bit. I can't hear you from there.”

  You return to the doorway.

  “I haven't said anything.”

  “You asked me something. I don't know what it is yet.”

  You try to explain. She waits through the part about knowing where she is. When you reach the part about keeping her there, you stop.

  “I won't be long,” she says. “I've got this to do.”

  She turns the tap up. You remain in the doorway until she asks you to take the clean plate back.

- **Stay by the bench and listen without asking her to continue.**

  Ada sings the uncertain part once more. You think the next note will go down. It goes up. You do not know whether she has got it right.

  You look at the woman with the folded coat. Her eyes are open now.

  “Do you know this one?” you ask.

  “Bits.”

  You want to ask which bits. She looks toward the workshop and you let the question wait.

  Simon begins a sentence. Ada answers over the top of it. The tap comes on fully and takes the words away. You move your foot, then put it back. The woman beside you is brushing the last crumbs from her coat into the plate.

### Common

The Miriam on the bench asks the other whether she could sit beside her. The woman with the coat gets up. She starts to lift the chair with her good hand, then leaves it where it is and takes the place on the bench instead.

“Here?”

“Yes.”

Their shoulders almost touch. The coat has slipped between them. They both try to lift it, then the one who brought it lets go.

You had kept the sound chair in view. Without her on it, you have to find her again. The two women are looking at a loose thread in the coat lining. One holds the cloth; the other tries to pinch the thread free. Neither notices when you take the empty chair.

Ada comes out carrying the saucepan. You stand up too quickly. She stops to avoid you and a little water runs over the lip onto her hand.

“Stay there.”

You sit. She puts the pan on the tiles, out of the way of your feet. It is full of cloudy water.

“Have you eaten?”

You look at the empty plate on the bench.

“I cut it.”

“That's not what I asked.”

Simon comes to the doorway with another piece of bread. You hold out your hand before he reaches you. He puts it in your palm, warm from his fingers on one side.

You bite through the crust. It makes more noise in your head than Ada's voice did from the other room. She is asking Simon where the clean cloths have gone. He goes back to look.

The two women have found scissors for the loose thread in the small sewing tin Ada brought from the cupboard. One holds the lining flat. The other asks her to move a finger.

You turn toward them. Behind you, Ada picks up the pan. You hear the water shift before she takes a step.

## Integration and self-review

This proposal turns the original listening scene three ways: knowing a singer by her breath becomes a wish to monitor Ada; the remembered chair/pan arrangement recurs without reproducing the wrist event; Miriam's dry-foot exchange becomes awkward help across two presently different wants. No sentence grants Blaise the unheard contents of the kitchen conversation or the women's minds. The two women keep the same verbal habits, including mistakenly answering for one another, while making different immediate requests. Their final proximity is requested by the injured woman, not arranged for an observation.

The scissors/tin are proposed ordinary props, not evidence; they can be replaced with an already established repair item at integration. They introduce no mark, test, wound or source. The wet sock comes off here, so B-E03's later wet-sock-removal beat must be replaced with simply putting on the shoe if this scene is accepted. Do not install both as if the sock were still on. B-E01's peas, positions and coat need their ordinary move to the table; neither Miriam leaves the building.

Both choices rejoin with no difference to the recurrence mechanism, doctrine, evidence or ending access. The first gives Ada knowledge of this particular request; the second does not. Both preserve Blaise's uncertainty. The ordinary-pleasure interval is longer than current scene practice; test its pace inside a complete route. This is a self-reviewed proposal, not an independent or human reading. If the food and sock echoes crowd the singer's scene, retain the listening choice and the women's requested move together; protect the existing first-night sock passage rather than cutting it to accommodate this addition.
