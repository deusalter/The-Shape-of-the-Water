## Kant: a condition of an experiment and a condition of experience

**Primary doctrine inspected.** The *Critique of Pure Reason*, Meiklejohn, retained lines 2248–2340, distinguishes the empirical reality of space from its transcendental ideality and argues that representing succession and coexistence presupposes time. The claim is not that instruments introduce distortions which sufficiently good instruments can subtract. The question concerns what must already be available for something to be given as an outer object, or for its positions and changes to be compared at all. The novel presence of two embodied women is therefore still empirical material. Calling them appearances cannot annul one of them.

The Second Analogy, 5980–6045, distinguishes the order of apprehension from an objective event sequence and explicitly denies a phenomenon's going back from a succeeding to a preceding time. This has an immediate fictional consequence: the engine may order Blaise's encounters, but it cannot label that sequence “Kantian reverse time.” The presented room could recur while external duration continues. The three screen labels, followed later by three changed labels, do not select one objective chronology. Conversely, if the lead eventually asserts literal reverse travel in one objective time, record it as a substantive fictional challenge to this passage, not a prediction derived from the Aesthetic.

The phenomena/noumena passage, 7310–7390, distinguishes the negative abstraction from our sensible mode of intuition from a positive object of intellectual intuition that we do not possess. A coherent phrase does not establish the real possibility or cognition of its proposed object. An unseen yard, missing parcel, hidden camera record or another person's unreported experience is consequently not a noumenon: each is a possible empirical investigation. Opening another door does not remove every condition of experience.

**Scholarly check and objection.** Nicholas F. Stang's SEP *Kant's Transcendental Idealism*, retained §4.1, 1709–1785, examines the same-object/two-aspect passages and distinguishes interpretations; §4.3.1, 1966–2020, addresses the objection that unknowability becomes trivial by definition. This checks the temptation to turn the two women into one phenomenal and one noumenal Miriam. Both are within the encountered scene. It also prevents a cheap response to Simon: saying that testing presupposes a field of experience is not a complete demonstration that its spatial relations fail to belong to things considered independently of that field. His realist question survives.

**Performed consequence.** LP-R06 gives Simon a genuine empirical success: the two pieces are separately manipulable. He continues measuring after rejecting the use of “appearance” to dismiss the object. The metaphysical disagreement does not invalidate that measurement and does not make it prove the stronger realist view. The Appendix's regulative use of ideas, 14110–14160, supports inquiry guided by unification without pretending to possess its completed object. That distinction informs the proposal, rather than functioning as a line spoken by a designated Kant character.

**Preserved repair.** The Paralogisms, 9020–9100 and 9220–9278, distinguishes the unity expressed by “I think” from cognition of a substantial, simple, enduring or separable soul, and distinguishes rational psychology from inquiry containing empirical experience. Michelle Grier's SEP *Kant's Critique of Metaphysics*, retained 530–615, checks the equivocation diagnosis and its scholarly complexity. Keep B-E01's correction: Blaise did not assert disembodiment merely by saying his recollection is his. The restriction does not prove he is unreal or make ordinary recognition wait on soul inspection.

## Spinoza: one Nature does not answer for this missing person

**Primary doctrine inspected.** *Ethics*, R. H. M. Elwes, I definitions 3–8 (within 31–80) distinguishes self-conceived substance, attributes expressing essence, and modes conceived through another. God is not merely the sum of objects; finite people are not each substances. I18 (690–710) makes God the immanent, not transitive, cause. I28 (846–888) still requires finite conditioned causes for finite occurrences. “Nature caused it” is therefore insufficient as a concrete account of the service crossing. Divine immanence gives no permission to omit the parcel, departure route or observed people.

II7/s, II13 and II18/s (1581–1645, 1800–1850, 2118–2165) make thought and extension expressions of one order, the human mind the idea of its actually existing body, and memory an association connected to bodily affections. The fiction has not inspected a complete bodily state. It must not assert that Blaise's body is wholly identical in every respect while his memory changes, then call that a demonstration of Spinoza. Ordinary visible restoration leaves hidden correlates open. Memory is not thereby worthless; remembering a mark is simply not the same achievement as explaining its causes.

IV39/s, within 6424–6505, makes organized bodily continuity consequential and allows radical change without a corpse, with the Spanish poet example, then declines to settle the full issue. It offers a serious pressure rather than a diagnostic rule that amnesia creates a new person. In LP-E04 the interior Miriam asks precisely which kind of loss Blaise anticipates. He cannot answer that by saying “may not remember” when he also cannot locate the previous interior Ada.

**Intention remains disputed.** I Appendix, 1245–1307, criticizes final causes, including the claim that God would act for something lacking and the retreat into divine will as explanation of unexplained circumstances. BLAISE-ALLOWED already lets Blaise ask whether giving can be intentional without a lack to be filled. Keep that objection and Miriam's admission that she has not disposed of it merely by rejecting an intention outside Nature. Spinoza's rejection of divine purposiveness is historical doctrine; the adequacy of that rejection is an argument characters can contest. Neither the unfortunate post-injury anchor nor fortunate recurrence proves an entire theology.

**Ethical development and its difficulty.** I D8 denies that eternity means duration, even duration without endpoints. V21–26, V34s–37 and V41–42 (8050–8135, 8260–8338, 8436–8518) connect memory to the enduring body, assert an idea expressing this body's essence under eternity, and make intellectual love an activity of God's self-love insofar explained through a particular mind. Blessedness is activity/virtue, not an external reward for choosing correctly. This is stronger than “facts remain true after death” and much stronger than generic calmness. It remains difficult to explain what individuates the eternal aspect and its relation to the finite person's development; these excerpts do not resolve that controversy.

**Scholarly check.** A fresh retrieval of Steven Nadler's SEP *Baruch Spinoza* was inspected in three selected paragraphs: immanent substance/cause, mind/body expressions, and intellectual love/freedom/eternity. Retrieval hash and paragraph indices are in scholarly-retrieval.json. Nadler supports the nonpersonal-memory interpretation and the positive account of active understanding. His “after” language is an expository aid, not grounds to convert eternity into another stretch of time. His characterization of rational benevolence does not establish that either Miriam's particular care or courage is third-kind knowledge. No cited monograph was read indirectly by reading his bibliography.

**Performed consequence.** LP-E04 lets E affirm immanence and eternity while still wanting to preserve a finite account and hear the other woman's answer. She chooses exterior custody of the paper; interior Miriam chooses where to stand and insists that a later speaker cannot inherit her decision. Their disagreement changes custody and the record's wording, not a philosophical correctness score. The all-front option lets the player preserve the current ensemble without professing either view. V41 supplies a reason not to make ethical life depend on prior knowledge of eternity; it does not command this exact action or guarantee survival.

## Fictional commitments versus unresolved claims

**Inherited L3 commitments:** co-present embodied speakers; a renewed local tableau; one observed exterior continuation; optional empty-handed recurrence. None follows from Kant or Spinoza. **New proposals:** a present open-circle intervention, and an exterior sheet presently reinspected after the optional repeat. These are author decisions offered for selection. Their source classification is explicit in the ledger.

**Unresolved:** the mechanism, exact perimeter, whole-world chronology, the numerical identity or fate of unavailable interior people, and what would happen if both Miriams waited outside during a further crossing. Calling those unresolved does not permit a contradictory local custody chain or a missing disclosure. Our finite story makes a smaller empirical claim and needs that claim's actual witnesses.

Reject these adaptations: a look beyond the doorway as noumenal vision; an immortal memory file as Spinozist eternity; independent actions as proof of uncaused freedom; two Miriams as two substances; paper as an administrative restoration of a lost person; or leaving the service door as reward for accepting one metaphysics. Each would collapse a distinction that the inspected text and the performed encounters keep open.
