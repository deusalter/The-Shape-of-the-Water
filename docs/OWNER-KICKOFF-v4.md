## 3. Substantial philosophical investigation

Read relevant primary passages, then check difficult interpretations against serious scholarship. Research must investigate arguments rather than collect summaries or quotations. Keep textual claims, scholarly interpretations, fictional adaptations, and your own proposals distinguishable.

Retain eight connected research dossiers, but replace their emphasis as follows. Roughly 600–1,000 words per dossier is a planning range, not a quota. Use cross-references to avoid repetition.

### Kant

PH-K01: Investigate space and time as forms of sensibility, the conditions of possible experience, and the relationship between transcendental idealism and empirical realism.

PH-K02: Investigate the categories, the constitution of objective experience, and the difference between an empirical discovery and an argument concerning the conditions under which objects can be experienced.

PH-K03: Investigate phenomena, things in themselves, and noumena, including negative and positive uses of noumenon. Examine competing interpretations of the appearance/thing-in-itself distinction rather than assuming a literal two-world cosmology.

PH-K04: Investigate reason's demand for the unconditioned, transcendental illusion, relevant antinomies, and the limits of speculative metaphysical knowledge. Ask how an investigator's ambition to obtain a complete explanation could be challenged without invalidating empirical inquiry.

Primary starting point: Critique of Pure Reason, especially the Transcendental Aesthetic, relevant portions of the Analytic, the phenomena/noumena discussion, and selected Dialectic passages. Groundwork is optional supporting material, not a required central research track.

Guardrails: do not equate phenomena with falsehood, noumena with undiscovered evidence, or transcendental idealism with everyone possessing a private truth. Do not present an unlockable vision of the noumenal as faithful Kantian doctrine. Distinguish conceivability from warranted knowledge. A fictional departure is possible, but identify and examine it in author notes. [K1, K2]

### Spinoza

PH-S01: Investigate substance, attributes, modes, God or Nature, immanent causation, necessity, and natura naturans/natura naturata. Examine the meaning and limitations of the label pantheism rather than using it as a slogan.

PH-S02: Investigate thought and extension, the mind/body relation, finite individuality, and kinds of knowledge. Ask what a finite person's understanding of reality can involve within this system.

PH-S03: Investigate conatus, action and passion, joy and sadness, virtue, reason, and relations with others. Trace the ethical argument's dependence on the preceding metaphysics rather than substituting generic therapeutic advice.

PH-S04: Investigate freedom, intuitive knowledge, the intellectual love of God, eternity, and blessedness, giving the culmination of Ethics V substantial attention. Examine interpretive difficulties and the relationship between understanding particular things and understanding God.

Primary starting point: Ethics I for metaphysics, II for mind and knowledge, III–IV for affects and ethical life, and V for freedom, intellectual love, and blessedness. Read selected passages in context and trace their dependencies.

Guardrails: do not reduce God or Nature to a benevolent cosmic personality, magical interconnectedness, an identity-allocation machine, or the idea that everything happens for someone's personal growth. Do not turn necessity into the claim that acting is pointless. Investigate eternity without casually equating it with endless personal survival, and blessedness without reducing it to a reward unlocked by correct answers. [S1, S2]

## 4. Turn the investigation into fiction

Before locking the full story, develop two genuinely different treatments and actual scene auditions. They must differ in dramatic construction, not simply names or atmosphere. Select through one lead writer's judgment and preserve the rejected approach with a brief reason.

For each treatment, identify the concrete case, people with immediate wants, proposed underlying history, and the encounters through which its metaphysical questions become unavoidable. Any extraordinary premise needs intelligible constraints and an effect on particular lives.

One possible question is: someone claims to have discovered reality's fundamental nature, and their life appears transformed; what would distinguish understanding from a powerful interpretation of experience? This is an optional experiment, not a mandatory plot.

Do not settle on a supernatural twist solely because the brief mentions metaphysics. Equally, do not dilute metaphysics into psychology because it is easier to write. The fiction may make original metaphysical commitments, provided they are developed coherently and distinguished from historical doctrine in author notes.

Keep separate the author's fictional commitments, established events, what characters believe, what they say, and what the player has actually encountered. Unknown metaphysical matters may remain explicitly undetermined; do not fill them with unsupported hidden facts.

The concrete mystery needs evidentially supported answers or clearly delimited remaining uncertainty. Noumenal unknowability cannot excuse an unfinished causal plot. Conversely, a solved case cannot automatically certify a total metaphysics.

Maintain narrative/PHILOSOPHY-MAP.md linking researched distinctions to actual scenes, later challenges, player actions, and consequences. A question must evolve through encounters rather than recur as the same speech. Do not force one clue per concept or one character per doctrine.

Permit sustained philosophical argument when the participants have reasons to pursue it. Avoid both lecture substitutes for scenes and the opposite rule that every difficult thought must be hidden in imagery. Removing philosopher names should not remove the inquiry, but explicit thought must still be possible.

Draft the ending encounters and disclosure structure before expanding the full middle. Do not force a neutral final lecture, doctrine-selection buttons, a philosophical correctness meter, or an ending where reality changes to flatter the player's chosen belief.
