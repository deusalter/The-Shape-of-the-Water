# Narrative production plan
## Create the literary work, do not merely describe it

The previous case bible is retired. This plan specifies how the development team creates the replacement and what must be settled before implementation. It intentionally leaves the final fictional answer to a bounded creative-development phase rather than freezing an arbitrary story before testing its voice.

## 1. Authority and outputs

One lead writer owns final canon and final prose. The coordinator manages integration and verifies that creative decisions reach the engine and tests. Independent reviewers inspect fresh versions and return findings. They do not silently edit the canonical story.

The lead writes `narrative/CANON.md`, `narrative/CHARACTERS.md`, `narrative/DISCLOSURE-MAP.md`, `narrative/VOICE.md`, `narrative/ENDING-CONTRACTS.md`, and the scene source files. These are working documents with decisions and examples, not giant theoretical systems. Use stable IDs once an item enters implementation.

`CANON.md` must distinguish locked fact, character belief, contested interpretation, and provisional idea. A draft conversation is not automatically evidence that an event happened. A clever review suggestion is not automatically canon.

Before a final story lock, execute the source investigation and comparison in docs/09-PHILOSOPHY-INVESTIGATION.md and the naming/voice auditions in docs/10-NAMING-AND-VOICES.md. Provisional scenes may develop in parallel. Add `NAMES.md` and `PHILOSOPHY-MAP.md` to the lead's canonical author documents. Keep exact current anchors in `VOICE.md`.

## 2. N0: select a human conflict

Develop two genuinely different candidate treatments, not twenty premises. Each treatment should include an immediate situation, a protagonist with a specific relationship to it, a compact place with ordinary work and pleasures, an absent or contested person/event, and a consequential action the player may eventually take.

Do not begin with a cosmological system. Begin with what someone is asking of someone else and why the request is hard to refuse. The tension must survive in an ordinary room without an introductory lecture.

Both treatments should support investigating incompatible accounts and returning to earlier material with changed understanding. Neither may use the retired administrative mechanism. Do not make a secret document the universal source of every important truth.

For each treatment, draft a 700–1,200 word dramatic encounter. These ranges guide scope; the quality of the scene controls. The alternatives must differ in actual scene design, not just voice adjectives.

The supplied audition scene is only a test of one possible conflict. It is not required canon. The lead may discard it entirely.

N0 output: two concise treatments, two scene samples, a comparison grounded in their actual lines and play possibilities, and a selected provisional direction. Continue autonomously under that decision unless the owner redirects. Never claim the owner approved it without a response.

## 3. N1: establish a voice by writing

Develop the selected encounter into a 15–25 minute minimally interactive opening. The player should meet someone with an immediate practical concern, make a meaningful choice, encounter an interpretive uncertainty, and see a consequence or a reason to revisit.

Draft two substantially different versions of a key exchange. A different version can change the relationship's starting balance, the speaker's tactic, the order in which information emerges, or the pressure of a physical task. Avoid synonym substitution as revision.

The writer then selects, rewrites, and integrates one version. A blind reviewer reads only what the player can see, not the intended themes. Ask what they think the characters want, what they believe happened, which line feels generic, where curiosity drops, and what the available choices seem to permit.

A separate dramatic reviewer reads author notes and tests whether the scene's behavior actually supports them. A line editor inspects rhythm, specificity, viewpoint, and explanatory excess. These can be sequential reviewer assignments rather than simultaneous agents.

N1 output: integrated opening, branch consequences, exact disclosure requirements, a short voice guide with real approved passages, rejected approaches with reasons, and unresolved concerns. Do not supply adjectives such as "haunting" in place of prose.

## 4. N2: lock the story contract

Before whole-game scene production, settle the following through actual fiction and a concise causal account.

### Underlying history

Write the relevant past in ordinary chronological sentences. Identify who acted, what they knew, what happened physically, who observed it, and what survives now. Distinguish author certainty from intentional uncertainty. A later ending cannot change these facts.

Every major reveal needs a source available before it becomes necessary. A testimony may corroborate a reconstruction after the player reaches someone; it cannot circularly supply the only information needed to reach that person.

### Protagonist

Establish their practical role, reason to stay, knowledge at entry, prior relationships, characteristic attention, and freedom to act. Do not use blank amnesia to avoid writing a person. Do not tell the player their character remembers something essential only after a decision that depended on it.

Define the limits of role-playing. Some history and conduct can be fixed; choices determine the present response. Avoid promising unlimited freedom that the authored story does not support.

### Major characters

Develop each through two or more actual encounters, preferably with different relationship pressures. In notes, record what they want now, what they can know, what they conceal, and how a prior shared incident affects their behavior. Do not complete a psychological matrix for its own sake.

Know where two characters' descriptions of the same incident overlap and where they conflict. Do not make every disagreement evidence of dishonesty. Establish differences in vantage, memory, emotional relevance, misunderstanding, and deliberate concealment only where the particular history supports them.

### Place

Define ordinary life sufficient for scenes: how people get food, where they gather, what work interrupts them, what they enjoy, and how the setting's recent history affects an actual room or relationship. Do not encode every world detail as a symbol or puzzle.

Fictional institutions may exist incidentally. They may not become an administrative metaphysics in disguise. The game's point of contact with power should be concrete events and people rather than a faceless form-processing mechanism.

### Interpretive returns

Identify the earlier scenes that change under later knowledge. Record their original wording, initial plausible reading, later source, revised possibilities, and any unlocked player action. The late reading must not require contradicting the earlier physical description.

Allow some changes to remain emotional rather than mechanically scored. A player who finally understands why someone avoided a chair need not receive an achievement.

### Endings

Decide which actions distinguish the endings and what immediate effects follow. Record who knows what, which promises remain, and which consequences prior choices modify. Avoid a tidy menu of three equally validated philosophies. Two or more routes suffice for the initial choice-persistence proof.

The ending should enact the result. Write the final encounter and the return to earlier material before expanding all middle scenes. This tests whether the destination is worth the setup.

N2 output: locked first story, content IDs, factual proof routes, alternative acquisition routes where mandatory material can be missed, endpoint contracts, scene inventory, and walkthroughs for the intended route families. This is the prerequisite for implementing the complete fiction.

## 5. N3: write the whole first draft

Write by dramatic sequence and relationship dependencies, not by evenly assigning one chapter to each agent. The lead controls voice. A worker can draft a bounded encounter from a complete brief, but the lead must integrate and revise it.

Each scene source identifies its entry state, disclosures, choices, repeat/revisit behavior, and exit effects. Keep author-only notes out of runtime prose. Annotate evidence-bearing lines so a later cut does not remove the sole support for a conclusion.

Create all required branch text before labeling a chapter complete. "NPC responds angrily" is a stage direction, not implemented dialogue. Endings need prose, state conditions, and downstream consequence variants.

Use a complete playable draft before extensive late-stage polish. However, do not use a complete rough draft as an excuse to skip the already required opening-quality work. The opening is the voice reference for the rest.

## 6. N4: revise in separate passes

### Causal and disclosure pass

Read the source history against every player route. Check knowledge, timing, physical possibility, clue independence, alternative explanations, and fair access. Repair the underlying event when necessary rather than adding an omniscient explanation.

### Dramatic pass

Read scenes without theme notes. Identify where someone has nothing to do, where dialogue is merely a disguised interview, where conflict is decorative, and where the player is offered an action the scene does not honor. Preserve quiet scenes that earn their place through attention or relationship.

### Character and relationship pass

Read each major character's scenes consecutively, then read important relationships across the full game. Check that later behavior responds to previous encounters. Do not erase contradiction by making everyone consistent in a simplistic way. Check whether the contradictions have causes.

### Intellectual pass

Reopen relevant primary-source passages where a scene's claim has changed. Track whether it is accurate exposition, an interpretation, a character's error, or original invention. Preserve the source-backed difference between the two central philosophers. Run the philosophical subtraction and over-suppression tests from doc 09.


For each central question, find where the attractive answer is tested by experience. Identify a genuine objection the game has avoided. Repair the scene or consequence rather than adding a lecture. A character may articulate a difficult idea when that is the act the scene needs.

### Line and rhythm pass

Read aloud where possible. Inspect point of view, sentence variety, overexplained images, abstract language, repeated cadence, and borrowed-sounding formulations. Delete a concluding interpretation and see whether the scene becomes stronger. Preserve unusual phrasing that earns its effect.

### Return and ending pass

Reread the opening after each ending. Verify that earlier words remain defensible, new significance is supported, and emotional consequences are not reduced to a final explanatory paragraph. Check that optional interpersonal failures are acknowledged rather than magically repaired.

N4 output: revised prose, change rationale for consequential edits, updated tests where facts changed, and explicit unresolved disagreements. Do not produce a numerical "literariness" score.

## 7. Originality and research

Use broad craft influences, not recognizable copied passages or a reskinned ensemble. Do not retrieve a complete commercial game's script as training material or ask agents to reproduce it. The project needs its own language, history, and situations.

Alongside the required philosophical investigation in doc 09, research concrete unfamiliar setting details when they matter to the selected setting. Use primary sources where available and retain factual notes separately from fiction. A historical fact is not automatically suitable dialogue. Do not import the researcher's academic voice into the scene.

No real-person imitation, private user biography, or Hêrte canon is required. Do not turn the owner's personal relationships into story material without an explicit request.

## 8. Source format and traceability

Store prose in reviewable text files with stable scene and block identifiers. Store branching conditions/effects in structured adjacent data or a schema-supported unified format. The authoring UI and compiler must preserve both without hand-maintained duplicate copies.

Maintain a small trace map from mandatory factual conclusions to their supporting passages. It should answer "Which scenes break if this fact changes?" It is an engineering aid, not a demand that every line have a mechanical purpose.

A style scan may report banned punctuation and candidate clichés in narrative fields. It cannot certify authorship or literary quality. Reports include exact spans and explanations; automatic replacements are forbidden. Technical prose and deliberate examples of rejected writing are outside the scan's fiction scope.

## 9. Review limits and ownership

A reviewer cannot approve their own previous draft as an independent check. Record the content hash or revision they saw. Give blind reviewers only the actual disclosure sequence. Do not leak author truth in filenames, prompt titles, or comments included in the blind packet.

An agent's simulated player reaction is not human playtesting. Mark it correctly. Request human feedback when available without freezing all useful work while waiting. No fabricated quotes, time measurements, approval, or reader counts.

At the checkpoint after two substantial revision cycles on a bounded scene, identify what remains unresolved and select the next method. Further warranted cycles remain required under doc 11; two is not a maximum. Preserve a provisional accepted baseline and do not polish the first page forever while the rest stays unwritten.

## 10. Definition of narrative completion

The whole first game is authored and playable. Required events are causally accounted for. Characters remain distinguishable without nameplates. The player can understand factual consequences without reading the design notes. The ending has actually been written. Required branches contain prose rather than instructions to write prose later.

Completion does not mean every ambiguity is explained, every character is healed, or every reader will admire the work. It means the team has made a coherent artistic attempt, revised it seriously, and delivered it honestly.

## 11. Revision 3 continuous operation

Use docs/11-CONTINUOUS-REVISION-AND-HANDOFF.md for continuing substantive passes across sessions. A two-pass checkpoint does not prohibit additional warranted revision. Change the method when repetition yields no improvement, keep the accepted baseline, and preserve exact work at handoff.
