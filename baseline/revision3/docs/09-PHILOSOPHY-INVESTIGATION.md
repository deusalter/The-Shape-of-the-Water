# Philosophy investigation and dramatic integration
## Revision 3, 3 October 2026

The owner requests substantial philosophical investigation and proposes Kant and Spinoza. Make them the central research commitments. This document contains a checked starting orientation and a production assignment, not a claim that a complete research program has already been performed. Read the selected primary passages before marking the corresponding work complete. Research must produce intellectual discoveries and alter the work, rather than decorate a fixed plot with quotations.

The story remains separate from Hêrte. No plot, protagonist, or example name becomes canon through this supplement. The existing literary-first, modest-presentation scope remains in force.

## 1. Why this pairing, and where it is insufficient

Kant's restrictions on cognition coexist with empirical realism. His account of appearances is not a license to claim that physical evidence is imaginary or that every witness's belief is equally true. His theoretical and practical projects must be distinguished. Moral autonomy is not whatever a person feels like doing. Respect for persons is particularly relevant to the way an investigator obtains cooperation. [S15, S16, S18, S19]

Spinoza puts human thought, desire, and action within nature. Necessity, adequate understanding, conatus, and the affects belong to a connected system. His denial of an absolutely undetermined will does not mean that deliberation, action, and efforts to understand have no consequences. His account of freedom must be investigated in its own terms rather than replaced with either resignation or modern self-help. [S17, S20]

Do not combine these into one harmonious doctrine. Investigate their substantive disagreement about the reach of reason, nature, and freedom. A character can exploit either vocabulary badly; the research notes must distinguish that misuse from an accurate account of the philosopher.

The pairing addresses knowledge and human agency particularly well. It is not a complete theory of practical detection. Add one tightly bounded methods note on explanatory inference, alternative hypotheses, and testing. Distinguish Peirce's hypothesis-generating abduction from later inference-to-the-best-explanation accounts. This methods note does not require adding a third philosophical faction or a new character. [S21, S22]

## 2. Research outputs and standard of depth

Produce eight connected dossiers, PH-K01 through PH-S04 below, followed by a comparative synthesis and a scene experiment. Approximately 600–1,000 words per dossier is a planning range, not a quota; some require more argument, others fewer words. Integrate recurring material by reference instead of re-explaining an entire philosophy eight times. Produce no fabricated source counts, page references, or claims of having read entire books.

Each dossier needs the following, in a compact form appropriate to the question:

- The problem and the philosopher's claim, with the reasoning that connects them. Separate textual support, scholarly interpretation, and the team's own inference.
- Primary work, translator/edition, and stable location. Use Kant A/B pagination or Academy volume/page only when actually verified; otherwise use a reliable section heading plus a text locator. For Spinoza use part, definition/proposition, and scholium/corollary where relevant.
- A serious difficulty, counterargument, or interpretive dispute. When reporting an established scholarly disagreement, cite the participating readings rather than inventing two schools.
- The attractive popular distortion and precisely why it is misleading.
- Two possible dramatic uses that do not require characters to name the philosopher. Include an explicit discussion where the argument itself could matter.
- A consequence for investigation, choice, attention, or later rereading, plus one proposed application the lead should reject as too literal, misleading, or dull.

Research files are author-only. They are not player dialogue or an encyclopedic in-game codex. Long copyright-protected excerpts are unnecessary. Keep brief quotations exact and labeled; prefer original paraphrase and source locations. Primary public-domain translations may have dated vocabulary and should not silently become the game's speech register.

Required outputs:

`research/philosophy/KANT.md`, `research/philosophy/SPINOZA.md`, `research/philosophy/COMPARISON.md`, `research/philosophy/INFERENCE-METHOD.md`, and `research/philosophy/SOURCES.json`.

While research runs in parallel, Kant source records belong in `research/philosophy/sources/KANT.json` and Spinoza records in `research/philosophy/sources/SPINOZA.json`. Each record identifies author, work, edition/translator, source location, passages actually inspected, supported claims, and unresolved limitations. The coordinator merges them into the final SOURCES.json at T25. Researchers must not concurrently edit the shared source file or one another's dossiers.

The eight dossier sections can live within the two philosopher files. Do not create dozens of tiny documents merely to imply rigor. Add new sources because an actual problem requires them, not to meet a bibliography quota.

## 3. Kant investigation

### PH-K01: conditions and limits of experience

Primary waypoints: Critique of Pure Reason, Introduction and Transcendental Aesthetic; the chapter distinguishing phenomena and noumena. Consult S15 for contested interpretations. Confirm exact locations in the selected edition. [S15, S18]

Investigate the distinction between the conditions of possible experience and differences between individual observers. Space and time are forms of intuition in this account; do not carelessly call them learned personal biases or treat them as identical to the categories of understanding.

Dramatic question: what does the investigator genuinely observe, and what explanatory structure do they add? This is an artistic application, not a claim that conflicting witness testimony demonstrates transcendental idealism.

Required restraint: an undiscovered room, a concealed weapon, or an unknown motive is not thereby a Kantian thing-in-itself. Empirical inquiry can discover previously hidden facts. Do not use noumena as a label for whatever the plot has not disclosed.

### PH-K02: causality, objective sequence, and the demand for explanation

Primary waypoints: Critique, Second Analogy and the discussion of reason's search for conditions in the Dialectic. Treat exact A/B numbers as items to verify, not citations supplied by memory. [S18]

Investigate why an objective sequence is different from a merely subjective order of perceptions. Ask what an investigator would need to distinguish an event happening before another from one causing the other. Do not pretend Kant supplies a forensic algorithm or licenses inferring a particular culprit a priori.

Scene experiment: reconstruct a small sequence from two sincere witnesses with different opportunities to observe. Preserve at least two initially plausible causal explanations and make further inquiry discriminate between them.

### PH-K03: natural causality and practical freedom

Primary waypoints: Critique, Third Antinomy and its resolution; Groundwork, Section III. [S18, S19]

Investigate the relation and tension between explaining an action causally and regarding oneself as an agent under moral demands. Do not turn Kant into a simple opponent of all causal explanation, or claim that a known causal history straightforwardly proves or disproves autonomy.

Dramatic question: can a person ask to be understood causally while insisting that their commitment or refusal has authority? Give the opposing interpretation a serious encounter rather than a paragraph of authorial dismissal.

### PH-K04: dignity, means, maxims, and moral self-description

Primary waypoints: Groundwork, Section II, especially humanity as an end and autonomy; use S16 as a scholarly orientation. [S16, S19]

Investigate the difference between treating a person as a means and merely as a means. Examine maxims rather than assuming that an attractive outcome settles a moral question. Do not reduce Kant to obeying arbitrary external rules or deny the possibility of affection accompanying action from duty.

Scene experiment: an investigator can obtain accurate evidence through a believable manipulation. Develop the attraction and the objection without making the manipulated person's hurt disappear when the investigator gets the right answer. Do not mechanically award a Kant score.

## 4. Spinoza investigation

### PH-S01: immanence, necessity, and the absence of an external exception

Primary waypoints: Ethics I, Definition 7; Propositions 15, 29, and 33; Appendix. [S20]

Investigate substance and modes sufficiently to avoid presenting one substance as a communal mind, a computer, or a magical city that allocates personal identity. Follow the critique of final causes without concluding that ordinary purposes do not exist in human explanation.

Dramatic question: what changes when an investigator includes their own attachments and interventions within the causal situation rather than imagining a position outside it? This is an artistic application, not a proof of Spinoza's metaphysics.

### PH-S02: imagination, inadequate ideas, and understanding

Primary waypoints: Ethics II, Definition 4; Propositions 35, 40 and its second scholium, 43, and 48. [S20]

Investigate the difference between a vivid image, a persuasive story, an adequate idea, and a true conclusion reached for poor reasons. Do not identify Spinozist adequacy with possessing many clues or knowing every biographical event. A finite detective's fallible empirical reconstruction is not automatically adequate knowledge in Spinoza's technical sense.

Scene experiment: one person has the accurate conclusion but cannot justify it, another has a coherent explanation that is wrong. The player must decide what further inquiry can distinguish their epistemic positions.

### PH-S03: conatus, affect, and relational contagion

Primary waypoints: Ethics III, Preface; Propositions 6–9; Proposition 11 and scholium; relevant definitions of affects; Proposition 27 where social imitation matters. [S20]

Investigate striving, changes in power of acting, and the distinction between action and passion. Do not reduce conatus to a conscious selfish goal, a game health meter, an unchanging personality trait, or a requirement that everyone seeks domination.

Dramatic question: why can a person preserve an explanation that diminishes them? Develop the material and relational conditions that sustain their conduct without claiming the philosophical concept provides a complete diagnosis.

The voice pass should use this investigation to vary how speakers are affected by one another. No mechanical mood bars need be exposed to the player.

### PH-S04: freedom through understanding and its limits

Primary waypoints: Ethics IV, Propositions 7 and 14, the scholium to 18, and the free-person sequence near 67–73; Ethics V, Propositions 3, 6, and 10. [S20]

Investigate why knowledge and passion are not simply switched by discovering a fact. Examine the relation between understanding necessity and becoming more active. Avoid the claim that telling a person why they hurt instantly frees them, or that understanding a harmful act establishes a duty to forgive it.

Scene experiment: the relevant explanation becomes known; the next conversation remains difficult. Write materially different possibilities for acting on that understanding without returning to ignorance to manufacture drama.

## 5. Comparative synthesis

Write a synthesis that preserves disagreements. Treat the following as research questions, not predetermined answers:

1. What kinds of knowledge of a person are available, and which ambitions exceed what the investigator has established?
2. What does causal understanding do to blame, resentment, and future conduct? Which inferences are warranted, and which are merely emotionally attractive?
3. How do the accounts of freedom differ? What would a cheap fictional reconciliation erase?
4. What is the difference between interpreting a person's motives and respecting their capacity to answer, refuse, or act?
5. Can an investigator use a true explanation manipulatively? Can an admirable commitment be defended with an inaccurate account?
6. What can the final factual reconstruction establish, and what must the final encounter leave to the participants?

The project may develop an original artistic position. Do not label it a scholarly conclusion or announce that the game resolves the two philosophers. An unresolved disagreement is not an excuse for an unfinished causal plot.

## 6. Research must change the game

Maintain `narrative/PHILOSOPHY-MAP.md` with rows linking a question to a source-backed distinction, actual scene IDs, initial interpretation, later pressure or objection, player action, and observable consequences. Cite textual locations in author notes, not in ordinary dialogue.

Before story lock, identify which discoveries changed the proposed human conflict, a relationship, or a choice. At the first whole-story review, identify a substantive philosophical weakness and repair it where one is found. A critic may find no new weakness; no flaw quota applies.

For each central question, require at least one consequential encounter and one later return that changes the conditions of the question. Several questions may share a scene. Do not add scenes merely to satisfy a matrix.

At least one bounded scene experiment should let two people sustain a difficult argument long enough to expose a real disagreement. It needs a conversational reason, a listener capable of resistance, and an outcome. It need not involve violence, shouting, or a clue reward. The scene may be quiet and extensive.

Apply the subtraction test: remove philosopher names, epigraphs, and explicit theme labels from the player text. Can a reader still encounter the problem? Also apply the opposite test: has the fear of exposition erased every developed thought? Repair either failure.

## 7. Blindness, truth, and ending constraints

A blind literary reader gets only the disclosed player sequence. A philosophical critic gets the research and relevant author context. Do not use one as a substitute for the other.

The same underlying event should support more than one ethically consequential response where the story warrants it. Do not make the world retrospectively alter its history to confirm whichever philosophy the player favors.

Reject philosophy-selection buttons, ideology meters, a true-ending doctrine, or a final neutral lecture summarizing that everyone is partly right. Factual support, practical choice, and emotional response stay separate.

Research continues when drafting exposes a genuine gap. It does not continue indefinitely before the first scene is written. Generic engine work and provisional auditions may proceed alongside the source investigation; story lock and full narrative expansion require the research synthesis and scene integration checks.
