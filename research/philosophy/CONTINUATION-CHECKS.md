## 1. Kant: a more complete measurement does not cross the boundary

**Primary recheck.** The Aesthetic begins with receptive sensibility and objects given through intuition, not with conflicting reports. Its spatial argument says representing objects outside and beside one another presupposes space; space cannot be derived from those already spatial relations without circularity. It concludes empirical reality alongside transcendental ideality. In §9, increased empirical clarity never by itself approaches knowledge of things considered apart from sensible conditions. The rainbow and raindrops remain distinct at the empirical level while both fall within the transcendental account of appearance.

**Consequence for the actual claim.** Simon's “subtract their defects” and invariance proposal in `gallery` deserves a real success. It may establish a stable optical relation across cameras with different defects. That success cannot alone establish transcendental realism; a failure also cannot establish transcendental idealism. The conditions of this apparatus and the conditions under which anything can be given as an outer object belong to different arguments. Do not reduce Kant to noticing that instruments have settings or people have perspectives.

**A developing encounter.** First let a shared measurement change a practical decision about rebuilding or demonstrating the instrument. Later someone asks whether the recovered relation is a property of the world independently of our manner of receiving it. A reply must do more than repeat “we always have conditions”: the Aesthetic's presupposition argument can be pressed, and Simon can ask why a condition of representation cannot also belong to represented reality. That is a real pressure, not evidence that he has failed to understand a slogan. The question may remain unsettled after both participants improve their local measurements.

**Interpretive caution.** Stang's discussion distinguishes epistemic and metaphysical dual-aspect readings and examines objections to making unknowability trivial. The game can use a limit without claiming all historical interpretation is settled. “Not a second workshop” properly rejects a literal hidden empirical room; it does not prove one selected scholarly reading by itself. A calibrated coordinate transformation is an empirical achievement, not a performed transcendental deduction.

## 2. Noumenon: a thinkable limit is not an undiscovered source

The rechecked phenomena/noumena passage distinguishes the negative abstraction from our sensible intuition from a positive object of intellectual intuition. Logical consistency is insufficient to establish an object's real possibility or cognition. Removing sensibility from a description does not leave an instrument-ready object behind it.

The expansion should let a newly found empirical condition remain discoverable. A concealed reflector, different camera crop, omitted view, or unreported rehearsal is not a noumenon. Conversely, uncertainty about things in themselves does not make any untestable story equally supported. One useful later challenge would ask Simon what his proposed unconditioned object would count as, and what warrants identifying it as this world. That challenge differs from the earlier finding that his camera has an exposure setting. The dialogue needs a reason to continue, such as the wording of a public invitation or what a sister is being asked to trust, but that reason need not reduce the argument to advertising ethics.

Kant's Appendix retains regulative unity as a productive aim. This supports actual further inquiry after an overclaim is abandoned. It is stronger than a polite compromise that everyone may keep an inspiring opinion. An inquiry can seek deeper unification while withholding a claim to have cognized the completed unconditioned whole.

## 3. Spinoza: independence, immanence and the adequacy challenge

**Primary recheck.** I D3–6 distinguishes what is in/conceived through itself from modes existing and conceived through another; I P4–5 argues against substances sharing an attribute; I P14 depends additionally on the necessary existence and infinity of God. I P18 makes God immanent cause. I P25c makes particular things determinate expressions of attributes. I P28 still requires finite causes for finite conditioned occurrences. None of this lets “Nature caused it” replace the investigation.

The current `optics` correctly leaves Miriam's inference from independence to one reality incomplete. Expansion must not silently treat her exchange about common properties as the whole argument for I P14. Simon's question why anything satisfies the definition, and his question about thought, remain live. Neither ordinary shared properties nor a finite object's spatial distinctness decides the substance argument. Thought is not an extra material component or a little agent operating the body; one substance is not a pile or a cosmic individual with preferences.

**A sharper next encounter.** II P37 says a property common to all does not constitute a particular essence. II P38–40 locates adequate common notions and distinguishes reason from intuitive knowledge proceeding from attributes to essences. A sound optical rule shared by cameras therefore does not automatically give the essence of Simon, Miriam, or the whole world. The next case work can move from possessing a rule to asking what kind of understanding of this particular situation it supplies. That opens the stronger question rather than merely adding the word “adequate.”

II P43 treats a true idea as the act of understanding rather than a lifeless picture, and gives truth its own standard. That is a substantive answer available to Miriam, not a license for “my confidence proves it.” A listener can demand the reasoning and distinguish knowing from misidentifying one's certainty as knowledge. II P45–47 makes a bold claim to finite adequate knowledge of God's essence. Do not turn Miriam into a generic limit theorist who believes all ultimate claims are only useful perspectives. Nor should a player-facing proof validator grade that metaphysical argument as it grades the cord sequence.

## 4. Ethics V: particular understanding, love and the difficult eternal aspect

IV P14–15 prevents true knowledge from automatically defeating stronger affects. V P3–4 nevertheless makes a positive claim about becoming less passive through understanding. It would be a dilution to turn this entire development into “explanations never help.” A later scene should allow a genuine increase in understanding or power to act, while other dependencies, grief and uncertainty remain. The same outward action can have different causal grounds; V P4s explicitly says desires leading to the same action can arise from adequate or inadequate ideas. Helping is consequently not an external enlightenment assay.

V P14–20 connects understanding oneself and affects with love of God, denies ordinary divine passions and reciprocal favor, and describes a shareable good. This can put pressure on Simon's desire to own the effect or Miriam's desire to have another person become like her, but neither rational cooperation nor intellectual joy requires giving away all work or relinquishing every boundary. Do not make the person who refuses a favor the philosophical loser.

V P24–29 connects understanding particulars, the third kind of knowledge and necessary dependence through God. V P32–36 develops intellectual love; V P36s explicitly distinguishes a general demonstration of dependence from its more powerful understanding through a particular essence. V P36 also identifies the mind's intellectual love with God's self-love insofar expressed through this mind under eternity. This is stronger than subjective appreciation, yet it does not restore the passionate reciprocity denied in V P17–19. “Why call it love rather than comprehension?” is an available serious objection. That is the unfinished work in `miriam_private`: the mother's dependence should not remain a moving illustration appended to the end of a substance lecture. A later practical encounter can expose what Miriam understands about another person's capacities, and what her general account has failed to tell her. Whether that encounter realizes the historical third kind must remain disputed unless the author deliberately stipulates more and marks the departure.

V P21–23 and P34s rule out treating eternity as indefinite personal memory. But the positive claim about the mind's eternal aspect is not erased by ruling out reunion. What individuates this eternal understanding when memory is excluded? V P33s calls blessedness possession of perfection, while ordinary joy is transition. The text's own explanatory fiction of acquisition does not settle the relation between timeless possession and a changing life. These are fruitful difficulties for the promised argument, not an instruction to add an afterlife subplot.

V P39 adds an uncomfortable pressure: bodily capacities are connected to intellectual power and the mind's eternal part. Miriam's teaching and care history can challenge what this valuation leaves out about a particular loved life. This is not permission for the narrator to rank injured or disabled people by moral worth. A character may reject or modify an implication; author notes should then identify the adaptation instead of claiming historical Spinoza endorses every contemporary ethical commitment. Nadler's nonpersonal interpretation checks the reunion error but does not settle all these puzzles.

V P41 allows the ethical life its force without relying on knowledge of eternity; P42 makes blessedness virtue itself rather than its reward. A final supper, public correction, returned camera or repaired friendship can matter without being the prize for a metaphysical verdict. The strongest culmination would enact a changed activity whose intelligibility remains an issue the characters have actually argued about.

## 5. Optical apparatus: constraints before new experimental results become canon

New checked physics source: Samuel J. Ling, Jeff Sanny and William Moebs, *University Physics Volume 3*, OpenStax §§1.2 and 2.1. Reading-scope records, URLs, retrieved HTML hashes and bounded excerpts are under `sources/expansion-openstax-*`. No game dialogue has been copied from them. These checks concern elementary geometrical optics, not a built prototype or a perceptual experiment.

1. **Specify what the camera sees.** A mirror's virtual image and light projected onto a diffuse screen are different. A virtual image can be photographed through a camera lens; placing paper at its apparent location does not reveal a projected image there. The current “stripe lies across the glass” can be an ordinary description of a seen reflection. A later diagnostic test must not confuse that description with light physically focused onto the glass surface.
2. **Separate invariants from detector output.** Specular reflection follows the local surface normal. The virtual source location may stay fixed while different camera positions intercept different patches, crop the line, or record different brightness and pixel coordinates. Two cameras need not produce identical images to support one geometrical account. Define “same” before interpreting agreement or disagreement.
3. **Do not manufacture infinity.** A finite object reflected by ordinary plane surfaces has finite virtual image positions under successive reflection constructions. A report of no felt distance may be honest when cues are limited; it does not establish literal infinite distance or an escape from spatial form. Suppressed cues do not guarantee that every observer loses every depth cue. No psychophysical claim has been tested here.
4. **Account for the dark field in the open model.** Once the opaque cabinet is gone, masking, backdrop, viewing direction, lighting and camera exposure still matter. The source supports reflections, not an unexplained black field in open daylight. A practical return could discover which contrast conditions the first description omitted without calling the original experience fabricated.
5. **Track the source and time.** The slit, water, mirror, camera and daylight must occupy compatible positions. Water ripples alter surface normals and can distort or redirect the image. Covering the actual light path should extinguish that contribution; moving a card elsewhere does not. If a lamp replaces fading daylight, this is a new source condition. It may make a better-controlled comparison, not an exact recreation of the earlier event.
6. **Keep the safe model distinct from the accident.** The mirror mounted on an outward-opening door and the secondary table model need separate dimensions and purposes. A ray sketch can test a proposed geometry, but cannot certify the physical apparatus or reproduce subjective depthlessness. No such certification is claimed by this research.

These constraints create investigation rather than extra vocabulary: one result can fail because an image was cropped, succeed under a predicted position change, or expose an overbroad invariance claim. None of those empirical outcomes proves or refutes either philosopher by itself.
