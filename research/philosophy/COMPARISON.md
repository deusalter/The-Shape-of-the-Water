# Comparison: what a discovered cause can, and cannot, settle

Status: coordinator's provisional comparative inquiry, grounded in selected passages actually inspected on 2026-10-03. This is an argument-development note, not a claim to have read either complete work or resolved their scholarly controversies. Cross-references: PH-K01–04 in KANT.md and PH-S01–04 in SPINOZA.md. Those dossiers carry the fuller source records.

## A common investigative scene does not make a common philosophy

Suppose an investigator establishes that a safety release was held shut before a line was cut. The before/after relation is an empirical claim supported or defeated by marks, mechanisms and testimony. It is not made private by being experienced. Kant's Aesthetic expressly affirms the empirical reality and objective validity of spatial relations while denying that space can be asserted as a determination of things considered independently of our sensibility. In the Meiklejohn Gutenberg text, the conclusion to the exposition of space also warns against explaining the ideality of space through the examples of colour or taste. An optical deception is a change within experience. Discovering its mechanism is not an experiment that demonstrates what makes experience in general possible.

The consequence for our bath story is exact. A sight which a witness mistook for the disappearance of spatial boundaries can be reproduced, explained and trusted as a real event of seeing. The argument about the status of spatiality remains a different argument. Characters must be allowed to understand this difference. Neither repairing the mechanism nor exposing the claimant's negligence disproves all his metaphysical claims by association.

Spinoza's explanation also reaches beyond an isolated sequence of events, but on significantly different grounds. Ethics I definitions 3–6, propositions 14–18 and 29 scholium situate finite things as modes of the one substance and distinguish productive Nature from what follows from it. The investigator and the machinery are not substances outside God interacting with a transcendent maker. Immanent causation is not a force that intermittently appears inside an otherwise independent world. Yet a causal diagram of the bath does not by itself derive substance monism. Its intelligibility is a dramatic place to investigate the argument, not an empirical confirmation that the argument has won.

## The contested reach of understanding

In the phenomena/noumena chapter, Kant distinguishes a thing considered apart from our sensible intuition from a positive object of intellectual intuition. The latter requires a kind of intuition we do not possess. A noncontradictory conception does not establish the real possibility of its object. We therefore cannot place an extra hidden object behind every discovered object and offer a final clue that gives direct access to it. This would turn an epistemic boundary into an unopened cupboard.

Spinoza's Ethics V.24–25 connects understanding particular things with understanding God and identifies the third kind of knowledge as proceeding from adequate knowledge of attributes to adequate knowledge of essences. His intellectual intuition must not simply be equated with the non-sensible intuition Kant denies humans. Their concepts occupy different argumentative systems. Nevertheless the disagreement is substantial: can a finite understanding have knowledge of the relation of particular things to the ultimate nature of reality, of a kind that constitutes freedom and blessedness? Kant's limitation cannot be removed merely because such understanding would transform a life; Spinoza's claim cannot be replaced with a useful personal perspective without abandoning its ambition to truth.

Stang's SEP entry makes the interpretive uncertainty on the Kantian side unavoidable: phenomenalist, epistemic dual-aspect and metaphysical dual-aspect readings disagree about what the appearance/thing-in-itself distinction commits Kant to. Nadler's Spinoza entry likewise identifies disputes over inherence, causation and the scope of Nature. These disagreements should constrain confident author notes. The game need not convert every scholarly position into a character or faction.

## Transformation is evidence of a change, not a metaphysical certificate

Spinoza's ethical argument depends on the preceding account of minds and bodies, adequate ideas, conatus and active affects. It is not the maxim that explanations make people feel better. V.24–36 gives understanding and intellectual love a connection to the nature of God, while V.41 says the ethical conclusions remain important even without knowledge of the mind's eternity. V.42 identifies blessedness with virtue itself rather than a reward after self-control. The order of dependence matters. A character who repairs damage to gain a revelation at the final screen reverses it.

A change of conduct can therefore put a claim to understanding under pressure without furnishing a diagnostic test that historical Spinoza endorses. Someone who ceases treating a person as replaceable may have become more active through understanding, may be responding to shame, or may be doing both. The fictional case can establish what they did; it cannot infer adequate intuitive knowledge directly from outward kindness. A person who denies the metaphysical account may still act with courage. The player need not select a doctrine to permit the injured person's recovery or the factual resolution.

Eternity requires similar discipline. V.21 ties memory and imagination to the body's duration; V.23 and its scholium introduce a difficult claim about the mind's eternal aspect while excluding temporal recollection as its explanation. Nadler reads this against personal immortality. The dossier preserves the difficulty rather than treating that account as an uncontested proof. An echo of a dead person's speech is not evidence that their personality survives under Spinoza's account. This is one reason a surviving-voice treatment needs more examination than an evocative premise supplies.

## Inquiry after disclosure

Kant's Dialectic appendix gives ideas a regulative employment: they direct inquiry towards unity without licensing the claim that a completed totality has been known. This supports an ending which continues investigation after the injury's causes are settled. It does not recommend intellectual resignation. Conversely, Spinoza's understanding of particulars cannot be reduced to collecting an endless inventory of local causes. Its ethical significance involves the intelligible relation of a particular essence to God or Nature, a claim the Kantian challenge keeps open to argument.

Proposed dramatic sequence: establish the cut and the timing; test the release; hear a volunteered account; revisit the sight without overwriting its first description; then confront the difference between a reconstructible event, an argument about possible experience, and an argument about immanent reality. The final practical encounter should belong to a particular relationship, with room for refusal or unfinished work. Neither a neutral concluding lecture nor a belief selector can carry this disagreement.
