## 1. Kant's time does not supply the reset mechanism

**Primary:** *Critique of Pure Reason*, Meiklejohn, Transcendental Aesthetic, Section II, §§5–8 (retained lines 2299–2538). Succession and coexistence presuppose time; different times are successive parts of one time; time conditions inner experience immediately and outer appearances mediately. Its empirical reality is not removed by denying that it characterizes things independently of our sensible conditions. The objection that changes in our representations are real receives an affirmative response about empirical reality, not permission to rearrange time by preference.

**Consequence for this proposal:** Henrietta's recollection of Sunday followed by a Saturday-looking occasion is still a successive experience. Looking at an earlier date, remembering a later one and successfully forecasting a message do not constitute an intuition outside time. They may support an extraordinary empirical finding about the relation between her recollection and the present. The evidence does not by itself settle literal return, reproduction, branching, or remote-world scope. The proposal's distinction between fictional commitment and encountered warrant is useful here.

There is a stronger historical difficulty than merely saying Kant did not predict loops. The Second Analogy explicitly denies passage of a phenomenon from a succeeding point of time to a preceding one (lines 6020–6028, within the inspected opening 5897–6040). Its distinction between inspecting a house in different orders and apprehending a ship's determinate movement concerns objective succession. If canon eventually certifies literal backward passage within the same objective time, that should be identified as a substantive fictional challenge or departure from the inspected account. Calling time a form of sensibility cannot make that difficulty disappear. Alternatively, restored calendar/apparatus states could occur in a successive order of occasions; that is a possible fictional description, not an interpretation proved by Kant.

**Specific scene experiment:** after the first successful message prediction, let Henrietta distinguish the order she experienced from the dates she reads. A participant can press whether she returned to the very same event or encountered an extraordinarily similar state. This need not prevent her taking practical precautions. A reset rule should use the world's authored observable condition, not a choice in which she denies time's reality. Anomaly does not make every account equally supported.

## 2. Third Antinomy: a different reply does not expose an unconditioned cause

**Primary newly inspected in this wave:** Third Conflict, thesis, antithesis and observations (lines 10397–10550, ending partway through the antithesis observation); complete core freedom solution, beginning with its definition of cosmological freedom and ending at its express limitation (12037–12533). These passages were not directly inspected in the original four Kant dossiers; that earlier limit remains historically accurate for the earlier reading.

The thesis demands a spontaneous causal beginning to avoid an unfinished regress; the antithesis argues that such a temporal interruption destroys the connected order of experience. These are opposed positions staged for criticism. Neither should be lifted out as Kant's final unqualified doctrine. The resolution distinguishes the same action considered empirically and intelligibly. It preserves its complete empirical connection. An intelligible character does not wake up at Sunday closing, travel to Saturday, then acquire a new determination. It is not a second person operating behind Henrietta.

The concluding qualification is decisive: the discussion has not established freedom's actual existence, nor cognized its real possibility; it establishes that the proposed nature/freedom opposition need not be a contradiction. It does not produce an empirical test for transcendental freedom. This limit does not exhaust Kant's practical philosophy. Rohlf's inspected §5.2 and the opening of §5.3 discuss stronger practical claims and their difficulties; the relevant later primary works were not newly inspected here.

**Consequence for Henrietta and Ada:** on the second occasion Henrietta remembers a conversation and selects different words. A changed memory is a changed condition. The observation is not two wholly identical causal states yielding different choices. Conversely, repeating Ada's earlier reply is not proof that her will is nothing but a programmable response. Even perfect empirical predictability would not settle the question as Kant has framed it. A failure to predict is not a demonstration of freedom either.

**Preserve disagreement:** Miriam's C1-S08 answer that understanding leaves her less at the mercy of her first idea is compatible with Spinoza's account of increased activity. It does not simply settle Kant's question about the grounding of agency. A later objection can ask whether becoming better informed within a determined sequence is enough to count as free. Miriam can defend an answer through adequate causation; another participant can question its sufficiency. Do not flatten both into “understanding gives us better choices,” or make either the philosopher's whole doctrine.

**Scholarship checked:** Michelle Grier, *Kant's Critique of Metaphysics*, §4.2, retained lines 788–858; Michael Rohlf, *Immanuel Kant*, §5.2 and opening §5.3, lines 1587–1766. Grier distinguishes dynamical resolution from the mathematical antinomies. Rohlf notes serious problems on both two-object and two-aspect accounts, including how multiple agents interact. Neither establishes a software model of noumenal agency. A/B references appearing in these secondary texts were not independently collated against a paginated primary edition, so this report uses section and local-line locators.

## 3. Memory persisting while injuries disappear: a real Spinozist pressure

**Primary:** *Ethics* I D7–8 (lines 54–66); II D5 (1449–1455); II7 with scholium (1581–1625), II13 opening (1800–1834), II17c/s–18s (2060–2171); III2 with opening scholium (3253–3278). The order and connection of ideas and things is one order under different attributes; mind and body are not separately interacting substances. Memory is an association of ideas according to bodily affections. Its being mental does not mean that its cause or preservation lies outside nature. The historical account of bodily traces is not modern neuroscience; the structural commitment remains consequential.

**Exact draft issue:** proposal table, Memory row, originally says “Her bodily condition returns to the anchor's condition” while autobiographical memory and affect persist. If this means an exactly identical total bodily state, rather than the observed absence of a bruise and fatigue, it conflicts with the parallel account the story's Miriam defends. V21's dependence of memory on enduring embodiment cannot explain retained episodic memory by invoking the mind's eternal aspect.

Two legitimate editorial options are available. Narrow the commitment to restored observable injuries/fatigue while leaving the bodily correlate of memory unestablished. Or retain exact bodily restoration as an explicit fictional departure and let Miriam face evidence that strains her view. Merely calling the anomalous memory eternal would be an author error if presented as Spinoza's explanation. Miriam may herself propose that mistaken explanation, provided the scene recognizes and develops the challenge. Neither option requires inventing a neurological mechanism.

If she remembers tiredness, learns fresh facts and then acts on them, those are genuine developments. The plot need not deny their force. It must avoid describing a disembodied memory-module pushing a bodily puppet as Spinozist mind/body identity. The engine's separate memory and physical-state fields are an implementation convenience, not a demonstration of metaphysical separation.

## 4. Recurrence is duration; eternal understanding is a different claim

**Primary:** I D8 denies explaining eternity through time, even continuance without beginning or end. II D5 defines duration as indefinite continuance. V21–23 excludes treating the eternal aspect as recollection before the body. V29s distinguishes a thing's existence at a time/place from its necessary dependence on God. V33s distinguishes eternal perfection from the imagined temporal acquisition used to explain it. V34s expressly diagnoses confusing eternity with survival of imagination and memory. Inspected range: 8060–8290, with V35–36s continued through 8328.

Henrietta can have several remembered weekends, or many, without thereby having an experience of eternity in this sense. The intellectual route through attributes and particular essences differs from retaining more episodes. This does not reduce eternity to an uplifting attitude: V30–36 makes a positive claim to knowledge of God and intellectual love as divine self-love expressed through a finite mind under eternity. Its coherence, individuation and relation to development in a finite life remain difficult. No specialist consensus on those puzzles is claimed here. The preceding selected Nadler check is retained as prior scholarship, not reported as a new reading in this wave.

**Specific use in the current story:** Henrietta may wish to keep an unrepeatable intimacy available indefinitely; refusing another return can still be a loss to her. Miriam can deny that retention is what she meant by eternity without dismissing the intimacy. A stronger disagreement asks what, if anything, is preserved in her intellectual account when memory of this conversation is absent. That is more than correcting vocabulary. The answer may fail to give Henrietta what she wants, even if she understands it correctly.

## 5. Prediction, adequate understanding, love and a non-reward ending

II18s's associative memory can explain the difference between remembering which words were followed by a response and understanding why the response followed. The proposal's predicted incoming message can be impressive public evidence while remaining evidence of prediction. It does not become third-kind knowledge. II48 denies an absolute faculty of free will; V3–4 positively connects understanding with becoming less passive and explicitly allows similar acts to arise from adequate or inadequate ideas. No visible kind act or successful conversation is an adequacy detector.

**Actual scene diagnosis, C1-S08:** lines 293–337 distinguish common rules, particular understanding, confidence, intellectual love and limited increased activity. The player's objection at 323–329 correctly challenges the inference from belonging to one nature to adequate cognition; Miriam concedes that a local instance does not prove the infinite account. The branch at 319 includes joy in activity and participation in divine self-understanding, while the branch at 315 refuses to certify Ruth's singular essence from her swimming result. These are supported features, not only author intentions. The scene still postpones its fuller substance argument; later use should develop it instead of claiming this lesson has discharged it.

Under the new proposal, this gives Henrietta a consequential comparison: she can improve the timing of her news until Ada gives the reply she wants, yet still possess only a remembered association and an incomplete account of Ada. Experiment: retain one technically successful replay, then let Ada make an unprompted request that the script does not answer. This is a proposed test of the scene's claim, not an instruction to punish control or to ensure the attempt fails. Henrietta can understand a practical cause better and still use that knowledge manipulatively. A philosophical verdict must not follow from whether Ada forgives her.

V32–36 supports the positive intellectual-love claim. V41 says ethical life retains force without knowledge of the mind's eternity. V42 makes blessedness virtue/activity itself rather than an external reward. The proposal's forward route should therefore remain available without a confession, reconciliation or correct answer. Henrietta's release of a real work arrangement may change lives without operating the service door. I Appendix (1170–1320) challenges projection of human purposes onto God's governance: the reset is not evidence that Nature wants her to stay, move, improve, or love someone. A character may believe it is; the author should not silently certify that belief through the exit rule.
