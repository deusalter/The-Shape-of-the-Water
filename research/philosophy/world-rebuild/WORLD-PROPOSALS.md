# Two incompatible world proposals

Proposal status only, 2026-10-04. These are design experiments for the sole lead writer, not scene prose or selected canon. Character roles below are placeholders, not names. Blaise Bloom remains fixed. Neither proposal copies the cast, plot or cosmology of Hello Charlotte; the separate reference researcher owns claims about that game. No personal playthrough is claimed here.

## A. The Molting Country

### The commitment

**Authored ontology:** there is one immanent existence. Its local finite arrangements include persons, landscapes and the processes that repeatedly reconstitute them. The traversable country is one enormously complex finite arrangement, not God depicted as a gigantic person. There is no overseeing consciousness that grades lives. Matter and thought do not transmit messages across an ontological gap. The world permits genuine material transformation; its persons are not dreaming ordinary Earth.

The country is an exposed interior whose surfaces turn through one another. Chalk cliffs open like the inside of a closed hand; orchards root in moving membranes; immense dry chambers keep the casts of former streets. The inhabited shore periodically folds through a seam of its own past. In the recurring district, an earlier day can be entered again. A slower surrounding process carries scars and travelers from one crossing to another. Recurrence is local, finite in its exploitable routes and physically costly; it is not a universal reset that erases whatever the author finds inconvenient.

**Law A1, preservation:** a finite individual's living organization can continue through replacement and through the return seam only while its reciprocal activities remain connected. Its original matter is neither necessary nor sufficient. Reassembling exact old parts after those activities have broken produces another individual. In the fiction this is a real distinction with observable consequences, although historical Spinoza supplies no such executable identity test.

**Law A2, cost:** crossing a returning district requires another continuing arrangement to carry what the district resets. Blaise's remembered turns have been sustained by a living region outside the return, using finite capacities that otherwise support its inhabitants. He is neither an immortal exception nor the world's observer. He can transfer support or end a crossing, but cannot create an unconditioned vantage from which everyone can be restored.

The author must specify the seam's exact reach and which processes it resets before drafting evidence. The rules cannot first appear when they are needed to forbid the attractive rescue.

### The case and the desire

Blaise seeks someone whose familiar body returns at every opening of the day, while the person herself becomes less reachable. He wants the woman who knows a particular shared afternoon, not an abstract demonstration that matter can be recycled. At the first major discovery, her familiar body is alive and responsive while her continuing life is elsewhere, distributed through an orchard and the bodies helping sustain it. This is not a mystical hive: the different people remain distinct, can disagree and can withdraw cooperation. The distributed woman is finite, vulnerable and developing desires Blaise has not witnessed.

The concrete crime is the intentional severing of her returning body's last reciprocal connection during a transfer. A keeper of the shell gardens did it to stop her continuing changes from reaching a dependent loved one. He substituted an exact earlier bodily arrangement and represented the apparent recovery as her return. His practical motive has intelligible urgency; the substitution nevertheless destroyed a particular continuity. The missing woman is not secretly alive as the shell. A surviving branch of her organization elsewhere can still be helped, but it cannot cancel that loss or give Blaise ownership of the outcome.

**Decisive evidence:** a distinctive cut exposes the direction in which the connection was severed; a harmless test on an independent small organism distinguishes continuously relayed activity from reconstructed appearance; traces carried through two separately visited support sites establish when the cut occurred relative to the return. The culprit's access and preparation have evidence independent of his metaphysical explanation. A confession can deepen the act, not substitute for the proof. These are proposed evidence functions, not a finished proof graph.

### Discoveries that change action

1. **Recover.** The shore and cast gardens teach travel, appetite, ordinary work and the return's seemingly restorative convenience. Blaise collects missing pieces and tries to make the old scene recur. A successful small restoration makes that hope reasonable.
2. **Maintain.** In a moving orchard and a crowded dwelling attached to it, he finds that continued exchange preserves a life through profound change. Taking a treasured original part away makes the person less stable. He must sustain a relation rather than acquire an object.
3. **Distinguish.** The gardens show many perfect returns that are newly living individuals. They have their own futures and cannot be treated as disposable failed copies. The missing woman's shell speaks plausibly but is no liar and no impostor. Its vulnerability becomes another claim on Blaise.
4. **Implicate.** The old chambers show how Blaise's own recurrence has been carried. His reliable memory did not establish that he was independent. The repeated day has depleted the region that sustains another person's future.
5. **Transform.** The last route offers a finite redistribution of support. Blaise can preserve the distributed woman's future by surrendering his exclusive return and some bodily capacities attached to it. He remains a person; the ending should not sell fusion or self-erasure as the definition of enlightenment. She survives differently and may refuse their former relationship. Another defensible route maintains his continuity and the shell's life while accepting the distributed woman's death. Neither should rewrite who cut the connection.

### What length develops

The six substantial places are the returning shore, cast gardens, traveling orchard, dwelling borne on the orchard, dry chambers of former streets, and the slow outer support region. Each needs residents who value something other than Blaise's problem. Meals depend on the country's cycles, but their taste can be discussed without metaphor. Body-maintenance work, courtship, private amusements, resentment of visitors and disputes over mobility follow from this society's conditions. The middle changes the rescue target and introduces competing lives; it does not repeat a culprit interrogation in increasingly strange rooms.

### Ending stance and limits

The ontology, cut, substitution, cost of recurrence and survival consequences are authored truths. There is a real answer to the case. An intimate final act realizes a known relation in a changed situation; it need not finish with an argument. A character can positively claim to understand a singular life's dependence on Nature and to love that intelligibility. A Kantian objection to the ultimate warrant survives without erasing what was discovered or turning the answer into “nothing can be known.”

Still undetermined: whether Blaise's claimed grasp amounts to Spinoza's third kind of knowledge; the full extent of Nature beyond the country; the precise philosophical status of his eternal aspect. Do not leave the distributed woman's actual fate undetermined. The strongest risk is turning continuity into a disguised soul commodity. The safeguard is that no portable unit, name, certificate or identity-bearing token carries a person. Living relations must be sustained in their particular material circumstances.

## B. The Unjoined Sea

### The commitment

**Authored ontology:** reality contains mutually joinable regions of objective experience, but no inhabited or inhabitable position is a view of the completed world. There is no pre-existing universal clock assigning every regional event a simultaneous partner elsewhere. This incompletability is a real feature of the fiction. It is not a mood or a limit waiting for a better camera. Unlike A, this proposal does not assert a completed Spinozist substance-ontology as the author's hidden solution.

The world is a sea composed of incompatible basins. One contains a white shallow ocean and houses suspended from their own shadows. Another is a steep red agricultural valley with water that arrives already old. Another is a desert in which yesterday's night stands in solid translucent ridges. These are usable regions with histories, not symbols projected by Blaise. Between them are engineered living bridges that carry reciprocal effects. The visible “sky” of a basin is its own returning far shore. There is no cinematic pullback showing all basins floating in a larger space.

**Law B1, connection:** two regions have a determinate shared temporal relation only while a reciprocal bridge establishes it. Local sequence and material change remain objective when a region is isolated. Isolation does not make its inhabitants imaginary. One-way traces can survive a separation, but they do not fix a common present. Rejoining fixes a new relation through a material operation at both ends; it cannot delete effects already encountered.

**Law B2, recurrence and price:** each isolated basin has a returning interval. Ordinary regional states recur, but a traveler or an effect carried into a connected basin can survive its return. The useful repetition therefore requires a route outside that local cycle. Synchronizing one region with another changes which route can carry continuity. A universal synchronization would remove the necessary external routes and force distinct lives into incompatible recurrent conditions. This is a concrete incompatibility to demonstrate with two small regions well before the finale, not an omniscient warning about forbidden knowledge.

These are radical fictional laws inspired by the Third Analogy and antinomy problem. Kant does not say communities physically manufacture time, and Spinoza would contest making the ultimate structure dependent on these finite conditions. The proposal should preserve that contest.

### The case and the desire

An entire inhabited harbor has aged through several generations during one supper in the region to which it was joined. Blaise finds a living friend there who has become old while he continues to return to their last afternoon. He initially wants to find the correct route back to her youth. She wants a way to meet her descendants on a coast she cannot presently reach. These aims can cooperate for much of the voyage before they prove incompatible.

The harbor's catastrophe was caused by a bridge builder who cut its return path while leaving a one-way path open from a much faster local cycle. The builder wanted to keep an ill child inside a repeated hour and exported the accumulated change into the harbor. The exact physical transaction, the builder's knowledge of its consequences, and the earlier warning he concealed are solvable. Whether his act happened “before supper” in the disconnected world is an ill-formed global question; whether he performed the cut before the influx in the harbor's own causal history is answerable.

**Decisive evidence:** growth bands retain the number and ordering of imported cycles; a protected object sent one way carries the builder's preparation into the harbor; the severed return structure explains why the influx lacked compensating outflow. Independent voyages to two intermediate regions reconstruct a partial causal order without inventing a master chronology. A small-scale reenactment predicts which material changes will recur and which will persist in the neighboring region. The original harbor deaths remain facts on every route.

### Discoveries that change action

1. **Compare.** Blaise collects times, dates and alibis and discovers genuine contradictions in the assumed common calendar. Regional witnesses can all be accurate. This cannot be the ending's entire lesson.
2. **Exchange.** A voyage establishes co-presence through a reciprocal intervention. The player acquires a new practical operation, carrying an effect and waiting for its return, rather than merely selecting a new theory.
3. **Refuse the shortcut.** A region close to Blaise's desired afternoon also contains a newly living family whose future his shortcut would fold into recurrence. They can be encountered before he learns their function in his route. He must negotiate travel and material support with them.
4. **Reconstruct.** The apparently impossible alibi is resolved through the surviving partial order. The builder is confronted with what he did, not with a metaphysical multiple-choice test. His saved child is another person, with a response to the life being repeatedly preserved for her.
5. **Relinquish universal simultaneity.** The promised master reunion would force all coasts into the same returning interval and destroy continuities that now sustain different people. Blaise can join his friend's present coast to her descendants' coast, but that leaves their shared afternoon inaccessible. He might choose a smaller stable community instead, with clear specified exclusions. The ending makes a real world configuration possible and another impossible.

### What length develops

This is a voyage and gradually enlarging collaboration, structurally distinct from A's bodily missing-person investigation. Six regions should bring changing forms of work, leisure, travel and kinship. Generations have adapted to the absence of universal time; only some people regard it as a defect. Long separations change relationships in ways the player must actually encounter. Recurrences allow revisits, but each trip changes who can travel with Blaise and what connection remains available elsewhere. Keep an understandable route diagram in author notes; the player can use witnessed landmarks and tested relations.

### Ending stance and limits

The world really has no universal experienced present, the harbor's destruction really occurred, the builder's act is established, and the final joining has determinate surviving inhabitants. No revelation withdraws those truths. A Spinozist character has a serious objection: a complete intelligible causal order need not be a panoramic experience or master clock. Destroying a synchronization device would not disprove substance monism. This objection should stop Blaise from congratulating himself that his practical success has refuted it.

Still undetermined: whether regional intelligibility is grounded in one substance, and whether any human account reaches that ultimate dependence. The source and limits of the world are not a further level promised after the ending. The hard commitment concerns the lack of a global temporal perspective, which the finale actively realizes. The strongest risk is arbitrary portal fantasy. Prevent it with a fixed regional graph, conservation/accounting of exported material change, explicit bridge operations, and every exception authored before proof construction. Use at most two temporal operations, not a growing catalogue of powers.

## Choice for the lead

A puts finite individuality, immanence and embodied transformation at the center; it supports a stranger positive ontology and an intimate conflict in which the desired rescue target changes. B makes the conditions of coexistence and the demand for totality reshape an expanding expedition; it supports a larger geography and intergenerational consequences. Both require new causality review before selection. Neither is a completed treatment, and neither's visual inventory should be installed as canon merely because it is listed here.

## Proposal-stage criticism received, not a binding repair

The world dramaturg identified an unresolved ambiguity in B above: “each isolated basin” recurring does not yet explain why the harbor's aging persists after the return bridge is cut, or how the builder's source continues to recur while retaining one-way outflow. The bridge's return path and the region's recurrence were insufficiently distinguished. The causal answer is therefore an intended construction, not yet a proven coherent mechanism. “Accumulated change” must also become a specified sequence of material events; time must not quietly become a transferable currency. The original proposal is preserved so the defect is visible.

The lead writer is developing a separate B treatment that revises the recurrence rule. That treatment, once written and inspected, governs its own critique. This research note does not impose a repair. An optional alternative would confine recurrence to a bounded current within a basin, let the rest of the basin retain physical change, and specify exactly which material effects can cross each bridge. That alternative still requires a finite diagram and a worked example before it could support a case.

Root also correctly requires A's finite support cost and B's harmful universal synchronization to follow from earlier observable operations. A should demonstrate the same coupling during an ordinary nonfatal task before it can impose a personal sacrifice. B should demonstrate an actual incompatible joining of two limited regions before a larger version threatens lives. Neither “Nature demands a price” nor a last-minute prohibition is an adequate law. The source research establishes no such conservation law; any selected constraint belongs explicitly to the fiction.
