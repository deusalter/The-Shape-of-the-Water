# Metaphysics as a scene's operating conditions

Research checkpoint, 2026-10-05 UTC; folder preserves the owner's 2026-10-04 direction label. Research/proposal only. No canon, code, names or released prose changed.

The strongest transferable principle is that a metaphysical proposition should alter what can happen, how a happening can be recognized, or who is doing the recognizing. A later disclosure can change an earlier scene's ontological category without replacing its observed details. Beauty can be one of the conditions under which people navigate, survive, misidentify, make or destroy something. It need not be an illustration of a conversation about love.

## Borges: an idea becomes the form of an event

**Inspected evidence.** Beatriz Sarlo's *Borges: A Writer on the Edge*, chapters 4 and 5, is hosted by the Borges Center. Chapter 4 develops her term “philosophical narrative situation”: ideas constitute the material and organization of the plot instead of appearing as a supplemental discussion. Her treatment of recursive representation links the represented observer, the image containing its image and the question of the reader's own status. These are Sarlo's critical interpretations, with short Borges quotations inside her argument, not a fresh inspection of complete Borges originals. [B1]

Chapter 5 reconstructs the escalating status of Tlön: elusive textual account, systematic invented world, then materially present objects. Sarlo stresses the beauty and apparent comprehensibility of constructed order. She also distinguishes the library's hypothesized infinity from what a traveler could empirically establish. Her ideological and historical reading is explicitly her reading, not something she claims Borges would endorse. Some compressed plot/doctrinal summaries are interpretive; they should not substitute for the original stories. [B2]

**Transfer.** Make an account first usable, then locally operative, then insufficient. An apparently decorative relation between harbor, horizon and reflected city could become a condition of travel. Characters can discover one repeatable consequence before knowing whether they have found an underlying unity, an imposed appearance or a local defect. Each stage keeps the earlier details available for reconsideration. A view which visibly includes its observer should compromise the narrator's assumed place outside the description, not grant a complete God's-eye view.

**Reject.** An encyclopedia that makes reality, a library-universe, an Aleph substitute, copied dream recursion, or a cosmic classification office. Also reject making every strange thing merely an unreliable image. Sarlo's analysis is useful precisely because the problem takes narrative and spatial form.

Sources: [B1](https://www.borges.pitt.edu/bsol/bsi4.php), [B2](https://www.borges.pitt.edu/bsol/bsi5.php).

## R. Scott Bakker: beauty has force; explanations have conditions

**Inspected evidence.** In his main About essay, Bakker names *The Second Apocalypse* as his principal means of exploring troubling possibilities about meaning. This establishes his stated artistic project, not the correctness of his philosophy or any particular fictional doctrine. He explicitly distrusts explanations which conveniently flatter the people offering them. [K1]

The author-hosted *The False Sun* is primary fiction in that setting. Selected arrival and closing-collapse passages were inspected, not the entire story. In the arrival, impossible daylight produces shadows, warmth and the threat of sunburn; it also affects tactical advantage. At the end, destruction of the supporting terrain changes the situation sustaining sorcery: the suspended figures fall, and a creature's wings become decisive. The narration ties failure to the sorcery's meaning under drastically changed circumstances. This is a concrete fictional dependency, not evidence that belief alone makes the world's facts. The story also renders catastrophic destruction as beautiful. Beauty does not certify goodness or safety. [K2]

**Transfer.** Give a beautiful impossibility physical affordances. Show someone depending competently on them. Then reveal a condition of that success by changing the relation on which it depended. The revelation should make an earlier successful act newly intelligible, while producing an unintended consequence that continues beyond the protagonist's moral development.

For the proposed coast, the voyagers might already possess a useful procedure whose premise they mistake. A change of relation invalidates the procedure without erasing its former success. The new discovery permits a different action, with public witnesses and material aftermath. What that discovered relation ultimately *is* remains disputed.

**Reject.** Copying the Day Lantern, Ark, named sorceries, damnation apparatus or the story's signature imagery. Also reject using this example to announce that historically Spinozist thought changes extension. The transferable device is a causal dependency becoming perceptible through failure, not Bakker's cosmology.

Sources: [K1](https://rsbakker.wordpress.com/about/), [K2](https://rsbakker.wordpress.com/stories/the-false-sun/).

## Gene Wolfe: disclosure changes the speaker, not the evidence

**Inspected evidence.** In Larry McCaffery's interview, Wolfe says refracted narration should suit its subject rather than deliberately mystify. He explains that Severian remembers how he formerly remembered, as distinct from simply retaining an event. Discussing *The Fifth Head of Cerberus*, he describes introducing the researcher before the material researched, presenting an earlier period as a surviving legend, and supplying behavioral clues to the eventual narrator's identity. His stated example is differing competence with firearms. That gives the reader grounds to reattribute an account. This is creator testimony in a secondary mirror, not an independent novel reading. The mirror has inconsistent year metadata: its issue citation is *Science Fiction Studies* 46, volume 15 part 3, November 1988, while an added heading says 1989. The original print issue was not inspected. [W1]

Wolfe's original-publisher Clarkesworld interview offers a useful check on abstract dialogue. His revision example expands a synopsis into concrete professional stakes and distinctive speech. He says dialogue acts on the situation and should fit this speaker addressing this person at this time. This does not prohibit long arguments; it gives them participants and pressures. [W2]

**Transfer.** Plant a small, ordinary competence or error before a metaphysical disclosure: how a person knots a rope, hears a note or misnames an orientation. Later evidence can relocate the source of a voice, memory or action without making the entire encounter fraudulent. Let Blaise's account retain his earlier construal and his present difficulty understanding why it seemed obvious. Narration then participates in the mystery rather than hovering above it as a neutral explanation.

Make the new identification inferable. “Nobody can know anything” would throw away Wolfe's explicit interest in recoverable clues. Preserve an exact distinction between what the player encountered and what Blaise later thinks he encountered.

**Reject.** Severian's memory premise, a disguised duplicate of his narrator, the same shape-changing solution, or decorative obscure vocabulary. Reject postponing all explanation just to manufacture difficulty.

Sources: [W1](https://gwern.net/doc/fiction/gene-wolfe/1988-11-mccaffery-onencompassingtheentireuniverseaninterviewwithgenewolfe.html), [W2](https://clarkesworldmagazine.com/wolfe_interview/).

## Umineko: rival accounts share a concrete burden

**Inspected evidence, narrower than the other references.** The official Question Arcs description opposes a human and a witch account around an apparently bounded population, repeated murders and materially located riddles. The Answer Arcs description promises teaching while preserving the reader's work on the epitaph. These publisher descriptions establish advertised structure and investigative invitation. They do not establish how the full game's narrative levels, truth rules or revelations work. [U1, U2]

Prior familiarity suggests the relevance of its colored truth conventions and layered tellings, but those mechanisms were **not independently verified in this pass** and are not used as sourced claims or an implementation specification. A creator interview would improve this part of the dossier. The attempted Witch Hunt and fan-hosted interview routes were inaccessible. No transcript or ending was inspected.

**Transfer supported by the narrower evidence.** Give rival ontological explanations the same stubborn thing to explain. A body arrived, a rope broke, a shoreline changed position, or a voyager returned with a measurable injury. Let a scene make both accounts attractive for different reasons, and let investigation rule out some claims without automatically settling the entire world's nature. The underlying event should have a causal answer even if the cosmic explanation remains open.

**Reject.** Copying colored truth speech, witch-versus-detective staging, a sealed-island family mystery or a revelation that licenses erasing earlier observations. Do not turn uncertainty into a menu in which player belief retroactively chooses reality.

Sources: [U1](https://store.steampowered.com/app/406550/Umineko_When_They_Cry__Question_Arcs/), [U2](https://store.steampowered.com/app/639490/Umineko_When_They_Cry__Answer_Arcs/).

## Consequences for the present revision

1. **Make a finite commitment.** Establish one extraordinary relation through an action that succeeds, fails or leaves a remainder. Its existence need not wait for agreement about total ontology.
2. **Give disagreement unequal local strengths.** One account predicts an action; another explains a feature it misses. Neither speaker needs to become a clean proxy for a historical philosopher. They may revise a thought while remaining wrong about its scope.
3. **Make the beautiful event costly to describe.** Its scale, order or viewpoint should force Blaise to retract or reform a sentence. Sustained monologue can emerge from that labor without resolving into an aphorism or a relationship lesson.
4. **Let strangers already have lives within it.** Voyagers, artisans or animals use the extraordinary place before Blaise arrives. Their practices and consequences survive his exit. This avoids an entire universe arranged for one man's improvement.
5. **Preserve earlier observations.** Revelation should alter an event's category, causal relation or speaker attribution while leaving the original encounter available. Specify which part has been established and which remains a character's inference.

These are original construction proposals derived from the inspected material. They are not fictional laws already installed in Mercy. They do not establish access to Kantian noumena, eternity as a frozen sum of every moment, or subjective conviction as the creator of reality. Philosophical complication should produce more exact scene behavior, not merely more unanswered questions.
