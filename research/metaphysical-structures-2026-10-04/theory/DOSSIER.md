# What makes a fictional metaphysical structure

Research by the coordinator, 2026-10-05 UTC; folder and owner record use the client date 2026-10-04. This is comparative research, not a new fictional law or scene proposal. Contemporary theory was read in selected sections, not as complete books. The two additional primary examples are public-domain fiction. Named-reference dossiers are separate.

## A useful distinction, without making it a rigid genre law

Marie-Laure Ryan's *Possible Worlds* entry reconstructs Brian McHale's distinction between an epistemological dominant, concerned with what can be known, and an ontological dominant, concerned with what exists. This is a critical account of tendencies in literature, not a proof that every work belongs to one box. An unreliable witness can make knowledge difficult without changing what kinds of things exist. Conversely, a fictional world can literally contain conflicting realities, impossible objects or crossings between levels of representation. Those require more than doubt about testimony. [T1]

Ryan describes several modal systems, drawing on Lubomír Doležel: possibility and necessity; permission and obligation; value; and knowledge or belief. They do different work in plots. A world in which a dead person is remembered, one in which the dead person can act, and one in which a person's being remembered causes that person to exist are three different constructions. The third also relates memory, agency and persistence. This example is our distinction, not an example Ryan gives.

The current rejected-as-insufficient proposal mostly modifies physical possibility and adds shared first-person experience. It does not yet establish how those modifications interact with time, finite identity, divine status and the protagonist's motives throughout the main story. Adding more anomalies would not by itself establish those interactions.

## Belief worlds are not automatically real alternate universes

Ryan distinguishes the narrative's asserted actual domain from the worlds characters believe, desire, anticipate and invent. A detective story can evoke several possible histories while eventually establishing one. Their evocative force does not make them all exist in its universe. She also describes works that challenge the hierarchy between one actual world and merely possible worlds. That is an additional ontological construction, not something implied by every counterfactual. [T1]

This distinction matters twice for the project. The owner's single substantial story does not require a combinatorial route structure: a shared sequence can sustain incompatible models of that sequence. At the same time, metaphorical alternatives should not be advertised as genuinely coexisting realities unless the fiction makes that commitment or leaves their status deliberately open with consequential evidence.

Ryan also compares Doležel's radical incompleteness of fictional worlds with approaches that assume fuller existents behind incomplete representation. She presents the location of the incompleteness as a difficulty, not a settled conclusion. We should therefore distinguish an unanswered question from a fact the author intends to withhold, and from an aspect of the fiction that is genuinely unspecified. Inventing a hidden answer to every ambiguous scene would narrow some works improperly; leaving the central causal mystery unwritten would create a different defect.

## A representation can be crossed, not merely observed

John Pier's *Metalepsis* entry studies transgressions between the world of the telling and the world told, including embedded worlds within a fiction. The term is useful only after identifying which boundary is crossed. An upside-down coast can be extraordinary geography while remaining on the same narrative level as a market. A painting of that coast is another sort of representation. A character whose action in the painting changes the painter's history crosses a different boundary. The last illustration is ours; it is not a proposal for this game's canon. [T2]

Pier surveys the distinction between a brief rhetorical gesture across levels and a crossing which permits lasting interpenetration. He also records disagreements over that distinction and over what counts as metalepsis in different media. Thus narrator self-reference, physical passage between places, character knowledge of being fictional, and causal action between embedded worlds cannot be treated as interchangeable. The fact that an actual novelist produced all these levels does not entail that a represented author is omnipotent within them.

Pier's transmedial discussion is particularly useful for a visual game. Images, panels, performance spaces and animation can enact a relation rather than explain it in speech. The significant event can be a change in who or what can affect a representation, or in which apparent interior contains the other. A camera view which includes the camera's observer does not alone prove that the observer has escaped every condition of observation. Visual shock and metaphysical disclosure need not have equal scope.

## Impossible fiction need not make every claim true

Fred Kroon and Alberto Voltolini's SEP entry *Fiction* distinguishes philosophical possible worlds from fictional worlds, which may be incomplete and inconsistent. Its section on impossible fictions describes a problem in David Lewis's account: if no possible world realizes a story, a vacuous truth clause would make anything true in it. That is an objection to a theory of fiction, not a rule that writers must avoid all contradictions. The entry then discusses attempts to handle different kinds of contradiction. Only the opening of that debate was inspected here. [T3]

The practical lesson is not to force our world into ordinary physics or classical consistency at every scale. It is that an intentional impossibility can have a scope. If two contrary accounts of a person are both operative, the fiction can still discriminate between actions that person can perform and actions they cannot. The impossibility should complicate the event's meaning without silently licensing arbitrary solutions. This is an editorial inference from the theoretical problem, not the SEP authors' game-design prescription.

## Primary case: Flatland makes the teacher's truth incomplete

Selected primary reading: Edwin Abbott Abbott, *Flatland*, sections 14, 17–19 and 22 in Gutenberg ebook 97. No complete-novel reading is claimed. [T4]

The Square first encounters a one-dimensional ruler whose methods work within his world. The King identifies people by hearing; what the Square calls a new direction has no place in the King's spatial language. The Square cannot simply draw the extra direction inside the line. Leaving that line looks like disappearance, which the King reclassifies rather than accepting a new dimension.

The Sphere then takes a similar position towards the Square. He removes an object from a locked cupboard without opening its door and painfully touches the Square's interior. These are concrete failures of the Square's model of enclosure. But the Sphere's true explanation is entangled with contempt and force; possessing the better spatial model does not make him a good teacher or harmless being.

Once raised into Spaceland, the Square sees interiors previously inferred. He calls this godlike. The Sphere replies that even criminals in his world have that capacity; superior access does not establish superior goodness. The Square then extends the very analogy he has learned, asking for a fourth dimension and access to the Sphere's interior. The Sphere refuses and returns him to Flatland. His authority has revealed something real without establishing the final dimensional limit.

Back in Flatland, the Square cannot adequately represent his discovery using its available diagrams. Knowledge changes his appetites and risks his freedom, while the memory of what he saw becomes harder to recover. The ending's uncertainty does not erase the earlier causal demonstration. Nor does the Sphere's refusal establish that the fourth dimension is absent.

The architecture therefore joins perceptual affordance, geometry, authority, analogy, social power, desire and memory. The same asymmetry recurs with changed roles. A revelation creates a new question which the revealer cannot settle. This is much richer than a single impossible vista followed by an argument about whether it was real.

For Kant, an extra dimension accessed through a new experience would still be an object of possible experience within this fiction, not automatically a noumenal realm. For Spinoza, broader perception would not itself establish adequate intuitive knowledge or blessedness. These are our application cautions informed by the project's retained philosophical research, not claims Abbott stages those exact doctrines.

## Primary case: Through the Looking-Glass combines local reversal with an unsettled container

Selected primary reading: Lewis Carroll, *Through the Looking-Glass*, the sleeping Red King passage in chapter IV; the White Queen's backwards-memory and pin sequence in chapter V; and chapters X–XII. No complete-work reading is claimed. [T5]

The White Queen describes memory of future events, punishment before trial and crime, and reports bleeding before she pricks her finger. Some claims are statements by the Queen; the pin episode directly stages screaming before the later prick. Alice's attempted prevention arrives too late. The distinction between reported rule and enacted sequence remains available.

The discussion does not become an orderly lecture. Employment, jam, a missing comb, impending injury, loneliness and belief interrupt one another. Argument emerges from those interruptions. Reversed order also pressures responsibility: Alice asks what happens if the crime never occurs. The Queen's reply does not resolve that problem by a sound proof, and her bodily commotion interrupts Alice's objection.

The Red King's dream introduces a different issue: the dependence of the apparent world on a dreamer who is himself within it. The twins assert that Alice disappears if he wakes. Alice appeals to her tears as evidence that she is real, but that appeal does not settle which level contains which. At the end, the Red Queen becomes a kitten as Alice wakes. This supplies an apparent exterior, yet the final question about Alice and the Red King refuses to certify its absolute priority. The local pin sequence is much more determinate than the total dream hierarchy.

This combination is transferable at the structural level: enacted temporal reversals can coexist with an unsettled explanation of what contains the world, while different speakers misunderstand different parts. We should not import Carroll's chess structure, mirror portal, twins, dreamer puzzle or distinctive comic devices. Nor should the dream debate make us describe every local event as unreal.

## Consequence for the research synthesis

Look for interacting relationships among world, representation, causal order, personal persistence and knowledge. Distinguish what a scene establishes locally from what it leaves metaphysically contested. Let a reliable explanation open a harder problem, rather than making every revelation another excuse for uncertainty. The named-reference studies should provide more extensive examples of those relationships before a new fictional architecture is selected.
