# Four voice auditions

Original demonstration, not canon. These names, actions, and circumstances are provisional. No character is an avatar for a philosopher. The scene question is held constant to expose differences in attention and conversational conduct, not to supply the final mystery.

## Shared question

The investigator asks, "Why didn't you open the door?"

### Ada Sorel

"I opened it. I shut it again when I saw who was there. Get that part right."

"Then you knew him."

"That's another question."

She draws the chair closer to the table. One leg has caught the edge of the rug; she leaves it there.

"Would you like me to ask it?"

"I'd like you to ask what you came to ask. You've been trying to make me do it for you."

Private audition note: Ada resists another person's formulation of her action. Her precision does social work. It must sometimes help another person, sometimes protect her, and sometimes fail. Do not make every line a correction.

### Blaise March

"It sticks when the air's wet. You lift it here. No, let go, I'll show you. Christ, you're going to take the handle off."

"You heard him."

"Everybody heard him. Upstairs heard him."

He puts his hand over yours, then removes it.

"I wasn't saying you couldn't have opened it."

"You were pulling it the wrong way."

Private audition note: Blaise retreats into an immediately demonstrable practical task. His clauses follow what is happening to the object. The repeated instruction is an evasion here, not a catchphrase to repeat throughout the game. A scene where his practical help is generous should complicate this one.

### Emmy Vane

"Because I was in the bath, darling. Is that allowed? Shall I fetch the towel? You look as though you'd like the towel."

"I only need the time."

"I don't take the clock into the bath."

"Your neighbor says she spoke to you."

"Through the door. People can do that. We're doing splendidly with a whole table between us."

Her niece enters carrying a cup without a saucer. Emmy moves a newspaper out of her way.

"Go upstairs, love. I'll bring it."

Private audition note: Emmy recruits an audience and humiliates the premise of the question. Her speech changes when someone she does not want in the exchange enters. The performance must not turn every serious response into a joke, and 'darling' cannot carry the entire voice.

### Michael Earnshaw

"Which time?"

"There was more than one?"

He looks at the other chair.

"Michael," says the woman sitting in it.

"I was asking which time."

The investigator waits.

"No. You ask her. She remembers it."

Private audition note: Michael negotiates permission in the presence of a particular person. The reader hears pressure through repetition, pronouns, and the refusal to finish a claim. Sparse speech is not evidence that he has no complex thought. He needs a different scene in which the listener gives him room to use it.

## A second-register check

Do not use these as four permanent verbal tricks. The lead must write ordinary exchanges as well. A possible seed is the group trying to set a table when the chairs do not match. No disappearance, secret, or philosophical speech is required. The goal is to discover who takes over, who helps without announcing it, who makes someone else laugh, and who notices that another person has stopped eating.

For a contrasting explicit intellectual exchange, make the question costly to these particular people. An investigator's accurate explanation of a past action might leave the person being explained objecting to how that explanation will be used. Let both participants develop their thought. Preserve the distinctions between explanation, excuse, prediction, and permission without converting the conversation into four labeled definitions.

## Why the audition is not a quality certificate

The excerpts are short and several share defensive circumstances. That makes the distinctness partly situational. They do not yet demonstrate sustained voices, a world, relationships across time, or a compelling game. Ada's controlled closing, Emmy's one-liners, and Michael's withheld answer could all become cheap habits through repetition.

Use these passages as concrete things to improve, replace, or discard. A final cast should be stronger and less mechanically legible than this isolated exercise.
