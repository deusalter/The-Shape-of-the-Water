# Opening audition

Original illustrative sample, not approved story canon or a finished opening. It tests whether a conflict about reenacting an absent person can become a playable encounter without a metaphysical explanation. The lead writer may reject the premise. This scene does not establish that the whole game concerns bereavement.

## Situation

A woman has hired the protagonist to sit through lunch in place of her absent son. The protagonist is an experienced performer, not a blank detective. This particular request need not be an ordinary social institution in the setting. Why the son is absent and why the performer accepted are questions for actual development, not secret answers hidden in this example.

## Sample

The money was under the bread plate. You had to lift it to count.

"After," she said.

You put the plate down. A crumb had stuck to the largest coin.

The coat was too short in the sleeves. She had brought it downstairs on a hanger and waited while you changed. When you came back she took hold of your wrist, pulled the cuff toward your hand, and let go.

"He wore it open."

You undid the buttons.

Now she was making tea. The pot had a metal handle wrapped in string. She tested it twice before lifting it, though you could see no steam.

"Tell me it's too weak."

You looked into the cup.

"The tea is too weak."

"No. You have to taste it."

You drank. It was cold, and stronger than you liked.

"The tea is too weak."

She took your cup away. In the kitchen a spoon struck the edge of the sink. You heard it turn, then settle.

When she came back, she had put sugar in it.

"He didn't take sugar," you said. She had told you this twice before you arrived.

"You do."

## Potential choices

These are alternative directions to draft and test, not final implemented branches.

Say, "Shall we start again?" The performer offers to resume the job, and she must decide whether that is still what she wants.

Ask, "What did he say after that?" The scene can continue into a specific remembered exchange. The writer must supply an actual incident, not a general account of a difficult relationship.

Push the bread plate toward her. This suggests ending the arrangement. The action should have a concrete response and should not automatically earn a kindness reward.

Drink the tea. Silence lets the present encounter continue without immediately turning it into an interview. The next event must still be authored; silence is not a dead button.

## Review questions

Does the cold tea feel like a real circumstance or an overly engineered symbol? Is the performer's question plausible given the job? Does the final line land too neatly? What could interrupt the encounter and make it less symmetrical? Can the woman become interesting beyond this one absence? Can the protagonist's professional habits generate conflict without an explanatory monologue?

This sample is deliberately open to criticism. A few effective paragraphs are not a solved protagonist, a full narrative, or evidence of masterpiece-level quality.
